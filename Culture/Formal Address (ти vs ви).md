# Formal Address (ти vs. ви)

See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the grammatical mechanism (ти = familiar singular, ви = formal singular / true plural) and [[../Words/ти|Words/ти]] for the pronoun's own etymology. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## A Doubly-Motivated Feature, Not a Simple Inheritance

The plural-for-polite-singular pattern (T–V distinction) is **not** a Common Slavic inheritance — there's no evidence Late Common Slavic itself distinguished formal/informal address this way. It's a real, well-documented pan-European areal phenomenon instead: the convention traces to Late Roman imperial court usage (addressing the Emperor, whose authority was formally construed as joint/plural, as *vos*), which spread through medieval Romance vernaculars as a courtly prestige marker and diffused from there into most of the rest of Europe — Slavic, Germanic, and beyond — via contact with Latin-literate, Romance-influenced elites over the medieval and early modern periods.

Sisician acquires the feature through **two independent, mutually reinforcing channels** — genuinely doubly-sourced, the same kind of Slavic+Dalmatian convergence that already made the pro-drop decision ([[../Grammar/Verbal Agreement|Grammar/Verbal Agreement]]) well-grounded rather than coincidental:

1. **The general European diffusion wave** — the same channel through which virtually every other Slavic language (Russian *ty/vy*, Serbo-Croatian *ti/vi*, etc.) picked it up, independent of Sisician's own specific history.
2. **Direct reinforcement from the Dalmatian founding elite** — [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]]'s founding household is Latin-literate, Romance-speaking, and court-connected from day one. Dalmatian/Vulgar Latin already carries (or is itself acquiring) this same courtly plural-address convention, so the elite's own domestic speech habits directly model and accelerate the vernacular's adoption of it, rather than Sisician having to wait for the feature to diffuse in from outside.

## Real, Not Distinctively Balkan — Said Plainly

Worth being precise rather than overclaiming a pedigree this doesn't have: T-V-via-plural-address is genuinely **pan-European**, not a Balkan Sprachbund-specific innovation the way case loss or the past-tense evidentiality system are. But Balkan languages fully participate in it — Bulgarian **Вие**, Greek **εσείς**, Turkish **siz**, Albanian **ju** all use exactly this plural-for-polite-singular pattern. So Sisician's version sits accurately within a broader European convention that Balkan languages are also part of, not one they distinctively originated.

## The Social Rule — Modeled on German Sie/du

**Default expectation on first contact with a stranger — answering the phone, answering the door, any formal introduction — is ви.** Moving to ти is not automatic with repeated contact or growing familiarity; it requires an **invitation**, mirroring German *Sie*/*du* practice and the tradition of *das Du anbieten* ("offering the du"). Following German norms directly: the **socially senior party initiates** — the elder, the higher-status person, or the host, depending on context — the same traditional hierarchy German etiquette itself follows (modern German usage has loosened this in some contexts, but the base pattern being modeled here is the traditional one).

**Using ти with a stranger, or before that invitation arrives, reads as presumptuous — even rude.** Not a minor slip: a real social misstep, the same weight it carries in traditional German social practice.

## The Invitation Ritual (Proposed, Pending Confirmation)

German's *Bruderschaft trinken* (a shared drink/toast that formalizes the switch to *du*, traditionally with linked arms and a cheek-kiss) is the model — similar in spirit, but built here from Sisician's own real cultural material rather than copied directly.

**Real anchoring material**, all already established or genuinely attested: South Slavic **pobratimstvo** (побратимство) — a real, historically significant folk institution of ritual sworn brotherhood across Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, and Montenegrin tradition — and the real Byzantine/Orthodox liturgical rite of *adelphopoiesis* ("brother-making"), a genuine historical Church blessing for exactly this kind of bond. Combined with [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s established Dalmatian wine culture and [[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]]'s core river/bridge identity motif ("we are the people who stand between worlds because the river passes through us"), this pulls on three of Sisician's four cultural strata at once — Slavic folk custom, Orthodox liturgical form, and Dalmatian material culture — rather than being invented from nothing.

**Proposed ritual (working name — a real Sisician term would need proper derivation once vocabulary work reaches this domain):** the senior party — matching the already-decided German-modeled initiation rule — proposes sharing a single cup of wine. The two drink from it in turn, often with arms crossed or the cups touched together, while a short spoken formula invoking the river/bridge motif is exchanged (something in the spirit of "as the [river] joins both its banks" — an illustrative gloss, not a finished Sisician-language formula). The switch to ти is spoken explicitly immediately after ("now you may call me ти") to close the ritual.

**Two-tier system, matching how the German model actually works in practice, not an idealized version of it:** ordinary, everyday ти-invitations are usually just the plain verbal cue described in § The Social Rule — a spoken offer, nothing more, the way most real German *du*-switches happen today. **The full wine-sharing ritual is reserved for especially significant bonds** — sworn friendship, business partnership, in-law/family bonds — the same way *Bruderschaft trinken* itself functions as an available formalizing custom in German culture rather than a mandatory step for every single switch.

**Differences from the German model, by design:** a spoken formula/invocation (reflecting Sisician's much heavier liturgical weight, where German's version is secular); river/bridge symbolism specific to Sisician's own founding identity, absent from German culture entirely; wine specifically rather than any drink, tying to both Dalmatian material culture and Orthodox Eucharistic resonance; and an explicitly asymmetric, senior-initiates structure throughout, rather than the more mutual framing German *Bruderschaft trinken* sometimes carries.
