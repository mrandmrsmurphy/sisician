# Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman

How Sisician speakers signal refinement, and mock its absence, through accent — the phonetic and performative flip side of [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|the prestige-register principle]]. See [[Sisician]] for the hub page and [[Sociolinguistic History]] for the orthographic history this connects to.

## A Sharper Asymmetry Than English/French

English can idolize French pronunciation, but it can never fully forget that real French people still exist right next door, speaking real French — that living comparison keeps English's Francophilia a little self-aware, a little performed. **Sisician doesn't have that check.** By [[History]] § 1300, Dalmatian is completely dead as a spoken community language — there is no living Dalmatian community anywhere to contradict Sisician's self-image. This makes Sisician **freer to imagine itself as still essentially Dalmatian** than English can ever fully imagine itself French, precisely because nothing real remains to correct the fantasy.

**But this same freedom needs a target, and the target isn't distant.** English mocking German is mocking a foreign nation — affectionate, low-stakes, a joke about outsiders. Sisician's actual target is its own unassimilated cousins: the real, living, ethnically-close Croatian/Serbian/Bosnian-speaking population all around it, who never went through the Dalmatian-elite fusion and simply stayed "just Slavic." This is **not** foreigner-mockery. It's the much older, nastier pattern of **intra-family class snobbery** — structurally closer to Anglo-Norman French nobility looking down on English-speaking peasantry (same broader population, stratified by cultural prestige, not by nationhood) than to any England-mocks-Germany joke. Sisicians consider themselves *better* than Slavs, not merely different from them — while being, demonstrably, Slavs themselves underneath the Dalmatian veneer. The snobbery is aimed inward at what Sisician itself would have been without the founding household's intervention.

## The Boogeyman Sound: ç → [χ]

**The real linguistic asymmetry this whole convention exploits:** Vulgar Latin and Dalmatian have no velar or uvular fricative at all (see [[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Sources/Vulgar Latin]], [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]) — real Romance languages generally lack this whole sound class. Sisician's own old */x/*, by contrast, was inherited straight from the Slavic norm — the one common, everyday phoneme the Dalmatian prestige layer never had anything to say about. Per [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] § x→ç, standard Modern Sisician's reflex is now palatal **/ç/** in every register — Sisician has actually pulled away from the Slavic norm here, not stayed "consistent with" it, since real Croatian/Serbian/Bosnian still have velar /x/ today.

Doing a mocking impression of "raw Slavic" speech means reaching *outside* the standard variety entirely — pushing the phoneme back from its own everyday **[ç]** into a rasping, exaggerated uvular **[χ]**, the same sound a genuine non-standard/rural dialect (one that never went through the fronting) still has, and piling it into words that don't even etymologically call for it. This is the exact mechanism of the real-world joke this design is modeled on (English caricaturing German by stuffing an exaggerated uvular fricative into every possible syllable) — a single consonant, deliberately overworked, standing in for an entire people's presumed unrefinement. A standard speaker doing "the Slav voice" isn't dialing into an internal option their own speech still has — there's no neutral [x] to reach for — they're specifically imitating an outside group's own real dialect.

## The Singsong Impression: Mocking a Melody Sisician Doesn't Have

Real Neo-Shtokavian BCS — the actual dialect of Sisician's living Slavic neighbors, as opposed to Sisician's own Kajkavian-descended ancestor (see [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]]) — has a genuine, famous four-way tonal pitch accent. Sisician's own prosody, descending from Kajkavian's **own distinct accentual system**, never had that melodic quality to begin with. This absence, like the /x/ asymmetry above, is exactly what makes it available for mockery: a Sisician doing "the Slav voice" exaggerates a rising-falling, singsong melody deliberately foreign to their own actual speech rhythm — mocking a real neighboring dialect's genuine prosodic feature precisely by performing more of it than any real speaker actually produces, a caricature built the same way as any exaggerated-accent impression, real or fictional.

## The Vulgar Consonant: Syllabic /r/

The third, quieter marker, fully developed on [[Sociolinguistic History]] § The "Eye-Spelling" Tradition: real bare syllabic /r//l/ (as in real BCS *vrt*, *smrt*, *prst*) reads as "vulgar Slavic" against Sisician's own standardized, epenthetic-vowel-corrected forms (*vart*, *smart*). Unlike the other two markers, this one isn't primarily a performance feature for mockery — it's a background class marker, always live in the language, that a caricature impression can additionally lean on by deliberately reverting to the bare, uncorrected consonant cluster form.

## Putting the Three Together

A full "Slav voice" impression, as a Sisician speaker would actually perform it: harsh, exaggerated uvular [χ] wherever the real word would simply have standard **ç**; an artificially melodic, exaggerated singsong intonation contour Sisician's own speech doesn't otherwise have; and a deliberate reversion to bare, uncorrected syllabic /r/ where standard Sisician would use the epenthetic-vowel form. All three markers work by the same underlying logic — performing more of a real linguistic feature (a Slavic sound, a Slavic prosody, a Slavic cluster) than any of it survives in Sisician's own idealized self-image, precisely because Sisician's self-image is free to have drifted as far from that reality as the century of Dalmatian's own death allowed it to.
