# Founding Myth and Naming

See [[Sisician]] for the hub page and [[Culture/History]] § The Croatian Succession Crisis for the real founding event this page's legend departs from.

## The Founder: Vitalis Madius de Corinio (decided 2026-09-25)

**Vitalis Madius de Corinio** — in the contemporary Dalmatian Romance vernacular, **Vitale Madi de Karin**. The founding elite originates in a **Dalmatian coastal city**, with **Zadar** preferred — Zadar keeps the founding family within the political world of medieval Croatia while giving it a clear Dalmatian identity. The project does not require reconstructing historical Zadar Dalmatian perfectly; instead, use historical Dalmatian evidence as the foundation and construct a plausible **Zadar-associated Dalmatian** variety.

**The name, piece by piece:**
- **Vitalis** — a Late Antique/early medieval Latin personal name, the kind the Dalmatian urban patriciate kept using long after the Croatian royal line had moved to Slavic names like Krešimir, Gojslav, and Držislav. Marks him instantly as Latin-literate and culturally distinct from the inland nobility.
- **Madius** — a Romance Dalmatian patronymic (he would sign a Latin charter *Ego Vitalis Madius* or *Vitalis filius Madii*), reinforcing the urban, Mediterranean identity a Slavic patronymic like *Vitalislav* would undercut.
- **de Corinio** — his frontier holding: **Karin** (Roman *Corinium*), on the Karin Sea in **Sidraga župa**, southeast of Zadar. Karin is real, attested, and — critically — **lay, not ecclesiastical**: after the Gothic-era ruin of the Roman town, it was rebuilt and became a seat of Croatian nobles well before 1000 AD, unlike Vrana (a Benedictine monastic property from the 9th century, granted to the Papacy in 1076, only fortified as a military holding under the Templars in the later 12th century — the locative originally proposed for this founder, replaced after that anachronism was caught and verified against the real record).

**Wealth without anachronism**: urban rents and maritime commerce from his Zadar patrician holdings, plus lay tolls and fisheries on the Karin Sea — deliberately *not* claiming the large monastic salt operations documented in this region, since those belong to the Church, not a lay frontier lord.

**Explicitly fictional, and explicitly not the same lineage as the real, later-attested Karinjani.** The real Karinjani (a branch of the Lapčan family) only take that name in the **14th century** — three centuries after this story's setting — and the real Kukar family (one of the twelve tribes named in the 1102 Pacta Conventa) is a *separate* lineage entirely, not the same as Karinjani despite both being tied to the broader Sidraga region. Vitalis's family are lay lords of Karin **before** any of that — he loses the seat when he flees north, and the real, later-documented Karinjani take Karin generations afterward as an entirely different family. Coincidental namesake geography, not invented continuity with an attested lineage.

The founder himself: from Zadar; a Dalmatian noble; Romance-speaking; urban; Latin-literate; accustomed to charters; familiar with Mediterranean commerce; connected to the Croatian royal court; politically ambitious; culturally distinct from inland Slavic nobility; militarily capable; politically unlucky; sufficiently wealthy to travel; and not so powerful that his disappearance destabilizes the entire Croatian kingdom — a middling frontier commander (*castellanus*) holding Karin for the Crown, not a great lord.

His descendants should gradually acquire Slavic names, giving a visible naming shift that mirrors the linguistic transition: **Vitalis** (Dalmatian/Latin) → **Son** (mixed) → **Grandson** (Slavic-Christian) → **Later dynasty** (fully local).

## The Political Tragedy — "The Wrong King"

When Stjepan Držislav dies (c. 997) and Svetoslav Suronja takes the throne, Vitalis — integrated into the coastal urban network that backs Svetoslav and favors continued Dalmatian-city privilege — backs him. When Krešimir and Gojslav rebel, Vitalis is caught holding a frontier fort against inland Slavic support for the brothers; when the brothers draw in outside powers (Bulgaria under Samuel, Venice under Pietro Orseolo II — see [[Culture/History]] § The Croatian Succession Crisis for the real events this departs from), Vitalis is exactly the kind of middling coastal commander who gets crushed in the resulting scramble. Wealthy enough to flee (to Venice or Byzantium), dangerous enough that the winning side wants him attainted, too middling to bring the kingdom down with him — which is precisely the profile this founder always needed.

## Marriage as the Language Engine

Marriage is a major mechanism of the shift. The founding generation marries locally. Their children have Romance-speaking grandparents, a Slavic-speaking parent, bilingual household servants, Orthodox clergy, and Slavic military companions. Within a few generations, bilingualism becomes normal; eventually, monolingual Sisician becomes normal. This should be a central part of the con-history — see [[Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] for the generation-by-generation linguistic detail this produces.

## The Founding Myth

The historical founder will almost inevitably become legendary. The real person: an ambitious nobleman who lost a succession war. The later legend: a faithful defender sent north by the rightful king. Potential legendary elements (all open/undecided): a banner; a sword; an oath; a Roman stone; a river crossing; a miraculous storm; an encounter with a monk; an old Roman prophecy; a Byzantine seal; a lost charter.

The important point: the founder's historical motivation can be opportunistic while the later national mythology becomes heroic.

## Naming — Open Questions

The polity and language currently use working English/historical names only:

- **Siscia** — ancient/Latin historical name
- **Sisak** — modern geographical referent
- **Sisician** — English name for the fictional language/culture (confirmed 2026-09-18)

**Decided 2026-09-25**: the founder's personal name — see § The Founder above, **Vitalis Madius de Corinio** / **Vitale Madi de Karin**. Unlike the names below, this one didn't need to wait on the sound-change chain: it's a real Dalmatian/Latin/Romance name (Vitalis, Madius) plus a real place-name (Corinio/Karin), not something derived *from* Sisician's own historical phonology the way the endonym or dynasty name will be.

**Decided 2026-09-25: the native endonym — сисачки (sisački).** The first name actually *derived from* the sound-change chain rather than cited from real history, now that [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes]] exists to derive it from. Full derivation on [[../Words/сисачки|Words/сисачки]]: the real, traceable toponymic chain Segestica→Siscia→Siscium (the historically correct c. 1000 citation form)→**Сисак** (the town's own vernacular Slavic name, landing on the same shape as real modern Croatian *Sisak* since nothing about this word triggers Sisician's own divergence points), plus the real, productive Common Slavic language-adjective suffix *\*-ьskъ* (the same suffix behind Polish *polski*, Russian *русский*, Serbian *српски*) — giving **сисачки** via already-established first palatalization (к→ч) and the same long-form adjective *-и* ending [[../Words/нув|нув]] already uses. Confirmed via search against the real attested adjective *sisački* (*Sisačko-moslavačka županija*, the real Croatian county name). Used substantivized, the way real Slavic language names always are (compare real *hrvatski*, elliptical for *hrvatski jezik*) — and deliberately in the native Slavic layer, not the Dalmatian one, matching the real cross-linguistic tendency for self-designations to stay native even in heavily contact-influenced languages.

**Decided 2026-09-25 (same day): the name of the people — сишчани (siščani), not the first-guessed *Сисачани*.** Full derivation on [[../Words/сишчани|Words/сишчани]]. Same root as сисачки (Сиск-), but a different real Common Slavic suffix, *\*-aninъ* ("one belonging to a place" — real Croatian *građanin* "citizen," *seljanin* "villager"), triggered by the glide */j/* rather than a front vowel — and when /j/ follows a whole cluster ending in /k/ (here, the root's own *-sk-*), the *cluster itself* undergoes a distinct, real, cross-Slavic sound law, ***skj → šč***, not the single-consonant к→ч palatalization сисачки's own *-ьskъ* suffix triggers. Confirmed via search against the real attested Croatian demonym *Siščanin/Siščanka/Siščani* — the user's own first guess (Сисачани, by analogy with real *Zagrepčani*) didn't survive the check, the same kind of real correction as Vrana→Karin, just smaller in narrative stakes. **Working out this word also caught and fixed a real internal inconsistency in сисачки's own already-published derivation** (see that word's own page) — its Step 2 had a yer "drop" while somehow leaving a vowel behind, which doesn't follow; corrected the same day.

**Still undecided**, and deliberately left open until they too get an actual derivation pass:

- name of the polity
- name of the dynasty
- the title of the lord

**Guiding principle, now demonstrated twice, not just stated: don't invent a name — derive it, and check the result against the real record even when the derivation feels solid.** сисачки and сишчани are the proof of concept: every step reused an already-decided, already-cited mechanism, and checking сишчани's own predicted form against real Croatian caught both a wrong initial guess *and* a logical gap in the previous word's own writeup. The remaining names (polity, dynasty, title) should get the same treatment once picked up.
