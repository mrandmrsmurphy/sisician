# Founding Myth and Naming

See [[Sisician]] for the hub page and [[Culture/History]] § The Croatian Succession Crisis for the real founding event this page's legend departs from.

## The Founder and His Origins

The founding elite originates in a **Dalmatian coastal city**, with **Zadar** preferred — Zadar keeps the founding family within the political world of medieval Croatia while giving it a clear Dalmatian identity. The project does not require reconstructing historical Zadar Dalmatian perfectly; instead, use historical Dalmatian evidence as the foundation and construct a plausible **Zadar-associated Dalmatian** variety.

The founder himself should be: from Zadar; a Dalmatian noble; Romance-speaking; urban; Latin-literate; accustomed to charters; familiar with Mediterranean commerce; connected to the Croatian royal court; politically ambitious; culturally distinct from inland Slavic nobility; militarily capable; politically unlucky; sufficiently wealthy to travel; and not so powerful that his disappearance destabilizes the entire Croatian kingdom.

His original name should be historically Dalmatian/Latin rather than invented from modern Croatian. His descendants should gradually acquire Slavic names, giving a visible naming shift that mirrors the linguistic transition: **Founder** (Dalmatian/Latin) → **Son** (mixed) → **Grandson** (Slavic-Christian) → **Later dynasty** (fully local).

## Marriage as the Language Engine

Marriage is a major mechanism of the shift. The founding generation marries locally. Their children have Romance-speaking grandparents, a Slavic-speaking parent, bilingual household servants, Orthodox clergy, and Slavic military companions. Within a few generations, bilingualism becomes normal; eventually, monolingual Sisician becomes normal. This should be a central part of the con-history — see [[Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] for the generation-by-generation linguistic detail this produces.

## The Founding Myth

The historical founder will almost inevitably become legendary. The real person: an ambitious nobleman who lost a succession war. The later legend: a faithful defender sent north by the rightful king. Potential legendary elements (all open/undecided): a banner; a sword; an oath; a Roman stone; a river crossing; a miraculous storm; an encounter with a monk; an old Roman prophecy; a Byzantine seal; a lost charter.

The important point: the founder's historical motivation can be opportunistic while the later national mythology becomes heroic.

## Naming — Open Questions

The polity and language currently use working English/historical names only:

- **Siscia** — ancient/Latin historical name
- **Sisak** — modern geographical referent
- **Sisician** — English name for the fictional language/culture (confirmed 2026-09-18)

**Still undecided**, and deliberately left open until the historical phonology exists:

- the native endonym
- name of the people
- name of the polity
- name of the dynasty
- the founder's personal name
- the title of the lord
- name of the modern language (as spoken by its own speakers, i.e. the endonym above)

**Guiding principle: do not invent the endonym until the sound changes are known.** These names should be generated from the historical phonology (Phase 2–3 of [[Conlang Workflow]]), not invented arbitrarily ahead of it.
