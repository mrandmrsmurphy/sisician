# Religion

See [[Sisician]] for the hub page and [[Culture/History]] § 1042 — The Byzantine Turn for the political event this page elaborates.

## The Byzantine Turn (1042)

Around 1042, in the alternate timeline, Byzantine power pushes back into the Balkans and the Siscia lords choose Constantinople — described as **"survival, not piety."** They accept the authority of the Patriarch of Constantinople, producing the extraordinary religious condition: **a Latin-rite cultural elite that becomes Byzantine-rite/Orthodox.** The earlier working phrase for this was **"Latin-rite Orthodox enclave"** — a historical anomaly rather than a standard ecclesiastical category.

The founding population is Dalmatian/Romance-speaking, Latin-literate, and culturally connected to the western Adriatic, politically surrounded by Latin and Slavic powers. But the ruling polity adopts Byzantine Christianity, Constantinopolitan ecclesiastical allegiance, eventually Church Slavonic liturgy, and Greek theological influence. The result: **Romance-speaking / Latin-literate / Byzantine-rite frontier aristocracy** — one of the defining peculiarities of Sisician civilization. The Great Schism of 1054 therefore becomes less a choice than a confirmation; the dynasty has already chosen its side.

## The Religious Ecology

The religious situation should not be simplified to "Orthodox = Greek-speaking." The intended ecology is more complicated, stratified by social function:

- **Clergy:** Church Slavonic becomes increasingly important.
- **Theology:** Greek provides prestige terminology.
- **Court:** Latin/Dalmatian survives for charters and administration.
- **People:** South Slavic is the ordinary vernacular.

Thus the emerging culture has three major written/liturgical traditions: **Latin** (ancestral/charter tradition), **Greek** (theological/Byzantine tradition), and **Church Slavonic** (Slavic liturgical vernacular). This three-way split is one of the reasons Sisician can develop such an unusual lexical profile — see [[Etymology/Semantic Domains]] for the specific vocabulary domains each tradition contributes.

## The Church as a Stabilizer

The Church prevents the language from becoming an undifferentiated mixture. It provides a stable learned tradition — Church Slavonic — giving Sisician speakers a reference language for prayer, Scripture, liturgy, theology, and ecclesiastical law. Greek provides another prestige language. Thus Sisician's Romance vocabulary does not invade everything; the language develops a cultural division:

> Romance for city. Slavic for earth. Slavonic/Greek for heaven.

This is one of the strongest conceptual formulations for the project — it's the reason the [[Etymology/Semantic Domains|three-layer lexical model]] holds together rather than collapsing into an undifferentiated mix.
