# Flag

See [[Sisician]] for the hub page, [[Culture/Political Identity]] for the political logic the flag compresses, and [[Culture/Religion]] for the Byzantine-rite side.

## Design

A modern national flag in 2:3 proportions, with no text or lettering on it.

- **Field:** deep Byzantine imperial purple.
- **Cross:** a large, plain, equal-armed gold Greek cross in the upper hoist (left), with slightly flared arm ends.
- **River:** a single wavy band across the lower third, running the full width of the flag. From top to bottom: red, white, blue, white, red.

## Symbolism

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Purple and gold | The Byzantine-rite, Orthodox side of the civilization ([[Culture/Religion]]). Purple and gold are also unusual among the Slavic neighbours, which suits the standing thread of distinctness from them. |
| Gold Greek cross | Orthodox Christianity. Deliberately generic: no inscription, no Byzantine imperial eagle, so it claims the rite and not the empire. |
| Wavy band | The Sava, the organizing geographical metaphor of the civilization ([[Culture/Geography and Economy]] § The River as Cultural Structure): "we are the people who stand between worlds because the river passes through us." |
| Red, white and blue | The Slavic palette, kept inside the river so that the flag does not read as simply Pan-Slavic. |

The flag as a whole mirrors the political self-image in [[Culture/Political Identity]]: a Byzantine-rite polity (purple, cross) whose identity is the Sava crossing (the river), with the Slavic population present but not dominant in the design.
