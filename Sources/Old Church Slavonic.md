# Sources: Old Church Slavonic

The real, historically-attested 9th–10th century literary Slavic language (the Cyril-and-Methodius mission), based on the Slavic dialect spoken around Thessaloniki — an early Bulgarian/Macedonian-type South Slavic variety. This is imported wholesale as Sisician's liturgical/learned register, per [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] Phase 1 and [[../Culture/Religion|Culture/Religion]] — it is **not** the direct ancestor of Sisician's vernacular grammar (that's [[Late Common Slavic]]'s local dialect instead). See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Vowels

/i y u e ě ę ǫ o a/, plus the two reduced vowels ("jers"): **ъ** (back jer, a short reduced back vowel) and **ь** (front jer, a short reduced front vowel) — see [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Etymology/Fall of the Jers]] for their eventual loss/vocalization (a vernacular-side development; OCS itself is imported as a fixed liturgical register, per this page's own framing, so it isn't itself subject to this Sisician-internal sound change). **ě** ("yat") is a distinct long open vowel of disputed exact phonetic quality. **ę** and **ǫ** are nasal vowels (front and back respectively) — real, attested Common Slavic nasal vowels that OCS preserves and that most later Slavic languages lost or converted to oral vowels + nasal consonant.

## Consonants

The standard Slavic consonant set (p b t d k g, s z, m n, l r, v), plus the products of Common Slavic's historical palatalizations and iotation — real, attested, and directly relevant to Sisician's own palatal series (see [[../Phonology/Design Principles|Phonology/Design Principles]] § Palatalization):

- **š ž** — from the first (regressive) palatalization of velars before front vowels, plus iotation of /s z/.
- **č** — from the first palatalization of /k/, and from iotation of /k/+j.
- **c** (and marginally **dz**) — from the second/third (progressive) palatalizations of velars.
- **l with epenthetic softening** (the "epenthetic l") after labials before /j/ — e.g. Proto-Slavic *zemja → OCS zemlja "land."
- **The št/žd reflex of Proto-Slavic *tj and *dj** — OCS's signature outcome for these clusters, e.g. *světja → **svěšta** "candle," *medja → **mežda** "border." **This is the register contrast, and it's three-tier, not two** (revised 2026-09-24 — see [[Late Common Slavic]] § Consolidated Phonology for the middle tier's own 2026-09-20 decision, which postdates and supersedes this page's earlier two-tier framing): [[Late Common Slavic]]'s colloquial vernacular has merged ć/đ away entirely (Kajkavian-based: *svěča*, *meja*, no ć/đ phonemes at all); a middle **conservative literary spelling** tier, reflecting genuine ongoing scribal/clerical contact with the broader Shtokavian-speaking world, keeps a handful of legal/administrative/formal-register words with a real, distinct ć/đ pronunciation (not št/žd — that shape is OCS's alone); and the top, true-imported-OCS tier alone uses the št/žd forms (*svěšta*, *mežda*). So ć/đ-*type* affricates survive in the middle tier; št/žd-*type* clusters survive only in the top, liturgical tier — two distinct foreign/learned overlays sitting above the fully-merged colloquial vernacular, not one.

## Prosody

OCS manuscripts don't mark stress or pitch accent, and it isn't fully recoverable — treat this as genuinely unknown rather than assume a specific system.

## Script

Historically written first in **Glagolitic** (Cyril's invention), then increasingly in **Cyrillic** (developed by his disciples in his honor). This connects directly to [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] § Orthographic History — OCS's own script history is part of why Sisician ends up written in Cyrillic despite its Latin-literate founding elite.
