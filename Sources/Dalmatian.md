# Sources: Dalmatian

The founding elite's language (see [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] and [[../Culture/Founding Myth and Naming|Culture/Founding Myth and Naming]]), derived from [[Vulgar Latin]] as needed, per [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] Phase 1. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## What's Real vs. What's Ours to Build

Real historical Dalmatian is directly attested only much later and only in two fragmentary branches: **Vegliote** (the Krk/Veglia dialect, extinct 1898, documented by Matteo Bartoli from its last speaker) and **Ragusan** (Dubrovnik, known only from scattered fragments). Neither is exactly what we need: both post-date 1000 AD by centuries, and **our founder is specifically from Zadar** — a third Dalmatian center whose own variety was never directly recorded at all.

Per the founding design document's own explicit principle ([[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]] § Why Dalmatian), this gap is a feature, not a problem: "enough gaps in the record to permit substantial creative reconstruction." So the plan is: anchor to Dalmatian's real, well-documented general tendencies (below), then construct a specific **Zadar-type Dalmatian, c. 1000 AD** that isn't required to match Vegliote or Ragusan exactly — informed by them, not bound by them.

## Real Anchoring Facts (safe to build from)

- **Diphthongization of stressed close-mid vowels.** Dalmatian's best-known distinguishing trait among Romance languages: Vulgar Latin's stressed **ē** and **ō** (see [[Vulgar Latin]] § Vowels) diphthongize — an unusual development shared, independently, by French. This is the single most Dalmatian-flavored sound law available and a signature feature. See § Diphthongization of Stressed Mid Vowels below for the specific rule.
- **Two directly attested reference points**, useful for comparison once we start deriving actual words: Vegliote (northern, more conservative/divergent) and Ragusan (southern, closer to Italian-influenced coastal norms). Zadar sits geographically and presumably dialectally between them.

## Diphthongization of Stressed Mid Vowels

Adopted wholesale from the real, well-documented Vegliote pattern (Bartoli) — the best-attested Dalmatian evidence available, used directly for Zadar-Dalmatian per this page's own method (anchor to real Dalmatian, since the Zadar variety itself is unattested).

**Conditioning factor: syllable structure, not the following vowel.** This is the important typological point — Dalmatian's diphthongization is *not* like Neapolitan or Romanian metaphony, where a following high vowel triggers the diphthong. It patterns instead with the open-syllable diphthongization of Old French or northern Italian dialects: the syllable structure *at the time of the change* determines the outcome, independent of what vowel follows.

**Two-stage development**, applying to stressed [[Vulgar Latin]] **ē** and **ō**:

1. **Stage 1:** stressed ē/ō diphthongize to rising diphthongs — **ē → ie**, **ō → uo**.
2. **Stage 2:** the outcome splits by syllable structure:
   - **Open syllable → monophthongize:** ie → **i**, uo → **u**
   - **Closed syllable → open further:** ie → **ja** (i.e. [ja]/[jä]), uo → **wa** (i.e. [wa])

| Latin | Syllable | Vowel | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| pētra "stone" | open (pē-tra) | ē | **pitra** |
| lōcu "place" | open (lō-cu) | ō | **luk** |
| fēsta "feast" | closed (fēs-ta) | ē | **fyasta** |
| mōrte "death" | closed (mōr-te) | ō | **mwart** |

**Open note for later (Phase 3, not now):** the closed-syllable outcome introduces a /w/ glide (*mwart*) that [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] doesn't currently have as a phoneme (only /j/ is listed as a glide) — South Slavic phonology generally doesn't have /w/ distinct from /v/ either. Whether Dalmatian loans with this /w/ get adapted to /v/ (or a /v/+glide cluster, or something else) when they enter Sisician proper is a Phase 3 adaptation question, not a Dalmatian-internal one — Dalmatian itself keeps its own /w/ here regardless of what happens to it later.

## No Lenition of Intervocalic Voiceless Stops

**Resolves the ambiguity flagged in [[Vulgar Latin]] § Consonants — in favor of preservation.** Dalmatian keeps Latin intervocalic voiceless stops /p t k/ as voiceless; it does not undergo the Western Romance lenition (voicing) that French/Spanish/Portuguese show. This places Dalmatian with the conservative/"peripheral Romance" languages (Italian, Romanian, Sardinian) rather than Western Romance on this isogloss.

Evidence, via the oldest stratum of Dalmatian loanwords into Croatian (as distinct from later Venetian loans, which do show lenition): **kapula** "onion" keeps its voiceless /p/, contrasting with Venetian-sourced **cipolla**'s lenited outcome; **kenur** "dinner" keeps its voiceless velar where Italian **cenare** instead palatalizes it (see next section). This pattern — "očuvani su latinski intervokalni bezvučni okluzivi" (preserved Latin intervocalic voiceless occlusives) — is cited as a diagnostic feature of the oldest Dalmatian loan stratum specifically.

## Velars Preserved Before /e/, Palatalize Before /i/

**Not a uniform "front vowels preserved" rule.** Wikipedia's own *Dalmatian language* article states the real, distinctive fact directly: Dalmatian is "the only Romance language that has palatalised /k/ and /ɡ/ before /i/, but not before /e/," citing exactly this example: Latin **civitate → Vegliot cituot** "city" (real, attested — see [[../Words/читвот|Words/читвот]] for the full derivation into Sisician). This is a genuinely different, more specific rule than "front vowels uniformly preserved":

- **Before /e/, velars stay velar** — well-evidenced: **cena → kaina** "dinner," **cenare → kenur** "to dine" (also cited in § No Lenition above) both show real Croatian coastal-dialect loanwords with intervocalic /k/ intact before /e/.
- **Before /i/, velars palatalize to affricates** (č from k, presumably dž from g by the same logic, though no g+i example is attested yet) — **civitate → cituot** is the real citation for this half of the rule.

**The "dicere → dekro" example doesn't bear on this rule at all**: DICERE's initial consonant is /d/, a **dental** stop, not a velar. Whether dentals behave like velars before /i/ (palatalizing) or unlike them (staying put, as *dekro* would suggest) is an **open, unverified question** — *dekro* itself hasn't been independently confirmed as a real attested Croatian-coastal loanword the way *kapula*/*kaina*/*kenur* have.

Still shared with Sardinian on the /e/ side (Sardinian *chentu* vs. Italian *cento* "hundred" — resisting palatalization before *e*), but the /i/-specific palatalization is a genuinely distinctive Dalmatian feature, not shared with the Sardinian comparandum.

## Additional Diphthong Outcomes, Not Yet Reconciled With the Model Above

*Cituot*'s own stressed syllable continues Latin CIVITĀTE's long **ā** (in *-tāte*), not ē or ō — meaning real Dalmatian evidently diphthongizes stressed **/a/ → /uo/** too, a process this page's existing Stage 1/Stage 2 model (which only touches ē/ō) doesn't cover at all. **A second independent attestation makes this more than a one-off**: real Vegliote *tuota* "father" (from Latin TATA, stressed short *a*) shows the identical *a→uo* correspondence. Search-engine summaries (not a primary source directly read — see [[../Words/читвот|Words/читвот]]'s own note on this) also suggest a parallel **/o/ → /ua/ or /au/** development (candidate examples *buask* "forest," *naun* "not," not independently verified here).

**Deliberately left unintegrated with the existing ē/ō model rather than forced into it**: there's no reliable account of what conditions *a→uo* (unconditional? syllable-structure-dependent, like ē/ō? a separate, later analogical spread from the ē/ō pattern?), and stacking a guessed conditioning rule on top of two real data points would be exactly the overbuilding [[../AIOS/memory/feedback_dont_overbuild_from_examples|feedback_dont_overbuild_from_examples]] warns against. Real, cited, and currently unmodeled — a genuine gap for a future session with better source access to close, not a decision made here.

**A third real outcome, from a second real word: real Vegliote *monaita* "coin" (< Latin MONĒTA, classical stress mo-NĒ-ta) shows stressed ē → ai, not this page's existing ē→ie rule.** See [[../Words/монајта|Words/монајта]]. Taken together, three real attested data points (*cituot*'s a→uo, *tuota*'s a→uo, *monaita*'s e→ai) sit outside the original Stage 1/Stage 2 model, which was built from only four words (*pētra, lōcu, fēsta, mōrte*) and may itself be under-generalized from too small a sample. Not yet resolved into a revised rule: three data points constrain a future account more than zero did, but don't yet amount to one.

## Degemination

Dalmatian degeminates (loses double/geminate consonants), unlike Italo-Romance, which generally keeps or even innovates geminates. This doesn't contradict the no-lenition finding above — it's a narrower, separate process, and one of the few lenition-type changes Dalmatian does share with Western Romance and Balkan Romance despite otherwise patterning conservative on the two points above.

**Exception: -NN- and -LL- palatalize before degeminating, rather than just losing their second consonant like every other geminate.** Real, well-attested Ibero-Romance precedent, not an invented exception: Latin **ANNUS** "year" → Spanish **año** [ɲ] (not †ano); Latin **CABALLUS** "horse" → Spanish **caballo**, historically /ʎ/ before modern yeísmo merged it with /j/ (Old Spanish *ll* was phonemically /ʎ/, a real, attested stage, not the modern merged pronunciation). This gives Dalmatian a **second, independent route into Sisician's native ɲ/ʎ**, alongside the already-attested -GN-/-LI- route (§ End-Stage Phonology below) — two unrelated Latin sources converging on the same already-occupied Sisician slot. Every other geminate (pp tt kk mm rr ss ff) still degeminates in the ordinary way; this is specifically an NN/LL diversion, not a reopening of the general rule. No word has used it yet.

## TJ/CJ and DJ/GJ Palatalize to Affricates

Consonant+yod clusters /tj/ /kj/ and /dj/ /gj/ merge into affricate outcomes — separately from, and despite, the preservation facts above (clusters palatalize even though plain intervocalic stops and pre-front-vowel velars don't). Confirmed examples for the voiceless pair (/tj/, /kj/ → **č**): **mrča < MYRTEA** "myrtle," **nevča < NĚPTIA**. The voiced pair (/dj/, /gj/) gives the parallel voiced affricate **dž** (matching the general "affricate reflexes" description).

Real comparative Romance confirms the same d+yod mechanism directly: Latin **RADIUS** "ray, spoke" → Italian **raggio** [ˈraddʒo]; Latin **DIURNUM** "daily" → Italian **giorno** [ˈdʒorno] — both well-attested /dj/-cluster-to-[dʒ] outcomes, the voiced counterpart of the exact mechanism already cited for mrča/nevča. Not a Dalmatian-attested example the way those two are — the same evidentiary tier as дешет/ноj's Sardinian comparanda elsewhere in this vault.

## Intervocalic /s/ Voices to [z]

A separate process from the no-lenition finding above — that rule concerns voiceless *stops* specifically; fricatives are independent. **Dalmatian's intervocalic /s/ voices to [z]**, exactly paralleling standard Italian's real, well-attested behavior (*casa* → [ˈkaza], *rosa* → [ˈrɔːza]) — and Italian is the closest available typological model for this, since it independently shows the identical pairing Dalmatian has: intervocalic voiceless stops stay voiceless (*capra*, not *cabra*), while intervocalic /s/ voices anyway. First application: Latin/Vulgar Latin **casa** "cottage, dwelling" → Dalmatian **kaza** (no diphthongization trigger — short a, not stressed ē/ō; no stop to lenite; this one change is the only relevant step) — see [[../Words/каза|Words/каза]] for what happens to this word once it enters Sisician proper.

## /rj/ Metathesizes to a Diphthong, Not a New Consonant

**Rounds out the Cj-cluster family** (alongside NJ/LJ→ɲ/ʎ, TJ/KJ→č, DJ/GJ→dž, SJ/ZJ→š/ž): what happens to Latin **-ARIU-**/-ORIU- type clusters, where the yod follows an /r/. Checked against real cross-Romance behavior first, since /r/ doesn't palatalize into a clean new consonant the way nasals, laterals, and sibilants do — no attested Romance variety has a phonemic "palatal r," and this vault has already firmly rejected inventing a systematic hard/soft consonant pairing (see [[../AIOS/memory/reference_south_slavic_palatalization|reference_south_slavic_palatalization]]), so a new rʲ phoneme was never on the table.

**Real, well-documented mechanism instead: glide metathesis.** Vulgar Latin /rj/ first palatalizes to a transitional [rʲ], then the glide **metathesizes — jumping to before the /r/ instead of after it** — giving [i̯r]: real Classical Latin **PARIA** (neuter plural of *par* "equal") → Vulgar Latin *parʲa* → **[pai̯ra]** → Old French **paire** "pair." This is cited, real, and specifically documented as *not* the same outcome as the other Cj clusters — a genuinely distinct fourth strategy (new consonant / fricative / diphthong-via-metathesis, three different outcomes for three different consonant classes, not one mechanism forced onto all of them).

**Adopted directly for Dalmatian, matching its already-established fondness for diphthongs**: /rj/ → the yod jumps left onto the preceding vowel, forming a diphthong, while /r/ surfaces plain immediately after. First application: [[../Words/денајр|денајр]] ("money," < DENARIUS) — see that page for the full derivation. This gives the -rj- cluster a genuinely different (and more interesting) fate than simply vanishing the way it does in Italian (*denaro*, no trace of r-adjacent yod at all) or surfacing as a plain vowel change the way it does in Spanish (*dinero*) — Dalmatian's own outcome is a real, cited, and distinctively *its own*.

## TR/DR Affricate to č/dž — a Third, Unrelated Kind of R-Effect

**Not part of the Cj-cluster family above — this is a completely different mechanism, triggered by /r/ itself, not a yod.** Real attested Vegliote **respondro** "to answer/respond" (< Latin RESPONDEŌ/RESPONDĒRE, cited on Wiktionary) has a genuine **-dr-** onset cluster (stop + rhotic, syllabifying as re-spon-**dro**, not split across syllables) — a structurally unrelated environment from every Cj rule above (no yod anywhere in sight).

**Real, well-documented, cross-linguistic mechanism: rhotic-triggered affrication of alveolar stop+r onset clusters.** This is not a Romance-specific or Dalmatian-specific quirk — it's independently documented for English itself: /tr/ and /dr/ as a single syllable onset (not split across a syllable boundary, e.g. *train*, *dragon* — contrast heterosyllabic *rat-race*, *hand-rail*, which don't affricate) are realized as post-alveolar affricates, [t̠ɹ̥]/[d̥̠ɹ], close enough to /tʃ/-/dʒ/ that some English dialects genuinely merge *train*/*chain*. Applying the same general phonetic tendency to Dalmatian's own onset **dr**: **respon-dro → respon-džo**, giving **респонџо**.

**One further step beyond the real citation, honestly flagged as this page's own extension**: real English affrication keeps a residual rhotic quality ([dʒr], not plain [dʒ]) — Dalmatian's own outcome is presented as going one step further, fully absorbing the /r/ into the affricate with no trace left (plain dž, not džr), since Sisician's own coda/cluster phonotactics has no particular need to preserve it and simplification-on-borrowing is already the norm elsewhere in this vault (final-vowel apocope, degemination). **Predicted voiceless counterpart, not yet exemplified**: TR → č, by the same mechanism and the same voiced/voiceless pairing already used throughout the Cj-cluster family (tj/kj→č pairs with dj/gj→dž; sj→š pairs with zj→ž) — presumed by symmetry only, the same evidentiary status dž itself had before *raggio*/*giorno* confirmed it.

## /ks/ and Sibilant+Yod Clusters Give š/ž

A second, independent route into Sisician's postalveolar *fricative* pair, alongside the affricate route above:

- **Latin -X- [ks] simplifies to š.** Real, well-attested across Ibero-Romance, and crucially *not* dependent on the front-vowel velar palatalization Dalmatian has already rejected (§ above): Portuguese **coxa** [ˈkoʃɐ] < COXA "hip, thigh"; Old Spanish **dexar** [deˈʃar] < LAXARE "to leave." Because this is a cluster-internal simplification (/ks/ → /ʃ/), not a vowel-conditioned palatalization, it doesn't run into the same problem an earlier, dropped idea did — Italian's real sc-before-front-vowel → š mechanism (*pesce*, *scienza*) only works because Italian palatalizes velars before front vowels in the first place, which Dalmatian doesn't.
- **/sj/ and /zj/ palatalize to š and ž respectively**, joining the general "consonant+yod clusters palatalize, outcome varies by branch" pattern already flagged on [[Vulgar Latin]] § Consonants — Dalmatian specifically chooses the fricative (not affricate) outcome for this particular pair. **Doubly motivated, the same category as -GN-/-LI-→ɲ/ʎ and TJ/KJ→č above**: this isn't an arbitrary pick among the branch-variable options — š/ž is also the *exact* outcome Common Slavic's own iotation gives for s+j/z+j (see [[Old Church Slavonic]] § Consonants), so a Dalmatian sj/zj-cluster word and a native Slavic one converge on the identical modern sound by two genuinely independent historical routes, the same convergence *type* as нув, just at the phoneme level rather than the whole-word level.

No word has been coined through either pathway yet — both are available mechanisms, not yet worked examples, the same status мварт had before it was connected to anything.

## Rare Mechanism: ts/dz via Boundary Reanalysis (Not a Regular Rule)

Unlike ɲ/ʎ/č/dž/š/ž above, Sisician's ts/dz (ц/ѕ) have no *regular* Dalmatian-internal source once TJ/KJ is committed to č rather than an Italian-style ts outcome. Worth noting directly: real Vegliote's own attested inventory (§ End-Stage Phonology below) **does** include ts and dz as phonemes — meaning the wider real Dalmatian dialect continuum evidently could produce them where Zadar-Dalmatian's own constructed chain instead converges on č/dž. That's a live option worth remembering, not a dead end.

Two rare, individually-motivated pathways (not blanket rules) are available for an isolated word, should one ever call for it:

1. **Morpheme/word-boundary reanalysis**: a Latin or Dalmatian sequence where /t/+/s/ or /d/+/z/ end up adjacent only because they belonged to two separate original words or morphemes, later fused/univerbated into a single Sisician word, could surface with a genuine ts/dz cluster — not a sound law, a one-off junctural accident, the same rarity class as [[../Words/абкле|абкле]]'s hybrid compounding.
2. **Dialect-contact borrowing**: a word could enter Sisician via a differently-behaving neighboring real Dalmatian-speaking center (Zadar, Krk/Vegliote, and Ragusa were three genuinely distinct, only loosely-related Dalmatian varieties — § What's Real vs. What's Ours to Build above) rather than through Zadar's own main chain, carrying real Vegliote's own ts/dz outcome instead of Zadar's č/dž.

Neither is built yet; flagged as available, rare, lexically-specific texture for a future word — exactly the status дешет and ноj had before they existed.

No specific Zadar-Dalmatian phoneme inventory has been assembled as a single table yet — the individual sound changes above are decided piecemeal; someone (a future session, or now) still needs to compile them into one coherent phoneme-by-phoneme picture and check they don't conflict with each other.

## End-Stage Phonology: Zadar-Dalmatian, c. 1300 (Just Before Extinction)

**Heavy convergence, not conservative preservation.** Per [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] § 1300, Dalmatian dies as Siscia's community language by approximately 1300 — three centuries of shrinking, bilingual, increasingly minority speech, not a stable coexistence. The governing model here is explicitly **not** "how much can Dalmatian change while staying clearly Dalmatian" — it's the real historical trajectory of **Anglo-Norman French in England**: a language that genuinely stopped being anyone's native tongue, became visibly anglicized in its own internal grammar even in its final living generations (real, attested: "confusion of genders and the adding of *-s* to form all plurals," everyday vocabulary increasingly borrowed back from the dominant contact language), and was eventually reduced to a debased, semi-fossilized formal register (Law French) before dying outright — while leaving permanent, often *prestigious* fingerprints on the surviving matrix language. Dalmatian's own end-stage phonology should read the same way: this is Dalmatian in its death throes, not a museum-piece snapshot of "authentic" Dalmatian preserved for 300 years.

**This is deliberately unattested, invented territory, not a real-world parallel being applied.** Checked directly: neither of the two real attested Dalmatian branches actually died to Slavic pressure — Vegliote died to **Venetian Italian** assimilation (Bartoli's own informant, Tuone Udaina, had not spoken Vegliote in two decades and could only produce "Dalmatianised Venetian" by the time he was interviewed), and Ragusan's decline is only vaguely attested but involved Italian as much as Croatian contact. So "a Dalmatian variety dying specifically under three centuries of Slavic-dominant bilingualism" is Siscia's own invented scenario — exactly the kind of gap [[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]] § Why Dalmatian already flags as fair creative territory, not a case of contradicting an attested fact.

### Consonants — Barely Change, and That's the Real Finding

**Checked directly against real Vegliote's own attested consonant inventory** (p b t d k g; m n ɲ ŋ; r; f v s z j; l ʎ; ts dz tʃ dʒ) **and [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]]'s own Sisician target** (p b, t d, k g, ts dz, tʃ dʒ, f v, s z, ʃ ʒ, x, m n ɲ, l r ʎ, j): they already match almost phoneme-for-phoneme. This is a genuinely useful, load-bearing finding, not a coincidence to gloss over — it means Dalmatian's consonant system needed almost no convergence pressure to fit the emerging Sisician mold, because it was already typologically compatible. Two real points of friction, both already resolved:

- **/w/ (from closed-syllable diphthongization, e.g. *mwart*) merges into /v/ already inside dying Dalmatian itself**, in its final generations, not just at the later Sisician-borrowing step. This is presented as a real, plausible bilingual-accommodation effect: speakers whose dominant language (Slavic) has no /w/ increasingly produce their weaker language's /w/ as /v/ even in their own Dalmatian speech, the same way a shrinking speaker pool's pronunciation drifts toward whatever their stronger language allows — general language-obsolescence sociolinguistics (imperfect transmission and L1-dominant substitution becoming the new community norm as fluent speakers die off), not a citation for this specific case.
- **/ŋ/, phonemic in Vegliote's own attested inventory, is not phonemic in Sisician's target** (there it's just positional [n] before velars). By 1300, Zadar-Dalmatian's own /ŋ/ is treated as already having collapsed into plain /n/ + velar sequences the same way, converging on the surrounding Slavic system's own allophonic treatment rather than staying a separate segment.

**/ɲ/ and /ʎ/ need no convergence at all** — both are already native Sisician phonemes (from Slavic's own historical palatalization), so Dalmatian's real ones (from Latin *-gn-*, *-ll-*/*-li-* clusters) simply land in an already-occupied, compatible slot. Worth stating plainly rather than treating every overlap as an engineered coincidence: some of this convergence is just two typologically similar systems meeting, not contact pressure doing the work.

### Vowels — Where the Real Collapse Happens

**Against [[Vulgar Latin]]'s own 7-vowel system** (/i e ɛ a ɔ o u/, the open/close mid-vowel distinction already inherited from Classical Latin's long/short merger): **the open/close mid-vowel distinction merges — ɛ→e, ɔ→o — collapsing Dalmatian's own vowel system to exactly five qualities: /i e a o u/.** This is doubly motivated, not a single invented mechanism:

1. **A real, independently-attested Romance-internal tendency.** Open/close mid-vowel mergers of exactly this kind are common across real Romance varieties under various conditions (unstressed-position neutralization is especially widespread) — this isn't a change that requires Slavic contact to be plausible on its own.
2. **Reinforced by 300 years of contact pressure specifically**, converging toward the very same five-vowel shape [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] already sketches as Sisician's own target — meaning by the time Dalmatian actually dies, its surviving vowel system requires essentially no further adaptation to fit the language it's about to vanish into. The fit isn't coincidental world-building convenience; it's the intended payoff of "heavy convergence."

**The diphthong-derived alternation (open syllable → *i*/*u* monophthongs; closed syllable → *ja*/*wa*, per the c. 1000 rule above) is presented as leveling by 1300, not staying a live productive rule.** Real language-obsolescence linguistics documents exactly this kind of outcome: a conditioned morphophonemic alternation, productive while a language has a full community of fluent speakers, tends to get **lexicalized** (one outcome or the other generalizes per word, memorized rather than derived) once the speaker pool shrinks and the conditioning rule is no longer reliably transmitted to semi-speakers. So by 1300, a given Dalmatian word simply *has* its diphthong outcome fixed (e.g. always *pitra*-type or always *mwart*-type, now *mvart* after the /w/→/v/ merger), rather than the outcome being freshly computed from current syllable structure. This is general sociolinguistic knowledge about dying languages, not a citation specific to Dalmatian.

### Prosody — The Real Payoff, and Where Sisician's Own Open Question Gets Resolved

**This directly resolves an open question already sitting on [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] and [[Vulgar Latin]]'s own pages: the source of Sisician's "penultimate stress, with many exceptions."** [[Vulgar Latin]] § Stress already flags that Classical Latin's own stress rule (penult if heavy, otherwise antepenult) independently leans penultimate-with-exceptions, "worth keeping in mind once the Dalmatian-loanword stress-adaptation rules get worked out." Here is that resolution:

- **Words borrowed from Dalmatian *before* c. 1300's heavy convergence** (Phase 2, the initial bilingual generations) carry their **original Latin-descended stress pattern** into Sisician largely unchanged — these are the genuine "exceptions" to Sisician's otherwise-regular pattern, a real, motivated fossil layer rather than an arbitrary irregularity.
- **Dalmatian's own last generations of speakers, by 1300, were themselves already pronouncing Dalmatian with prosody drifting toward the surrounding Kajkavian accentual system** — a real, well-documented general effect of language death (a dying language's last fluent-to-semi-fluent speakers typically show substantial phonological transfer from their dominant L1, since the dying language functions as an effectively-L2 system even for people who "still speak it"). Words absorbed from *this* end-stage Dalmatian arrive already prosodically Slavic-shaped, needing no separate adaptation step.

This gives the stress-exception pattern a genuine two-layer historical explanation — not "Dalmatian words are irregular" as a bare fact, but "early loans fossilize the donor language's own real stress rule; late loans arrive pre-adapted" — exactly the kind of "every weird feature should have a historical reason" result [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] calls for.

### What This Section Deliberately Does Not Cover

- **Morphology/grammar.** Anglo-Norman's own real "gender confusion, generalized -s plural" pattern is cited above as the *model* for what death-driven leveling looks like, but Dalmatian's own grammar isn't Sisician's grammar donor (that's Slavic, by the project's core design) — so no attempt is made here to work out Dalmatian's own terminal-stage morphology in detail. It matters for texture/flavor if a Dalmatian phrase ever gets quoted in-world, not for Sisician's own grammar.
- **The separate, more important design point this whole conversation surfaced**: Sisician itself treating certain Dalmatian-derived pronunciations as *prestigious* — the real English/French parallel (idolizing "je ne sais quoi," mocking German's /x/) — is about Sisician's own register system, not Dalmatian's death. See [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle]] for that principle, recorded separately rather than folded into this page.
