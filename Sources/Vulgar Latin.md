# Sources: Vulgar Latin

The ancestor of [[Dalmatian]]. This page holds real, well-established comparative-Romance-linguistics consensus, not conlang invention — it's imported as needed to derive Dalmatian forms, per [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] Phase 1. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Vowels

Classical Latin's ten vowel qualities-by-length (ī ĭ ē ĕ ā ă ō ŏ ū ŭ) collapsed, as vowel *length* stopped being distinctive and vowel *quality* took over the load, into the standard 7-vowel Proto-Romance system:

**/i e ɛ a ɔ o u/**

Correspondence to Classical Latin:

| Classical Latin | Vulgar Latin |
|---|---|
| ī | i |
| ĭ, ē | e |
| ĕ | ɛ |
| ā, ă | a |
| ŏ | ɔ |
| ō, ŭ | o |
| ū | u |

This is the standard reference point for deriving any Western Romance daughter language, Dalmatian included.

## Consonants

- **Stops:** /p b t d k ɡ/
- **Fricatives:** /f s/ (Classical /h/ already silent by the Classical period; /w/ moving toward [β] and merging with /b/ intervocalically in many regions — "betacism," not universal, a live regional process rather than a completed one)
- **Nasals:** /m n/
- **Liquids:** /l r/
- **Glides:** /j w/ (from consonantal Latin i/u)

**Ongoing changes relevant to Dalmatian's later divergence** (in progress at different rates/dates across the Vulgar Latin-speaking world — not yet settled facts, but the raw material [[Dalmatian]] will need to make specific choices about):

- **Palatalization of /k g/ before front vowels** (e, i) — the process that eventually gives Italian's /tʃ dʒ/, French's /s ʒ/, etc., depending on daughter language and date.
- **Palatalization of consonant+yod clusters** (/tj/, /dj/, /kj/, and similar) — outcomes vary enormously by Romance branch.
- **Lenition (voicing/spirantization) of intervocalic voiceless stops** (/p t k/ → /b d ɡ/ or further) — a hallmark of "Western Romance" (Gaul, Iberia); largely absent from "Eastern Romance" (central/southern Italian, Romanian). **Dalmatian's position on this isogloss is genuinely debated/transitional in the real historical record** — generally described as patterning closer to Eastern Romance (retaining voiceless stops more often than French/Spanish/Portuguese do), but not uniformly. This ambiguity is useful: it leaves real design latitude for [[Dalmatian]] rather than a fact we'd be contradicting either way.

## Stress

Classical Latin's stress rule — the penult if heavy (long vowel or closed syllable), otherwise the antepenult, carries essentially unchanged into Vulgar Latin and underlies the stress patterns of every Romance daughter language. Interesting incidental note: this already leans penultimate-with-exceptions in its own right, which may end up reinforcing (or at least not fighting) [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]]'s chosen "penultimate stress, with many exceptions" for Sisician — worth keeping in mind once the Dalmatian-loanword stress-adaptation rules get worked out, since Common Slavic's own pitch-accent system (see [[Late Common Slavic]]) is the other independent input to that same target pattern.
