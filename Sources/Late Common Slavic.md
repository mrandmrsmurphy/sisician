# Sources: Late Common Slavic

The vernacular Slavic ancestor — the local South Slavic dialect actually spoken in the Sava basin c. 1000 AD (see [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] § South Slavic Settlement), as distinct from [[Old Church Slavonic]], which is a real historical liturgical import from a different (Thessaloniki-area) Slavic dialect. This is the variety that Sisician's core grammar and native vocabulary actually descend from. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page and [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] Phase 1.

## Vowels (Common Slavic inheritance)

- Plain vowels **i e a o u**
- The two reduced vowels ("jers"): **ъ** (back), **ь** (front) — see [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Etymology/Fall of the Jers]] for their eventual loss/vocalization, always followed
- **ě** ("yat"), a distinct long open vowel — its later reflex is the source of the real Serbo-Croatian Ekavian/Ijekavian/Ikavian dialect split, and will need its own decision once sound changes are worked out
- Nasal vowels **ę** (front), **ǫ** (back)
- **Pitch accent** (rising vs. falling tone) inherited from Balto-Slavic — South Slavic (Serbo-Croatian, Slovenian) is the branch that best preserves this; most other Slavic branches lost it. Directly relevant background for [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]]'s "penultimate stress, with many exceptions" decision: a real pitch-accent system is one plausible source of some of those exceptions, alongside Dalmatian loanword stress (see [[Vulgar Latin]] § Stress).

## Consonants (Common Slavic inheritance)

The same historical palatalization/iotation processes described in [[Old Church Slavonic]] apply here too — **š ž č c (dz)**, the epenthetic-l, etc. are shared Common Slavic inheritance, not OCS-specific.

**Local difference from OCS — needs refinement, see below:** Proto-Slavic *tj and *dj reflect in most of South Slavic (Shtokavian, Chakavian) as **ć** and **đ**, contrasting with OCS's št/žd. That clean contrast is complicated by the dialect decision below.

## Dialect Zone: Kajkavian-Leaning

By ~1000 AD the South Slavic dialect continuum is already differentiating into the ancestors of modern Kajkavian, Chakavian, and Shtokavian. The user chose **Kajkavian-leaning** — the subtler option, matching Siscia/Sisak's actual real-world dialect geography (the Sisak/Zagreb area is historically Kajkavian-speaking): the language inherits the literal dialect of the site itself, echoing the same "inherits and revives, doesn't invent" logic already established for the fortress and its Roman groundwork ([[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]] § Why Siscia).

Real Kajkavian characteristics to build from:

- **Yat reflex:** generally **e** (an Ekavian-type outcome), patterning with Slovenian, Bulgarian, and Macedonian rather than with Ijekavian/Ikavian Shtokavian or Chakavian.
- **The interrogative *kaj* ("what")** — not Shtokavian *što* or Chakavian *ča*. This is the single most recognizable Kajkavian marker (the dialect is literally named for it), and the clearest one-word diagnostic to keep visible in Sisician's own vernacular register once Phase 2 vocabulary work starts.
- **Toponymic evidence anchoring the choice specifically to this corridor:** medieval toponyms in the Bačka–Slavonia area — i.e. the real-world stretch overlapping [[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]]'s Sava–Drina/Pannonian setting — show sound changes matching Kajkavian rather than Chakavian, e.g. the preposition *vъ* ("in/at") is retained as **v** (not reduced to Shtokavian's *u*) and undergoes regular regressive devoicing to **[f]** before voiceless obstruents (*v kući* → **f kući**, "in the house," since /k/ is voiceless). This is a real, independently attested reason the medieval Sava–Danube vernacular specifically (not just "Kajkavian in general") is the right choice, not just Sisak's modern dialect geography.
- **Accentuation:** Kajkavian has **its own distinct stress system**, separate from — not just a simplification of — the Neo-Shtokavian pitch-accent system that later spread across most of Croatia. This is a deliberate design asset, not incidental: it's what will let Sisician's prosody feel genuinely different from standard (Neo-Shtokavian-based) Croatian rather than a reskin of it, and it sits comfortably with the already-chosen [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] target ("penultimate stress, with many exceptions") without a fight between the two source languages' prosody.
- **Closer affinity to Slovenian** than to Shtokavian/Chakavian generally; some classifications treat Kajkavian as transitional between Slovene and the rest of South Slavic.
- **Real historical Hungarian/German contact** is disproportionately heavy in the Kajkavian-speaking area (a fact of real Zagreb/northern-Croatian history) — a pleasant thematic echo of Sisician's own alternate-history Hungarian political layer ([[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] § 1102), even though that specific contact intensifies later and shouldn't be back-dated to this c. 1000 stage.

## The Vernacular Merges ć→č and đ→j

**Not a design choice — this is what real Kajkavian does, unambiguously, including specifically in the Sisak/Sava-corridor area.** Kajkavian merges the standard-Croatian /č/:/ć/ opposition entirely into a single **č** (e.g. *svěča* "candle," *noč* "night" — no separate ć), and merges /dž/:/đ/ mostly into **j** (occasionally dž) — e.g. *meja* "border," from *medja. This is one of the dialect group's defining features (per real dialectological sources: Kajkavian has "only one sign" where standard Croatian distinguishes č/ć, and speakers historically resisted orthographic reforms trying to impose the standard distinction).

**Consequence — the vernacular has no /ć/ or /đ/ phonemes at all.** They survive only as a **written/learned overlay**, and in fact as *two* distinct overlay registers, not one — see [[Old Church Slavonic]] § Consonants for the resulting three-tier system this creates (colloquial Kajkavian-merged / Sisician's own conservative literary spelling / true imported OCS).

## Consolidated Phonology: The Siscia Vernacular, c. 1000 AD

**Calibrated at roughly 85% toward standard Croatian/Serbian/Bosnian (Neo-Shtokavian-descended), with a deliberate, occasional lean toward Slovenian for variety** — the dialect-zone decisions above (Kajkavian-leaning, kaj, ć/đ→č/j merger, v-retention) **are the 85% baseline, not a deviation from it.** Kajkavian genuinely is a real BCS-family dialect (the "C" in BCS covers exactly this kind of regional variation), and its documented closer affinity to Slovenian is precisely the texture this calibration wants. The two intended "variety" touches sit elsewhere:

- **The Ekavian yat reflex (ě→e)** doesn't need a separate justification — it's simultaneously the real Serbian standard outcome *and* the Slovenian one, a free twofer already built into the dialect-zone decision above.
- **[[../Words/јаз|Words/јаз]]'s own already-cited etymology** (choosing the Slovene-affinity form *jaz* over Croatian-standard *ja*) is the clearest existing example of this exact principle already live in the lexicon — not something needing revision, but the model for how future "occasional lean" moments should look: small, individually motivated, cited exceptions, not a systemic shift.
- **The three-tier register system's middle "conservative literary spelling" tier** (§ above) is clarified here rather than left as an unexplained placeholder: it isn't just cautious/hypercorrect spelling for its own sake — it reflects genuine, ongoing contact between Siscia's scribal/clerical class and the broader Shtokavian-speaking Croatian political and ecclesiastical world (the same real royal-court and church channels already established in [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] § 1102, § Byzantine Turn). A handful of legal, administrative, and formal-register words plausibly keep or restore a genuine ć/đ-distinct, Shtokavian-flavored pronunciation in this middle tier specifically, standing apart from the fully-merged colloquial vernacular — the concrete "occasional BCS-standard lean" moment, scoped narrowly rather than reopening the core merger decision.

### Vowels (Full Common Slavic Inventory, Not Yet Resolved)

At c. 1000, the deeper vowel-system changes that will eventually produce a modern-looking system are **still in progress, not complete** — this snapshot deliberately shows the inherited system intact, consistent with real Common Slavic dialectal differentiation timelines (dialect-specific consonant/lexical choices, like *kaj* and *v*-retention, differentiate earlier than the deep vowel mergers):

| Vowel class | Phonemes | Status at c. 1000 |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | i e a o u | Stable, inherited |
| Jers (reduced) | ъ ь | Still live; Havlík's Law has not yet resolved them — see [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Etymology/Fall of the Jers]], a Phase 2/3 development |
| Yat | ě | Still a distinct long vowel; the Ekavian **e**-reflex above is the eventual Kajkavian-dialect outcome, not yet complete at this snapshot |
| Nasals | ę ǫ | Still live nasal vowels; their denasalization (ę→e, ǫ→u — already individually cited across several [[../Words|Words/]] pronoun pages) is likewise a later development |

**Pitch accent** (rising vs. falling tone, inherited from Balto-Slavic) is present, with Kajkavian's own distinct accentual system (§ above) already differentiating from what will become Neo-Shtokavian pitch accent elsewhere in the dialect continuum.

### Consonants (Common Slavic Inheritance + Kajkavian-Specific Choices)

| Series | Phonemes | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stops | p b, t d, k g | Common Slavic inheritance |
| Fricatives | s z, š ž | š ž from the first palatalization of velars + iotation of s/z (see [[Old Church Slavonic]] § Consonants) |
| Affricates | c (dz marginal), č | c/dz from the second/third palatalizations; č from the first palatalization of k + iotation of k+j |
| Nasals | m n | Common Slavic inheritance |
| Liquids | l r, epenthetic-l | The "epenthetic l" after labials before /j/ (e.g. *zemja → zemlja), per [[Old Church Slavonic]] |
| Glide | v | **Retained as v, not reduced to u** — the specific Kajkavian/Siscia-corridor toponymic evidence already cited above (*vъ* → v, not Shtokavian's u), with regressive devoicing to [f] before voiceless obstruents |

**"Glide" describes this c. 1000 stage only, not modern Sisician.** Common Slavic *v is real, well-documented as a labial glide/approximant here (descending from PIE *w) — but both [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] and [[../Phonology/Orthography|Phonology/Orthography]] independently place modern Sisician's /v/ in the fricative series, not among the glides. That's not a page-to-page contradiction to resolve by picking one — it's this page correctly describing the ancestral stage, with the hardening step itself now stated explicitly on [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes]] rather than left as an unstated gap between here and the modern target. The Sava-corridor devoicing evidence above doesn't disambiguate glide-vs-fricative either way — real Slavic /v/ shows that same obstruent-like devoicing behavior even in dialects where it stays phonetically glide-like, so it was never evidence against hardening.

**No /ć/ or /đ/** in the colloquial vernacular (merged to č/j, per the decision above) — living only in the middle literary-register tier and the OCS liturgical import, per the two distinct overlay registers already established (§ above; three tiers total, including the fully-merged vernacular).

### What This Section Does Not Resolve

The deep vowel-system resolution (jer loss, yat merger, nasal denasalization) is real Phase 2/3 territory — individual outcomes have already been cited piecemeal across [[../Words|Words/]] pages (this is exactly the gap the planned, separate **Sisician Sound Changes** page is meant to consolidate), but this page's own job is the c. 1000 starting snapshot, not the destination.
