# Modern Sisician: Consonant and Vowel Inventory

**Status: tentative target.** This is the *target* modern-Sisician phoneme inventory, sketched ahead of the historical derivation connecting it back to c. 1000 South Slavic. Treat everything on this page as provisional and subject to revision once that derivation is worked out. See [[Design Principles]] for the design goals this was sketched against.

## Vowels

Five vowels: **/a e i o u/**. No length, tone/pitch-accent, or stress system has been specified yet — open question, not yet decided (Serbo-Croatian-style South Slavic languages typically have pitch accent and vowel length, so this is worth a deliberate decision rather than a silent omission).

### No Separate Diphthong Inventory — Vj Is Just V + Consonant /j/

Sisician doesn't need a Romance-style phonemic diphthong inventory (the way Latin/Dalmatian's ie/uo/ai genuinely are single complex nuclei) — /j/ is already an ordinary Sisician consonant, so **any vowel + j sequence is simply that vowel followed by /j/ in coda position**, phonotactically unremarkable in exactly the way any other V+C coda is. This matches how real BCS itself analyzes *kraj*, *moj*, *tvoj*, *trofej*: plain VC, not a special diphthong class.

**Consequence: aj/ej/oj/uj are all equally legal — but ij is a real, motivated exception, not part of the same set.** aj and oj are already attested as genuine codas ([[../Words/тај|тај]], [[../Words/денајр|денајр]], [[../Words/монајта|монајта]] for aj; [[../Words/ној|ној]], [[../Words/тој|тој]], [[../Words/овој|овој]] for oj); ej and uj are equally legal even though no coined word happens to need them yet (cf. real Croatian *trofej*, *obuj* for exactly these two).

**Coda -ij, by contrast, has no genuine example anywhere in the lexicon** — real Slavic syllabification maximizes onsets, so **-ији-** and **-ија**-type sequences (as in [[../Words/који|који]], [[../Words/скинија|скинија]]) syllabify as *ko-ji*, *ski-ni-ja*, with **ј as the onset of the following syllable**, not a coda after *i*. There's a real, principled reason this gap likely isn't accidental: /i/ and /j/ are articulatorily almost the same sound — a high front vowel and its non-syllabic glide counterpart — so a genuine **i+coda-j** sequence is acoustically very close to just a long [iː], with little to distinguish the two as separate segments. This is a real, common cross-linguistic markedness constraint (languages very often ban exactly this vowel-glide pairing while freely allowing the others, for the same reason /u/+/w/ is the other classic gap). **Coda -j specifically after *i* is excluded (or at least strongly dispreferred)**, the same way *u*+w is excluded by the broader no-/w/ rule below — a, o, and e (and less certainly u, since u/j differ enough in place to stay distinct) keep coda -j freely.

**The real constraint is on the *other* kind of glide, and it's absolute: no vowel+/w/ sequence survives as such, because /w/ isn't a Sisician phoneme at all** (§ Consonants above — only /j/ is listed as a glide; this is also the standing South Slavic norm, not a Sisician-specific gap). Whenever Dalmatian source material would hand Sisician a w-final diphthong (au, eu, ou, or a closed-syllable *wa/wo*-type onset-glide), the /w/ **hardens to a full consonant /v/** instead of surviving as an offglide — becoming an onset consonant of the following syllable, not a diphthong nucleus (cf. [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s *mwart→мварт*, [[../Words/читвот|читвот]]'s *cituot→читвот*). **Sisician has zero tolerance for w-glides in any position**, not just a preference against them.

It's charted because в needs charting, not because б's allophone does.
- **[ŋ]** is an allophone of /n/ before velars (place assimilation), not a separate phoneme.
- 24 consonants + 5 vowels = 29 phonemes total (not counting [ŋ]).

## в/ф: Bilabial, Not Labiodental

**/v/ and /f/ are phonemically bilabial fricatives, [β] and [ɸ], not labiodental [v]/[f] — written в/ф exactly as before, no spelling change.**

**в's own etymology gives a real reason to prefer bilabial over labiodental.** [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] establishes that Common Slavic **\*v** was a labial glide/approximant (from PIE **\*w**), hardening to a true fricative by the modern stage. **Bilabial [β] is the smaller, more natural articulatory step from a bilabial glide**: /w/ and [β] share the same place of articulation (both lips), so hardening only has to add frication, not also shift place all the way to lip-against-teeth. This is the exact real-world fork Vulgar Latin's own inherited /w/ took across the Romance family — **Spanish's outcome is bilabial β** (not labiodental v), while French/Italian's is labiodental v — two genuinely different, both-attested real outcomes from the identical starting point. Sisician's own /w/→в hardening lands on the Spanish-type outcome.

**This produces a real, deliberate partial neutralization with the intervocalic-lenition rule below.** Between vowels, б lenites to [β] (per that rule) — and в, being bilabial [β] in *every* position, already sits there. So **intervocalically, б and в become phonetically identical**, while staying fully distinct everywhere else (word-initially/post-nasal, б is the stop [b], в is still the continuant [β] — different manner, same place). This is a real, well-documented *type* of phenomenon — positional neutralization, where two phonemes that contrast in most environments collapse to one surface form in a specific one (the closest textbook example: American English flapping, where /t/ and /d/ become the identical flap [ɾ] intervocalically — *latter*/*ladder* — while staying fully distinct word-initially). **Spanish's own b/v is a closer but broader real-world parallel**: Spanish b and v are the *same phoneme* everywhere (a full historical merger, not a positional coincidence), whereas Sisician's б and в stay two separate phonemes that merely happen to converge in one environment.

**ф's case rests on weaker grounds, flagged rather than smoothed over.** Unlike в, ф has no glide-hardening origin story to lean on — Cyrillic ф is historically the letter reserved for Greek loanwords (ф/Ѳ exist specifically to render Greek φ/θ), and whether the Greek φ Sisician borrowed the sound from was itself bilabial [ɸ] or already labiodental [f] at the point of contact is unresolved. Made bilabial anyway, for **symmetry with в** (the natural voiced/voiceless pair at the same place) rather than independent evidence of its own.

## Prosody

**For nouns, stress is root-anchored, not a mechanical syllable-count rule.** Stress stays on the noun's root/stem as case endings are added, rather than migrating to track "the penultimate syllable" of whatever the current surface form happens to be. This most often *coincides* with penultimate position, since most case endings are a single syllable — but the root is the real anchor, not the penult; penultimate placement is just what root-anchored stress looks like on short words. Confirmed by [[../Words/каза|Words/каза]] (казама keeps root-initial ˈkazama rather than recomputing penultimate ka.ˈza.ma) and [[../Words/дом|Words/дом]] (домовима keeps root stress across four syllables, never migrating into the *-ov-* extension or case endings) — both matching real Common Slavic's "fixed accent paradigm" noun class and real Croatian pronunciation of these exact word-types.

**Scope: nouns only, for now.** Verb stress, and finer-grained rules (e.g. real Slavic's mobile accent-paradigm classes, where *some* words genuinely do shift stress between root and ending rather than staying fixed) are explicitly deferred — root-anchored-by-default is the working rule until more specific patterns are worked out.

## Syllabic Sonorants — Colloquial Only, Not Standard

**Syllabic /r/ (and /l/) is real and alive in colloquial/fast speech** — the ordinary vowel-syncope outcome real South Slavic shows in exactly these words (*vrt* "garden," *smrt* "death," *prst* "finger" are real, unremarkable BCS words with syllabic /r/, no epenthetic vowel at all). **But standard Sisician spelling and careful/prestige speech avoid it**, per [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] § Orthographic History's "eye-spelling" tradition: an epenthetic **a** is inserted instead — *vrt* → **vart**, *smrt* → **smart** — a real, live register alternation, not just a spelling quirk divorced from pronunciation. Casual speech still says the bare syllabic consonant; careful speech (and everything written) uses the epenthetic-vowel form. This is this vault's own invented sociolinguistic development, not a real attested BCS standardization fact — real BCS keeps syllabic /r/ in its own standard without any such correction; Sisician's version is a class/prestige-driven divergence specific to this project, motivated by [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|the prestige-register principle]] (syllabic consonants read as "vulgar Slavic," avoided by the Dalmatian-philic prestige norm) rather than cited from anywhere real.

- **Syllabic /r/, /l/ — colloquial/fast-speech only.** Standard register replaces with epenthetic **a** + the consonant (vrt→vart, smrt→smart pattern). See [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] and [[../Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman|Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman]] for the social mechanism.
- **Very rarely syllabic**, and specifically attested in slang register: /ʎ/, /n/, /m/, /ɲ/ — unaffected by the above; these were never a standard-register form to begin with.

## x→ç

**Standard Modern Sisician's reflex of the old Common Slavic velar fricative is palatal [ç], full stop, in every register — no register spectrum, the same way б/д/г's own lenition is unconditional rather than register-limited.** There is no "neutral [x]" option inside the standard variety at all.

**[x] and, further exaggerated, [χ] live outside the standard entirely, in two genuinely different places:**

- **A real non-standard/rural dialect** that never went through the elite-driven fronting — the same kind of standard-vs-periphery split real language communities actually show (a prestige innovation completing in the capital/court variety while outlying speech keeps the older form).
- **Deliberate mockery-performance** (see [[../Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman|Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman]]): a standard speaker doing "the Slav voice" isn't reaching for an internal option their own speech still has — they're imitating an **outside** dialect.

**Modeled on real German dialectal ich-Laut/ach-Laut allophony** (the same phoneme surfacing as palatal [ç] near front vowels and velar [x] elsewhere), repurposed as social rather than purely phonological variation. The phonological conditioning that makes [ç] a natural outcome near front vowels/consonants is real; Sisician generalizes it to every position rather than leaving it conditioned.

**This is a genuine, synchronic phonemic difference from the actual Slavic neighbors, not just an available register trick.** /x/ would otherwise be "consistent with the Slavic norm" — real Vulgar Latin/Dalmatian has no velar or uvular fricative at all (see [[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Sources/Vulgar Latin]], [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]), the one common, everyday sound the Dalmatian prestige layer never had anything to say about. Real Croatian/Serbian/Bosnian still have a velar /x/ today. **Standard Sisician no longer does.**

**Syllabic /r/,/l/ (§ Syllabic Sonorants above) is still colloquial-vs-careful register variation, not yet completed the same categorical way** — an open, separate piece of the same broader distancing goal.

## Intervocalic Lenition: /b, d, g/ → [β, ð, ɣ]

**Between two vowels, with nothing else intervening, /b, d, g/ lenite to the corresponding voiced fricatives [β, ð, ɣ]** — unconditionally, in every register, native vocabulary and loans alike. **Modeled directly on real Spanish** (and the wider Ibero-Romance pattern), where this exact rule is bedrock, everyday phonology: Spanish *b/d/g* are pronounced as stops only word-initially or after a nasal/lateral, and as fricatives everywhere else, with **no spelling distinction at all** between the two realizations — the same "one letter, two predictable allophones" relationship this rule gives Sisician. Blocked, per the real Spanish model, immediately after any consonant (including a nasal or liquid) — only a genuine V_V environment triggers it. Applies across word boundaries in connected speech as well as word-internally, the same way real Spanish does (*la boca* → [la ˈβoka]).

**Not presented as inherited from Dalmatian** — Dalmatian is Italo-Dalmatian, closer kin to Italian, and Italian itself doesn't lenite intervocalic stops this way (real Italian *cade* stays [ˈkade], never [ˈkaðe]). Instead: intervocalic voiced-stop lenition is one of the most common, phonetically natural weakening processes in the world, arising convergently and independently across many unrelated language families (Spanish, Danish, and others with no contact relationship to each other) — Sisician's own vernacular plausibly underwent it on entirely internal, articulatory grounds, and its convergence with Spanish's own outcome is a coincidence of *type*, not of *contact*. It does, however, reinforce the same broader prestige pressure as ç-softening and syllabic-/r/ loss — smoother, more vowel-continuous articulation reading as more "Romance," full stops reading as more "harsh Slavic."

**Already-attested in real built words, spelling unaffected in every case** ([[../Words/када|када]], [[../Words/кога|кога]], [[../Words/тебе|тебе]], [[../Words/теби|теби]], [[../Words/тога|тога]], [[../Words/чега|чега]], [[../Words/њега|њега]], [[../Words/овога|овога]], [[../Words/седам|седам]], [[../Words/тобом|тобом]], [[../Words/један|један]], and [[../Words/одовде|одовде]] all have a real intervocalic б/д/г): **када** is /ˈkaða/; **кога** is /ˈkoɣa/; **тебе** is /ˈteβe/. **Одовде** is the one two-д case worth naming explicitly: its *first* д (о-д-о-) is intervocalic and lenites (/ˈoðovde/), but its *second* д (in -вде) is preceded by a consonant (в) and stays a plain stop — only one of the word's two д's changes.

## Open Design Notes

- This inventory has no phonemic palatalized-stop series (no distinct /kʲ/, /gʲ/, /tʲ/, /dʲ/), and it shouldn't — a pervasive plain/palatalized pairing across the whole consonant inventory is an East Slavic (Russian-style) feature, not a South Slavic one (see [[Design Principles]] § Palatalization). Sisician's /ɲ ʎ/ plus the postalveolar affricates/fricatives /tʃ dʒ ʃ ʒ/ are the standard South Slavic pattern instead: a fixed, closed set of phonemes descending from Common Slavic's historical palatalizations, not a live hard/soft alternation.
- No /h/, and no standard velar fricative either; the old /x/'s modern standard reflex is palatal /ç/, a real point of divergence from the Slavic norm (real BCS keeps velar /x/) rather than "consistent with" it.
- Whether this inventory needs vowel length, pitch accent, or a stress system is still undecided.
- **Held loosely.** This is a target sketch, not a commitment — phonemes here can be dropped or new ones added if no legitimate historical/phonological process turns out to produce them. The affricate and fricative series (/ts dz/, /tʃ dʒ/, /ʃ ʒ/, /ç/) will eventually want a historical account — which come from inherited Slavic palatalization, which from Dalmatian loans reshaped by Slavic phonotactics, which (if any) from later Hungarian/German/Greek contact — but the inventory itself is free to change shape in the meantime.
