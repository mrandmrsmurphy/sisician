# Phonology: Design Principles

This page records the design targets and constraints agreed on for Sisician's phonology. A concrete (tentative) target inventory now exists at [[Modern Inventory]] — see [[Conlang Workflow]] for why it was sketched ahead of the Phase 1 pre-founding base and the Phase 3 sound-change chain that should eventually derive it. See [[Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Adapt, Don't Import

The underlying phonology should remain South Slavic. Potential defining characteristics: palatalization; hard/soft consonant distinctions; Slavic consonant clusters; inherited Slavic vowel developments; Romance roots reshaped by Slavic phonotactics; possible retention or reinterpretation of selected Romance sounds.

The language should not simply preserve French/Italian phonology. A Romance word entering Sisician around 1100 should undergo: **borrowing → phonological adaptation → sound changes → analogy → lexicalization.** By 1600, the word should often be nearly unrecognizable as Romance.

**Do not import modern Italian or French forms.** Use historically plausible medieval Dalmatian forms and then subject them to Sisician sound change (see [[Etymology/Methodology]] for the per-word pathway this implies).

## Palatalization (not a Russian-style hard/soft system)

A systematic, pervasive phonemic pairing of every consonant into a plain/palatalized ("hard"/"soft") twin is an **East Slavic** feature (Russian above all, and to a lesser extent Polish) — it is not how South Slavic phonology works, and Serbo-Croatian descriptions correctly don't foreground it.

What South Slavic languages actually have is the **fossilized output of Common Slavic's historical palatalizations** (the velar palatalizations, and iotation of dentals/labials/velars): a fixed, closed set of palatal/postalveolar phonemes — /ʎ/, /ɲ/, /tʃ dʒ/, /ʃ ʒ/, /j/ — that function as independent phonemes in their own right, not as one half of a live plain/palatalized alternation running across the whole consonant inventory. [[Modern Inventory]] adopts exactly this South Slavic pattern (via /ɲ ʎ/ and the postalveolar affricates/fricatives) rather than a Russian-style parallel palatalized-stop series (the originally speculated /k/:/kʲ/, /g/:/gʲ/, /n/:/nʲ/, /l/:/lʲ/, /t/:/tʲ/, /d/:/dʲ/ pairing has been dropped, not merely superseded).

The orthography can eventually develop explicit conventions for these contrasts (see [[Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] § Orthographic History). The important thing is that Romance roots get integrated into them — a borrowed Romance word does not retain an exotic foreign phonology forever. It becomes Sisician.
