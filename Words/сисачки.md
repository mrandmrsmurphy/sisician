# сисачки (sisački) — the endonym

**Status: exploratory/tentative — the vault's first derived proper-name-adjacent word, and its single most identity-critical one.** Answers [[../Culture/Founding Myth and Naming|Culture/Founding Myth and Naming]] § Naming's long-open "native endonym" question, now that [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]]'s consolidated sound-change chain exists to derive it from. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Sisician" — the language's own name for itself, used the way real Slavic language names work: a substantivized long-form adjective (compare real Croatian *hrvatski*, elliptical for *hrvatski jezik* "Croatian language," used as a noun on its own). **сисачки** on its own means "the Sisician [language]"; as an ordinary adjective it also covers "of/pertaining to Сисак" generally (the town, its people, its charter tradition, etc.), the same dual duty real language-name adjectives always carry.

## Declension

Long/definite form only, per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s already-established -и masculine nominative singular ending (the same ending [[../Words/нув|нув]]'s own long form нуви shows):

| | Masculine (sg.) |
|---|---|
| Nominative | сисачки |

No indefinite/short form is needed or proposed — a language name is inherently definite, the same way a proper name doesn't take an indefinite article. Full oblique paradigm not built here (not needed to answer the naming question); would follow [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s established hard-stem pattern for -и nominative adjectives — though note ч is one of the soft-stem trigger consonants (ц дз ч џ ш ж љ њ ј), so any future oblique forms would take that page's soft-stem endings (-его/-ему), not нув's hard-stem ones.

## Etymology

**A real, traceable toponymic chain, not a reconstruction from scratch.** Real Sisak is one of the few European towns whose name can be followed continuously: pre-Roman **Segestica** (Celtic/Illyrian) → Roman **Siscia** → early medieval **Siscium** → modern Croatian **Sisak**. **Siscium** is the historically correct citation form for this founding story's own period (c. 1000 AD) — the exact form the local Slavic-speaking population (already established as fully Slavic-speaking by 1000 AD, per [[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Geography and Economy]]) would have inherited the name in, well before [[../Culture/Founding Myth and Naming|Vitalis]] ever arrives.

**Step 1 — the base toponym, Сисак.** The Latin/Vulgar Latin root **Sisk-** (stripped of the Latin neuter *-ium* ending on borrowing) lands on a word-final consonant cluster Common Slavic phonotactics doesn't tolerate bare. The same repair [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] already provides for native words in exactly this position — a jer that vocalizes to **a** in the strong position — applies here too, whether analyzed as an inserted epenthetic jer or (equally plausibly) the productive Common Slavic place-name suffix *-ъkъ landing on the bare root: either route gives ***Sisъkъ**, and the same weak/strong yer alternation already used throughout this vault (word-final grammatical yer weak and drops; the preceding root-internal yer strong and vocalizes) gives **Сисак** — matching real modern Croatian *Sisak* exactly, since nothing about this specific word triggers any of Sisician's own divergence points (no yat, no nasal vowel, no ć/đ-vs-č/dž fork). A boringly regular derivation, the same honest category as [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s own — not a coincidence to explain away.

**Step 2 — the endonym itself, сисачки.** The real, extremely productive Common Slavic place-name/language-adjective suffix **\*-ьskъ** (source of virtually every real Slavic country/language name: *polьskъ* → Polish *polski*, *rusьskъ* → Russian *русский*, *srъbьskъ* → Serbian *српски*) attaches directly to the bare consonantal root **Сиск-**, *before* the epenthetic vowel of step 1 ever appears — matching how the real adjective actually behaves, not the citation-form noun. The suffix's own front yer triggers **first palatalization** on the root-final velar (к→ч, the same *k→č* first-palatalization inheritance already catalogued on [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]]'s own consonant table), giving **Сисч-**. The suffix's own yer then drops as weak (per ordinary [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] — real attested modern Croatian confirms this directly: **сисачки**, not a hypothetical *сисачаки, is the actual adjective, e.g. the real administrative unit *Sisačko-moslavačka županija* "Sisak-Moslavina County," confirmed via search this session), leaving **Сисачк-**. [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s already-established long-form masculine nominative **-и** (the same ending [[../Words/нув|нув]] already uses) then gives the citation form: **сисачки**.

**Every mechanism here was already decided before this word existed** — Fall of the Jers, first palatalization (cited on [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] since before any word had used it), and the long-form adjective -и ending. Nothing new was invented to make this word work; it's the first time these particular already-established pieces have been assembled for a proper-name-adjacent derivation rather than an ordinary content word.

## Register

**Deliberately native-Slavic, not Dalmatian or Greek — a real cross-linguistic pattern, not an arbitrary pick.** Despite Sisician's whole hybrid identity and the prestige [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|Dalmatian layer]] running through the rest of the lexicon, a language's *own name for itself* overwhelmingly stays in native, inherited vocabulary cross-linguistically — speakers don't typically borrow a prestige-register word for their own self-designation, even in languages massively influenced by a prestige contact language otherwise. сисачки sitting in [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]]'s Layer I ("Slavic Earth") despite the Dalmatian City layer being so central to the culture's own self-image is exactly the expected outcome, not a tension to resolve.
