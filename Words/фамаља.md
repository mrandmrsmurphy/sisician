# фамаља (famalja)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** A deliberate test case for [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s new (2026-09-24) ɲ/ʎ/š/ž loanword-adaptation routes, and a full lexical replacement modeled directly on real English — see Etymology below. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Family." **The sole modern word** — there is no living native register doublet. The two native candidates that would ordinarily have filled this slot (see Etymology) were never built as independent `Words/` pages, the same "superseded, not a separate coexisting word" treatment already given to дешет's десет and абкле's одкле.

## Declension (e-declension, feminine, soft stem)

Following [[../Words/каза|каза]]'s own e-declension pattern exactly (real Croatian *žena/žene/ženi/ženu/ženom*) — the soft-stem trigger consonant љ (per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s established hard/soft trigger set ц дз ч џ ш ж љ њ ј) doesn't actually change any ending here, since e-declension's own endings don't show a hard/soft split the way a-declension's instrumental singular (-ом vs. -ем) does — фамаља is simply the first soft-stem *e*-declension word built, confirming that non-split by example rather than leaving it an assumption.

|---|---|---|
| Nominative | фамаља /faˈmaʎa/ | фамаље /faˈmaʎe/ |
| Genitive | фамаље /faˈmaʎe/ | фамаља /faˈmaʎa/ |
| Dative | фамаљи /faˈmaʎi/ | фамаљама /faˈmaʎama/ |
| Accusative | фамаљу /faˈmaʎu/ | фамаље /faˈmaʎe/ |
| Instrumental | фамаљом /faˈmaʎom/ | фамаљама /faˈmaʎama/ |

Same inherited syncretisms as каза, for the same reason (shared declension class): nom.sg. **фамаља** = gen.pl. **фамаља**; gen.sg. **фамаље** = nom.pl./acc.pl. **фамаље**; dat.pl. = instr.pl. **фамаљама**.

**Stress: root-anchored**, on **-ма-** throughout (фаˈма.ља, gen. фаˈма.ље, dat.pl. фаˈма.ља.ма, etc.) — never migrating onto a case ending. This happens to coincide with plain penultimate placement in every form above (all either two or three syllables), so it isn't yet a case that distinguishes root-anchoring from mechanical penult-counting the way казама was — see Etymology for why "-ма-" is the anchor point in the first place.

## Etymology

**A full lexical replacement, modeled directly on real English — the user's own proposed test case for [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s newly-decided (2026-09-24) sound-change additions.**

### The Real English Parallel

Old English had two native words in this semantic space: **hīred** ("household, family, retinue," from Proto-West Germanic *hīwarād — survives today only vestigially, in modern *herd*) and **hād** (originally "person, condition, rank, order," source of the modern *-hood* suffix — *childhood*, *manhood*). Both were displaced by **family**, from Latin **familia** via Old French, entering Middle English and fully generalizing by the early modern period. Latin *familia* itself originally meant "a household" in the broad sense — everyone under one roof, including servants and slaves (from *famulus* "servant") — not the narrower "nuclear family" sense it carries today; the semantic narrowing happened gradually, in English, after borrowing.

### The Sisician Parallel — Two Native Candidates, Both Passed Over

Sisician had (or could have had) two native candidates for "family," neither of which was ever built as an independent `Words/` page:

- **породица**, a transparent native compound: **по-** + **\*rodъ** ("family, birth, clan, origin" — the same root behind real *rod*, *rodina*, *narod*) + the feminine/collective suffix **-ица**. All three morphemes are ordinary Common Slavic inheritance, so Sisician could perfectly well have coined this compound on its own, independent of whether the specific real BCS word *porodica* is itself an old formation or a comparatively recent standardization-era coinage — the ingredients were always available.
- **\*obitělь**, a real Proto-Slavic reconstruction (real descendants: Croatian *obitelj*, Russian *обитель*) meaning "abode, dwelling, habitation; inn, lodging, monastery" — from *obvitati* ("to dwell, abide") + the agent/instrument suffix *-ělь*. **A genuinely striking parallel to hīred worth stating explicitly**: real Croatian's own modern *obitelj* underwent exactly the same "dwelling/household → family" semantic narrowing that hīred shows natively in English, and that породица also implies (both routes lead to the same idea: the people who share your roof). Sisician could have taken either native path; instead, per the English model, a Dalmatian loanword takes the slot both would have filled.

**Both are passed over. Dalmatian's famalja wins the slot outright, the same way "family" won in English** — this is the deliberate point of the exercise, not an oversight. This is also a **narrow, precisely-targeted exception** to [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s Layer I ("Slavic Earth") listing "family" among the expected native-vocabulary domains — worth flagging directly rather than silently contradicting it. The real English parallel actually predicts exactly this narrowness: individual kinship terms (*mother*, *father*, *brother*, *sister*) stayed native Germanic in English even as the higher-order collective term went Latinate. Sisician's own individual kinship vocabulary (mother, father, brother, sister, etc. — none built yet) should be expected to stay native Slavic; фамаља is the one collective term that doesn't, by the same logic, not a general erosion of Layer I.

### The Sound Derivation

Latin **FAMILIA** [faˈmilia], stress on the antepenult per Classical Latin's own rule (penult *-li-* is light: short vowel, open syllable) — matching real Italian *famiglia*'s identical stress placement (fa-MI-glia) as an independent cross-check. Vulgar Latin's unstressed intervocalic *-i-* before a following vowel becomes consonantal (yod): *famiglia*-type /fa.mi.lja/, three syllables, stress unchanged on "mi."

Via [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]: no diphthongization trigger (the stressed vowel is *i*, not ē/ō); no intervocalic stop to preserve or lenite; no velar before a front vowel; no geminate to degeminate — the one relevant rule is the already-decided **-LI-+vowel → ʎ** route (§ End-Stage Phonology, "Dalmatian's real \[ɲ/ʎ\] ... from Latin *-gn-*, *-ll-*/*-li-* clusters"), giving Dalmatian **familja** [faˈmiʎa].

**The specific -i-→-a- shift (familja → famalja) is this vault's own proposed extension, not a citation**, but it isn't arbitrary: it's presented as happening specifically because this is a **late loan**, entering during Dalmatian's own dying, heavy-convergence phase (Early Modern Sisician, c. 1350–1650 — see [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]] § End-Stage Phonology), the same period already established for [[../Words/абкле|абкле]]'s own full generalization and matching the real Middle English timeline "family" itself displaced hīred/hād on. Per that section's own general finding — a dying language's last fluent-to-semi-fluent speakers show substantial phonological transfer from their dominant L1 — an unstressed medial *i* flanked on both sides by *a* (fa-M**I**-lja) is exactly the kind of vowel a Slavic-dominant semi-speaker population would be liable to level toward its neighbors, especially once it's carrying the "odd," non-native consonant+glide sequence *-lja* along with it. **familja → famalja/фамаља.** Flagged honestly as an extension applying an already-established general mechanism to one new word, not a fresh citation.

**Stress stays on "-ма-"** — the same syllable Latin's own "-mi-" occupied — as an ordinary inherited-stress fossil, independent of the separate vowel-quality shift; it also happens to coincide with Sisician's own general "penultimate, with many exceptions" default for a word this length, so the two accounts don't compete for this particular word.

## Register

Historically Dalmatian-formal (per frontmatter) — but, like [[../Words/абкле|абкле]], doesn't stay there. By the modern period it carries no more register weight than [[../Words/дешет|дешет]] or [[../Words/ној|ној]]: it's simply the ordinary, unmarked word for "family," the same way *family* itself carries no Latinate erudition in modern English.
