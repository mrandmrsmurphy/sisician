# тебе (tebe)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Twelfth individual pronoun entry, and the third oblique-case page — the exact 2sg parallel to [[мене]], down to the specific historical complication. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Two Cases, the Same Syncretism мене Already Has

тебе covers **genitive and accusative at once**, exactly mirroring [[мене]]'s own situation rather than being a separate design decision made twice: [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table already gives тебе for both cells, with short form [[те]]. Fetched the Proto-Slavic *ty declension table specifically to check whether this parallel holds at the *root* level too, not just the surface pattern — it does, exactly: Proto-Slavic had genitive **\*tȅbe** and a *third*, distinct accusative-only enclitic, **\*tę̑**, the precise structural twin of **\*mȅne**/**\*mę̃** on [[мене]]'s page. Sisician doesn't carry *\*tę̑* forward as an independent full form here either — accusative simply reuses genitive тебе/те, the same design point already made for 1st singular, now confirmed to extend to 2nd singular rather than being a one-off.

## Etymology

**Same unexplained-oblique-stem situation as [[мене]] — checked, not assumed.** The fetched *ty* reconstruction's etymology section traces only the nominative (*\*tūˀ* ← PIE *\*túh₂*); it does not derive the oblique **теб-/тоб-** stem at all, exactly matching what [[јаз]]'s page already found for *az-/men-*. So, as with [[мене]], the account below is general Indo-European background, not sourced to this specific citation.

**The oblique \*teb- stem** is the expected 2nd-singular counterpart of 1st-singular \*men-, part of the same ancient PIE oblique-pronoun formation pattern (suffixed to a base rather than sharing the nominative's root) — real cross-linguistic parallels include Latin *tū*/*tē* alongside *ego*/*mē*, the same nominative-vs-oblique-suffix asymmetry running through both persons in parallel. Not traced to a specific PIE morpheme here, the same open gap [[мене]]'s page left rather than guessed shut.

**The e~o ablaut already flagged on [[ти]]'s own page (тебе/теби vs. тобом) is the same alternation visible in the raw reconstruction**: genitive/dative *\*tȅbe/\*tebě̀* both keep *e*, while instrumental *\*tobòjǫ* shifts to *o* — inherited texture, not something invented when [[ти]]'s page first noted it.

**A form not carried forward here either: \*tę̑.** Exactly paralleling [[мене]]'s *\*mę̃*, Proto-Slavic's distinct accusative enclitic *\*tę̑* is the direct ancestor of Old Church Slavonic **тѧ** and — via the same regular ę-denasalization already discussed on [[ме]]'s page — of modern [[те]]. That denasalization fact is repeated from [[ме]]'s page, not re-verified independently here, since it's the identical sound change applying to the identical vowel in the identical morphological slot.

**Real Slavic comparanda (genitive/accusative full form):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | тебе (tebe, gen.); тѧ (tę, short acc.) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | tèbe (gen./acc. full form) |
| South Slavic | Slovene | têbe |
| East Slavic | Russian | тебя (tebja) |
| West Slavic | Polish | ciebie; Czech tebe |

**No branch tension found**, the same clean result as [[ти]]'s own nominative — every South Slavic language keeps a *tebe*-reflex for the full genitive/accusative form.

## Register

Same closed-class status as [[ти]]. Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], full тебе (like [[мене]]) is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; the clitic [[те]] takes the ordinary second-position slot otherwise. No additional T–V complication here beyond what [[ти]]'s own page already covers — the formal counterpart routes through [[ви]]'s own paradigm entirely, not through a formal variant of тебе.
