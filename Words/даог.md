# даог (daog)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Long," filling [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 28, from the Proto-Slavic adjective \*dьlgъ at the user's direction. It is the first word to test L-vocalization in a **pre-consonantal** position. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Long," of space and of time: **даог пут** "a long road," **даога нога** "a long leg." One word for both senses, as in the Slavic neighbors.

**Adverbial use: the masculine singular, unchanged**, per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § Adjective to Adverb: **даог** is also "for a long time."

## Forms

Gender and number agreement only, like every adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | даог | даога |
| Plural | даоги | даоге |

No case and no definiteness. A noun that is old-neuter-plural takes the feminine plural, by the neuter merger. Stress is root-anchored on the first syllable, **да-**, throughout.

**The г.** In даог it is word-final and stays a stop, /ˈdaog/. In даога, даоги, даоге it stands between vowels, so it lenites to [ɣ] ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 12): /ˈdaoɣa/, /ˈdaoɣi/, /ˈdaoɣe/. The о is stable across the paradigm; unlike [[../Words/светао|светао]] / светла, there is no form in which the л survives.

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*dь̀lgъ** "long," from Proto-Balto-Slavic \*dílˀgas and PIE \*dl̥h₁gʰós ([Wiktionary \*dьlgъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/dьlgъ)). Descendants listed there: OCS дльгъ / длъгъ, Serbo-Croatian дуг, Slovene dolg, Macedonian долг, Bulgarian дълъг / дълг.

**Sisician derivation, two steps, both already established:**

1. **Fall of the Jers** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): two jers. Counting from the right, the final ъ is weak and drops; the root ь is strong and vocalizes to **a**. \*dьlgъ → *далг*.
2. **L-vocalization**, coda /l/ → /o/ ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 10): the л closes the syllable before г, so *далг* → **даог**.

**The other forms.** In \*dьlga, \*dьlgi, \*dьlge only the root ь is a jer, so it is the rightmost jer and weak, and it drops, leaving a syllabic л: \*dlga. Syllabic /l/ takes the epenthetic a ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 13), giving *далга*, and the л is again a coda (дал.га), so § 10 applies and gives **даога**. Lenition then makes the г [ɣ]. There is no mobile-a alternation, because the vowel is the same in every form.

**Where it lands.** Serbian дуг vocalizes the syllabic л to у; Slovene dolg puts an o before an intact л; Sisician gets a vowel (a, as in the Kajkavian-leaning jer outcome) and then vocalizes the л, ending in the hiatus **ао** that [[../Words/мао|мао]] also has. It is unlike both neighbors.

**First pre-consonantal test of § 10.** Every other word that shows the change has a word-final л (радио, светао, мао). § 10 states the rule for both positions, and the user chose to keep it that way here, so any future word with a л before a consonant vocalizes too. This is more aggressive than standard Serbo-Croatian (which keeps pre-consonantal л, as in *bolnica*) and closer to Kajkavian and Čakavian *vouk* / *dougo* types, which I recall but have not checked.

## Register

## Open Points

- **The palatalized stem** \*dьlž- (Wiktionary lists \*dьlžь, \*dьlžina "length," \*dьlžiti, \*dьlžati) isn't used; no leveling as with [[../Words/друж|друж]].
- **The derived noun** \*dьlgota "length" isn't coined.
- **The comparative** isn't built.
- **Dalmatian *luang*** (the Swadesh row) isn't borrowed.
- **Other л-before-consonant words** are now predicted to vocalize; none exist yet.
