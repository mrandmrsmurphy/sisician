# њега (njega)

See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — A Different Kind of Genitive/Accusative Syncretism Than мене/тебе

њега covers genitive **and** accusative, like [[мене]] and [[тебе]] before it — but fetching the *jь* declension table for this page specifically showed the underlying history is **not** the same shape as those two. [[мене]]/[[тебе]] each had a genuinely separate, dedicated accusative-only enclitic in Proto-Slavic (*\*mę̃*/*\*tę̑*), later folded into the genitive form. **The *jь* paradigm has the opposite starting arrangement**: its accusative was originally just the bare nominative-look-alike *\*jь* itself, while genitive alone was the separately-suffixed *\*jego*. So њега's genitive/accusative syncretism isn't "the dedicated accusative form got absorbed into genitive" (мене/тебе's story) — it's **genitive absorbing accusative's job outright**, a different mechanism reaching a similar-looking surface result.

**This is a real, well-documented Slavic phenomenon in its own right, not invented to patch the gap:** Slavic generally marks masculine animate accusative with the *genitive* form rather than a distinct accusative shape — the "genitive-accusative syncretism for animates" found throughout the family (e.g. real Serbo-Croatian *vidim čovjeka*, "I see the man," genitive-shaped *čovjeka* used as accusative, vs. inanimate *vidim grad*, "I see the city," using the plain accusative/nominative-shaped form). A personal pronoun referring to a person is about as animate a referent as exists, so genitive-for-accusative is exactly what this general rule predicts here — njega's syncretism is the pronoun-paradigm instance of the same rule that already governs [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s own animate/inanimate accusative split.

## Etymology

**The prothetic-n mechanism is [[он]]'s own, not re-derived here** — see that page for the full citation (a preposition ending in *-n* resegmented onto the pronoun, e.g. "*vъ njego*" from "*vъn jego*," then generalized to every oblique context). This page adds the case-internal detail [[он]]'s didn't need to cover.

**Fetched the *jь* declension table specifically for this build**, confirming: masculine singular **nominative \*jь**, **accusative \*jь** (identical to nominative — no separate accusative shape at all in the base paradigm), **genitive \*jego**. That genitive *\*jego*, with prothetic n- attached, is the real, direct source of both њега's cells — genitive by inheritance, accusative by the animacy-syncretism rule above, not by *jь*'s own (identical-to-nominative) accusative surviving into Sisician at all. Put plainly: Sisician's accusative њега descends from the *genitive* branch of the tree, not the accusative branch — a real discontinuity worth stating explicitly rather than letting the two-birds-one-form surface pattern imply a false continuity.

**The short/clitic form [[га]]** parallels [[ме]]/[[те]]'s status as true clitics, but its own derivation is honestly less certain than theirs: repeated attempts to find a citable source for *how* Proto-Slavic/Old Church Slavonic's short 3rd-person forms (genitive short *\*go*, and a separately-attested accusative short *\*jь*/OCS *и*, і) collapsed into a single **га** didn't turn up a clean, quotable passage — unlike [[ме]]/[[те]]'s denasalization, which rests on solid general phonological grounds even without a fresh citation. **Flagging this explicitly as the least-well-grounded etymological claim in the pronoun set so far**, not glossing over it: real Serbo-Croatian's own Wiktionary entry for *ga* gives no etymology section at all, only cross-references to the full form *njega*. The safest honest statement is that **га** is inherited, real, and attested exactly this way in modern South Slavic — its precise derivational path from Proto-Slavic's separate short forms is not established here.

**No branch tension found** — the prothetic-n distribution and the genitive-for-accusative pattern are both areal-Slavic-wide, not a South-Slavic-specific innovation, so this matches [[он]]'s own "no jaz-style tension" finding.
