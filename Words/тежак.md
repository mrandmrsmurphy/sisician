# тежак (težak)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Heavy," filling [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 31, from the Proto-Slavic adjective \*tęžьkъ at the user's direction. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Heavy, of great weight": a heavy load, a heavy stone. Not claimed: the figurative "difficult" (*a heavy task*) that real Slavic *težak* also has, though nothing here rules it out.

**Adverbial use: the masculine singular, unchanged**, per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § Adjective to Adverb: **тежак** is also "heavily."

## Forms

Gender and number agreement only, like every adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System). **The masculine singular has a mobile а that the other three forms lack**, the same pattern as [[../Words/светао|светао]] / светла and сан / сна ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] § Mobile *a*):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | тежак | тежка |
| Plural | тежки | тежке |

No case and no definiteness. A noun that is old-neuter-plural takes the feminine plural, by the neuter merger. Stress is root-anchored on the first syllable, **те-**, throughout (ˈtežak, ˈtežka, ˈtežki, ˈtežke).

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*tęžьkъ** "heavy (great weight)," from earlier \*tę̑gъkъ, from \*tę̑gъ plus the suffix \*-ьkъ, with the medial *ž* taken from the comparative \*tęžьjь; ultimately Proto-Indo-European \*tengʰ-, with cognates in Lithuanian *tingùs* "lazy" and Proto-Germanic \*þunguz (Old Norse *þungr* "heavy") ([Wiktionary \*tęžьkъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/tęžьkъ)). Descendants listed there: OCS тѧжькъ, Serbo-Croatian тежак, Slovene težak / težek, Macedonian тежок, Bulgarian тежък.

**Sisician derivation, two steps, both already established:**

1. **Denasalization ę → e** ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 2): \*tęžьkъ → *težьkъ*.
2. **Fall of the Jers** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): two jers. Counting from the right, the final ъ is weak and drops; the ь is strong and vocalizes to **a**. → **тежак**.

**The other forms.** In \*tęžьka, \*tęžьki, \*tęžьke the only jer is the ь, so it is rightmost and weak, and it drops: **тежка, тежки, тежке**. This is the ordinary mobile-a alternation.

**The ж-к cluster follows the existing regressive devoicing.** The vault already treats regressive devoicing as a live pronunciation rule ([[../Words/абкле|абкле]] [apˈkle], [[../Words/одтамо|одтамо]] [oˈtamo]; see also [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]]'s Sava-corridor note), so the voiced ж devoices before the voiceless к: **тежка** is written with its stem visible, as абкле is, and pronounced **[ˈteʃka]** (phonemically /ˈtežka/), the same result as real Serbo-Croatian тешка. Spelling stays morphological, **тежка**, as with абкле; whether to spell it тешка the way испод and испред are spelled is open. A progressive-voicing alternative (†тежга) was considered and dropped as too unlike the region.

No other change applies: the ж is already an inventory phoneme, the к is not a lenition target (§ 12 is б, д, г only), and there is no л. The masculine singular is identical to Serbo-Croatian тежак.

## Register

Native, unmarked, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer I.

## Open Points

- **Spelling of the cluster:** тежка (morphological, as written) or тешка (as испод/испред are spelled); the pronunciation is [ˈteʃka] either way.
- **The comparative** is a real stem alternation (\*tęžьjь, the source of the medial ж) and isn't built.
- **The figurative sense** "difficult" isn't claimed or ruled out.
- **Dalmatian *pesunt*** (the Swadesh row; < Vulgar Latin \*pesante) isn't borrowed.
- **The opposite** (a word for "light," not on the Swadesh list) isn't coined.
