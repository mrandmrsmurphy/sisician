# јој (joj)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Twenty-ninth individual pronoun entry — the short/clitic counterpart to [[њој]], inheriting that page's unresolved vowel puzzle rather than adding a new one.

## Meaning

The unstressed second-position clitic covering dative for feminine "to her," per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table. Occupies the dedicated dative-clitic slot, the same one [[му]] occupies for masculine — distinct from the object-clitic slot [[је]]/[[ју]] use.

## Etymology — The Same Open Vowel Question, Minus the Prefix

**The clitics-never-take-njь- generalization, already established on [[их]]'s and [[њих]]'s pages and confirmed again for [[му]]/[[њему]], predicts јој's shape exactly**: strip the prothetic n- from њој and what's left is јој. That part isn't in question.

**What's still genuinely open is the same thing [[њој]]'s own page already flagged rather than resolved: why the vowel is *o* at all.** [[њој]]'s page fetched *jь*'s feminine dative (**\*jeji**) and cross-checked it against real attested Old Church Slavonic (**ѥи**, *jei*) — both show *e*, not *o*. Modern Serbo-Croatian-style јој/њој has *o* instead, and no source consulted for either page explains that shift. This page doesn't re-run those fetches; it simply inherits the same open question, since a clitic can't have a cleaner vowel history than the full form it's stripped from.

**The one thing this page can say with more confidence than [[њој]]'s own etymology:** whatever caused the *e→o* shift, it happened to *both* the full form and the clitic identically — real Serbo-Croatian *njoj* and *joj* aren't split on this vowel the way, say, masculine accusative's full and short forms sometimes diverge in shape ([[њега]] vs. [[га]]). This suggests the *o* is a property of the whole paradigm cell (dative feminine singular) rather than something that entered only through the stressed form and could have skipped the clitic — consistent with, though not proof of, [[њој]]'s own hypothesis that dative and instrumental leveled together as a pair.

**Real Slavic comparanda (dative clitic):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | no distinct short dative form found in the sources checked (full ѥи only) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | joj |
| South Slavic | Slovene | ji (note: not *jej* — a further real complication, see below) |
| East Slavic | Russian | ей (jej) doubles as both full and short in casual use |

**A genuinely interesting further wrinkle, checked directly rather than assumed:** fetched Slovene's own declension table for this pronoun, which confirms its dative clitic is **ji** — genuinely distinct from its own stressed forms (*njēj*/*njȅj*/*njȉ*), not a reduction of them sharing the same vowel. Slovene's full and short forms don't even agree with each other here, a real complication distinct from Serbo-Croatian's clean full/short vowel match (*njoj*/*joj*). This wasn't resolved here; it's noted as further evidence that this whole paradigm cell has a messier history across the family than most others built so far.

## Register — Clitic Placement

Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], јој occupies the dative-clitic slot in the second-position template, the same slot [[му]] occupies for masculine. Full [[њој]] is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; јој takes the ordinary slot otherwise.
