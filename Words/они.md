# они (oni)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Eighth individual pronoun entry, and the direct plural counterpart of [[он]]. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Where the Neuter Merger Lands in the Plural

"They" (masculine/mixed-gender/old-neuter-plural referents) — but the parallel with [[он]] isn't exact. [[он]] absorbs *every* old-neuter-**singular** referent directly. In the plural, [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger sends old-neuter-plural referents to **feminine** [[оне]] instead, not to они — matching the real Romanian-style pattern already adopted for nouns (old neuter singular → masculine agreement, old neuter plural → feminine agreement). So они itself is actually the *narrower* word of the two: strictly masculine/mixed-referent "they," never a catch-all for "things" the way он catches "it." That job belongs to [[оне]].

## Etymology — The Same Split as [[он]], Pluralized

**This isn't a new mechanism — it's [[он]]'s own nominative/oblique split, carried into the plural by the same two roots.** Fetched both Proto-Slavic reconstruction pages again specifically for their plural rows, rather than assuming the plural just pluralizes mechanically.

**Nominative они** ← Proto-Slavic **\*oni**, the masculine plural nominative of **\*onъ** (the same demonstrative that supplies [[он]]'s nominative). Per the *onъ* reconstruction's own declension table, feminine plural nominative is a distinct form, **\*ony** — the ancestor of [[оне]], not of они. So the gender split visible in Sisician's они/оне pair already existed in the Proto-Slavic paradigm itself, not invented for this project.

**Oblique stem њих/њима** ← Proto-Slavic **\*jixъ** (genitive/locative plural) and **\*jimъ**/**\*jimi** (dative/instrumental plural) — the plural forms of the *same* older pronoun ***jь*** whose singular oblique forms (њега/њему/њим) were already established on [[он]]'s page. The *jь* reconstruction's plural row gives identical *jixъ/jimъ/jimi* across all three genders (masculine, feminine, *and* neuter) — no gender distinction at all in the oblique plural, which is exactly why real Croatian *njih/njima* and Sisician's њих/њима are shared between они and [[оне]] in the table (only genitive/accusative have since been given a separate feminine form, ЊЕХ, per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] § Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate — an innovation on top of this inherited syncretism, not a continuation of it).

**The prothetic-n- mechanism is extended here, not independently re-confirmed.** [[он]]'s page cites the *jь* reconstruction's own text explicitly documenting prothetic *n-* attaching "following a preposition, in many Slavic languages" — but that text discusses the phenomenon in general terms and doesn't specifically call out the plural oblique forms. Real Croatian *njih/njima* show the same *nj-* onset as singular *njega/njemu*, so treating it as the same mechanism generalized across the whole paradigm (rather than a separate, second prothetic-n event) is a reasonable extension of a cited fact, not itself a separately cited fact — flagging the difference honestly, the same practice used for [[он]]'s own page.

**No branch tension found.** Serbo-Croatian *ȍni*, Slovene *ǫ̑ni* — South Slavic is stable on the masculine plural nominative, same as [[он]]'s singular.

## Register

Same closed-class status as every pronoun so far. Per [[../Grammar/Verbal Agreement|Verbal Agreement]]'s pro-drop, они can be omitted once a referent is established, but — as already noted on [[он]]'s page — 3rd-person reference needs restating more often than 1st/2nd in ordinary discourse. No T–V interaction; that system is 2nd person only.

## Related
