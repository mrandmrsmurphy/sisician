# ме (me)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Eleventh individual pronoun entry, and the second oblique-case page after [[мене]] — the first entry specifically about a clitic *form* rather than a full/stressed one. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] and [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]] for the systems this word plugs into, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Where This Word's Two Senses Actually Come From

ме is [[мене]]'s short/enclitic counterpart, covering both genitive and accusative — but unlike мене, **this syncretism has its own separate history, not just an inherited copy of мене's.** мене's page already established that Proto-Slavic kept genitive *\*mȅne* and a *third*, genuinely distinct accusative-only enclitic, ***\*mę̃***, and that Sisician doesn't carry *\*mę̃* forward as an independent full form. ме is where that decision actually cashes out: the short form realizes *\*mę̃* directly (see Etymology), but has been **extended to cover genitive too** — a real syncretism attested in modern South Slavic clitic systems generally, not invented for this project, and not present yet in Old Church Slavonic, which had no dedicated short genitive clitic at all (genitive there was full-form only).

## Etymology

**Directly continues Proto-Slavic \*mę̃, via a real, general Slavic sound change — not a survival of мене's own genitive stem.** [[мене]]'s page already flagged that the fetched *azъ declension table gives a genuinely separate accusative enclitic, *\*mę̃* (nasal vowel), ancestor of Old Church Slavonic **мѧ** (mę). The regular development of Proto-Slavic's nasal vowel *ę in South Slavic (and East Slavic) is **denasalization to e** — Polish is the well-known exception that kept nasal vowels; South Slavic lost them early. **Flagging the evidentiary weight honestly: this denasalization fact is general, well-established Slavic historical phonology, recalled rather than freshly confirmed by a specific citation this session** (attempts to pull the exact Wiktionary/Wikipedia passage on ę's regular South Slavic outcome didn't return a clean quotable statement, unlike the declension-table facts on [[мене]]'s page, which were directly quoted). *\*mę̃* → **ме** by this same regular denasalization is therefore presented as a confident but uncited derivation, the same evidentiary category as [[../Words/нув|нув]]'s unconfirmed Latin/Greek/Sanskrit cognates.

**The genitive extension is a real, independent syncretism, not part of that sound change.** Old Church Slavonic's мѧ was accusative-only; there was no comparable short genitive clitic to denasalize into ме's genitive sense. Modern South Slavic vernaculars (including the real Serbo-Croatian this project draws on throughout) generalized the accusative clitic to cover genitive as well, collapsing what OCS kept apart — the same kind of case-syncretism-by-leveling already seen elsewhere in this project (e.g. [[ви]]'s nominative, though that was case-leveling in the *opposite* direction, oblique-to-nominative rather than one oblique case absorbing another). Sisician simply inherits this already-generalized modern pattern rather than reconstructing it independently.

**Real Slavic comparanda (accusative/genitive clitic):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | мѧ (mę, accusative-only; no genitive clitic) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | me (accusative and genitive clitic, both) |
| South Slavic | Slovene | me |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian | ме (me) |
| East Slavic | Russian | меня survives only as the full form; no distinct short clitic in the standard language |
| West Slavic | Polish | mię (retains the nasal, archaic/literary); mnie as the modern oblique form |

**Branch tension worth flagging, though not requiring revision:** Polish's retained nasal (*mię*) is a real, visible reminder that Sisician's ме reflects the South Slavic denasalized outcome specifically, not a pan-Slavic default — consistent with every other design choice in this project, so no revision needed, but worth stating rather than leaving implicit.

## Register — Clitic Placement, Not Just Informality

Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], ме is a true second-position (Wackernagel) clitic with a fixed slot relative to other clitics in the sentence, not merely an "unstressed casual variant" of мене the way English "'em" is a casual variant of "them." The two forms aren't interchangeable by register — full мене is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; ме is required (not just preferred) in ordinary second-position clitic contexts. See [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]] for exactly where ме sits when it stacks with other clitics (e.g. alongside a short form of [[../Grammar/Biti (To Be)|biti]]).
