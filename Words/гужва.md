# гужва (gužva)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Coined for [[../Texts/Conlang Syntax Test Cases|Conlang Syntax Test Cases]] item 11 ("All the people shouted"), from the Proto-Slavic root the user specified. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"A crowd, a throng, a mass of people" gathered together, the people as a physical mass. Singular collective: **ел гужва** "the crowd, the people," with feminine singular agreement. Not "a people" or "a nation" (that is [[../Words/сишчани|сишчани]]'s territory), and not "persons" individually. Real Serbo-Croatian and Macedonian *gužva* extend it to "jam, crush" and, in Serbo-Croatian colloquially, "trouble"; Sisician's sense is the first.

The genitive plural **гужви** follows real Serbo-Croatian *gȗžvī*, whose ending is the usual one for stems ending in a consonant cluster, and it also avoids the sameness with the nominative singular that каза has. Stress is root-anchored on гу́-.

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*gǫži**, a feminine soft v-stem, from earlier \*(v)ǫži and PIE \*h₂eng- "to bend" ([Wiktionary \*gǫži](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/gǫži)). The oblique cases are built on **\*gǫžьv-** (gen. \*gǫžьve, acc. \*gǫžьvь). **Its original meaning is "wattle, pleach," most often bent into a ring**, so the Proto-Slavic sense is something woven or bent, not a crowd. The "crowd, jam" sense is a later development, attested in both Serbo-Croatian and Macedonian: the [Wiktionary entry for гужва](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/гужва) gives "crowd or jam" for both languages, each inherited from \*gǫži, and Macedonian also keeps "a straw bracelet" and "a stitch," the older woven/ring sense. I did not check Bulgarian, Slovene or the other branches for the crowd sense, so it is shown in two South Slavic languages, not established as the whole branch. Sisician takes the "crowd" meaning (a tangled, pressed-together mass) rather than the original one.

**Descendants listed:** Old East Slavic гѫжьва, Russian гужва, Bulgarian гъжва, Macedonian гужва, Serbo-Croatian гужва, Slovene gọ̑žva, Old Czech húžev/húžva, Polish dialectal gązwa. The page also lists Romanian *gujbă* and Hungarian *gúzsba* as borrowings, which is worth noticing for a language with Hungarian contact, though not used here.

**Sisician derivation, three steps, all already established:**

1. **Stem selection.** Like Serbo-Croatian, the oblique stem \*gǫžьv- generalizes and the nominative \*gǫži is replaced by an a-stem form, \*gǫžьva. This is a real Serbo-Croatian development, recalled rather than cited on the Wiktionary page itself.
2. **Fall of the Jers** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): the ь is the word's only jer, followed by a full vowel, hence weak: it drops: \*gǫžьva → \*gǫžva.
3. **ǫ → u** ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 2): \*gǫžva → **гужва**.

The initial г is word-initial, so lenition ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 12) does not apply, though after a vowel-final word it would surface as [ɣ]. The cluster -жв- is a fricative+fricative coda-onset pair, the same abstract type as the onset in [[../Words/все|все]]. The result is identical to Serbo-Croatian *gužva*.
