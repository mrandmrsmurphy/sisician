# је (je)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Seventeenth individual pronoun entry — and, like [[ми]] before it, a genuinely two-sense entry rather than a single word with two related jobs (contrast [[ви]], which really is one word doing two jobs).

## Meaning — Two Unrelated Words, Not One Word Extended

**је does double duty, and [[../Grammar/Biti (To Be)|Biti (To Be)]]'s own page already flagged the collision before this page existed to resolve it:** (1) **the short/enclitic genitive form of [[ње]], "her"/"of her"** — the primary subject of this entry; and (2) **[[../Grammar/Biti (To Be)|biti]]'s 3sg present clitic, "is"** (the ordinary copula and perfect-tense auxiliary). These are **historically and grammatically unrelated** — a pronoun oblique-case clitic and a verb-paradigm auxiliary clitic that simply happen to have converged on the identical shape. This is the same category of accident as [[ми]] (1pl nominative "we" / 1sg dative clitic "to me"), not the same category as [[ви]] (one word, one real extension). Resolved by syntactic position and context in speech, not by any formal marking — exactly as real Serbo-Croatian resolves the identical collision (*je* "her" clitic vs. *je* "is" clitic are both real, both common, and both genuinely homophonous there too, not a Sisician-specific coincidence).

This page covers sense (1); sense (2) belongs to [[../Grammar/Biti (To Be)|Biti (To Be)]]'s own paradigm and is only cross-referenced here.

## Etymology

**Directly continues [[ње]]'s stem, but — like [[га]] before it — the precise short-form derivation isn't cleanly citable.** [[ње]]'s page fetched the *jь* feminine singular declension and found only the full genitive **\*jeję̇**; a follow-up check specifically for a short/enclitic genitive alternate (parallel to how masculine accusative had documented short alternates *и/нь*) turned up nothing — **no short genitive form is shown or discussed for feminine in the reconstruction consulted.** So, as with [[га]], the honest position is: **је is real and attested exactly this way in modern South Slavic, but its specific reduction path from *\*jeję̇* (loss of unstressed final material, presumably, the same general kind of process as [[га]]'s hypothesized *(j)ego→go→ga*) is not established by any citation reached this session.**

**Real Slavic comparanda (genitive clitic):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | no distinct short genitive form found in the sources checked (full неѩ only) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | je (genitive clitic, distinct from accusative clitic ju) |
| South Slavic | Slovene | je |
| East Slavic | Russian | no comparable short clitic in the standard language (full её used) |

**Worth flagging explicitly: feminine je and masculine [[га]] didn't merge into one shared clitic**, even though masculine genitive/accusative did merge into single **га**. This is consistent with [[ње]]'s finding that feminine genitive and accusative were never the same slot to begin with (unlike masculine) — je (genitive) and ју (accusative) stay two different clitics for exactly the same underlying reason ње and њу stay two different full forms.

## Register — And the Practical Consequence of the Homophony

Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], the pronoun clitic је occupies the same second-position object-clitic slot as [[ме]]/[[те]]/[[га]]; the auxiliary clitic је (sense 2) occupies the separate auxiliary-clitic slot in that same template. **This means the two senses aren't just homophonous in isolation — they can, in principle, both appear in the same clause**, one in each clitic slot (e.g. a sentence meaning roughly "her [X] is [Y]," with pronoun-је and auxiliary-је both present, distinguished by position in the clitic string rather than by form at all). [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]]'s fixed template is what actually keeps this parseable, not anything about је itself.
