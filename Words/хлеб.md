# хлеб (hleb)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Bread" — part of the [[../Texts/Pater Noster|Pater Noster]] set: **хлеб наш насуштни дај нам данас** "give us this day our daily bread." Already used informally in [[../Grammar/Case Government|Case Government]]'s partitive examples (*Дао сам му хлеб* / *хлеба*) before it had a page of its own; this is that page. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Bread" — the everyday staple, warm and unmarked. In the prayer, the object of the petition (fronted to first position, liturgical order: хлеб наш насуштни дај нам...).

## Declension (a-declension, hard variant, masculine inanimate)

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | хлеб | хлебови |
| Genitive | хлеба | хлебова |
| Dative | хлебу | хлебовима |
| Accusative | хлеб (inan. = nom.) | хлебове (inan.) |
| Instrumental | хлебом | хлебовима |

Monosyllabic root, so the plural takes the **-ов- extension** (хлебови), the same pattern as [[../Words/дом|дом]] — real BCS *hleb* → *hlebovi* shows the identical shape. Inanimate: accusative = nominative.

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*xlěbъ** — from Proto-Germanic \*hlaibaz, Gothic 𐌷𐌻𐌰𐌹𐍆𐍃 *(hlaifs)* ([Wiktionary хлеб](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D1%85%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B1), fetched). A small irony worth noting: the "native" Slavic bread word is itself an old Germanic loan — cognate with English *loaf* (and, through the same Germanic word, *lady* and *lord*). **Derivation:** Ekavian yat (already in the c. 1000 baseline) gives ě→e: \*xlěbъ → \*xlebъ; the final ъ is weak and drops ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): **хлеб**. The х is the modern palatal /ç/ ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 11): /ˈçlɛb/. The final б stays a plain stop — it is word-final, not intervocalic, so § 12's lenition does not apply. Matches real Serbian *hleb*; the *j* of Croatian *hljeb* is a BCS-internal development not shared here.

## Register

Native, unmarked, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer I (household). The prayer's "daily bread" is ordinary vocabulary in a liturgical frame.
