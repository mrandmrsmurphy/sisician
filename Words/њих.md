# њих (njih)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Twentieth individual pronoun entry — the genitive/accusative cell of [[они]]'s paradigm, and the plural counterpart of [[њега]]'s cell, showing yet another one of the four genitive/accusative histories now on record.

## Meaning — Shared with [[оне]], Except Where ЊЕХ Differentiates

њих covers genitive and accusative for masculine/mixed-referent "them," and — per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s own table — **the identical form also served feminine "them" until the ЊЕХ innovation split them apart** (see that page's § Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate). So њих today is specifically the masculine/mixed value; [[оне]]'s own genitive/accusative is the innovated ЊЕХ, not њих, even though both descend from the same inherited syncretic root.

## Etymology — A Fifth Confirmation of the нас-Pattern, Not a New Mechanism

**Fetched the *jь* masculine plural row specifically, rather than assuming the singular's genitive-absorbs-accusative story ([[њега]]'s pattern) would repeat mechanically.** It didn't repeat that pattern — it repeated [[нас]]'s instead: the table gives **genitive \*jixъ** and a **genuinely distinct accusative, \*ję̇** — confirmed explicitly as two different forms, not a scraping artifact. This is structurally the [[нас]] story, not the [[њега]] story: a real, separate accusative-only shape existed in Proto-Slavic (*\*ję̇*, incidentally identical in shape across masculine *and* feminine plural, per the same fetch used for [[они]]'s own page) but **didn't survive**, and genitive *\*jixъ* generalized outward to cover both jobs instead — the same resolution already seen for [[нас]], now confirmed for the 3rd-plural masculine as well.

**The prothetic-n mechanism, already established on [[он]]'s page and extended to the plural on [[они]]'s, makes one further clean prediction worth stating explicitly here for the first time:** across every 3rd-person oblique cell built so far — [[њега]]/[[га]], [[ње]]/[[је]], and now њих/[[их]] — **the njь-prefix appears only on the full/stressed form, never on the clitic.** This isn't a separate fact needing its own citation; it follows directly from the mechanism itself — the prothetic *n-* arose specifically from resegmenting a *preposition* ending in *-n* onto the pronoun (*"vъn jego" → "vъ njego"*), an environment clitics don't occupy the same way full forms do. их's bare shape, with no њ-, is exactly what that mechanism predicts, not an unexplained gap in an otherwise-tidy paradigm.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | (н)их ((n)ixъ) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | njȋh; clitic ih |
| South Slavic | Slovene | njȋh; clitic jih |
| East Slavic | Russian | их (ix) |

**No branch tension found** — the genitive-generalization outcome and the prothetic-n distribution are both pan-Slavic-consistent, matching every other 3rd-person pronoun page built so far.

## Register

Same closed-class status as [[они]]. Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], full њих is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; [[их]] takes the ordinary second-position clitic slot otherwise.
