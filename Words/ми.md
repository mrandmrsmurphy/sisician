# ми (mi)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Sixth individual pronoun entry. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Genuinely Two Senses, Not One

**ми does double duty**, and this page has to cover both rather than picking one: (1) **1st person plural nominative, "we"** — the primary sense this entry is built around; and (2) **the short/enclitic dative form of [[јаз]], "to me"** (already flagged in [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s own homophony note). These are historically unrelated forms that happen to have converged on the identical shape — real Croatian has exactly the same collision, resolved by context/position, not by any formal marking. This word entry is specifically about sense (1); sense (2) belongs to [[јаз]]'s own paradigm and is only cross-referenced here.

## Etymology

**Deep root:** PIE **\*més** (probably shortened from **\*n̥smés**) ← Proto-Slavic **\*my**. Real cognates confirmed by this fetch: Lithuanian *mẽs*, Old Armenian *մեք* (*mekʻ*) — both "we," showing this exact root's spread across Indo-European. **Note on scope:** this citation didn't confirm a Latin/Greek/Sanskrit nominative cognate directly — flagging the gap rather than filling it with unsourced confidence, the same practice used for [[../Words/нув|нув]].

**A genuinely separate suppletive oblique stem, like every other pronoun so far:** нас/нама continue a *different* Proto-Slavic root, **\*nasъ**/**\*namъ**/**\*nami**, not ми itself. This is the same nominative/oblique suppletion pattern already seen in [[јаз]]/мене and [[он]]/њега — but here it's worth flagging a further comparative point (general Indo-European background, not sourced to this specific fetch): the oblique *nas-* stem is the same broad ancestral material that gives Latin its *entire* 1st-plural paradigm, *nos* — meaning Slavic's oblique "us/to us" and Latin's whole word for "we" ultimately share ancestry, even though Slavic kept a separate nominative (*my*) that Latin didn't.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | мꙑ (my) |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian, Macedonian | ние (real innovation — see below) |
| East Slavic | Russian | мы (my) |
| East Slavic | Ukrainian | ми (my) |
| West Slavic | Czech, Polish | my |
| West Slavic | Slovak | my |

**Checked for branch tension, and found one — but not in the branch that matters here.** Bulgarian and Macedonian genuinely replaced *my* with an innovated form, *ние*, via analogical reformation — a real Slavic-internal split, structurally the same *kind* of thing as the [[јаз]]/*jaz* situation. But this innovation is specifically East South Slavic (Bulgarian/Macedonian); it doesn't touch Serbo-Croatian, Slovene, or Kajkavian, which all keep the inherited *my* reflex. Since Sisician's vernacular is Kajkavian-based, ми needs no reconsideration — unlike [[јаз]], where the relevant branch split *did* land on Sisician's own side of the divide.
