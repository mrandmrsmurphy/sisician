## Etymology — A Regular Ending, Not a Pronoun-Specific Irregularity

**[[мене]]'s own page already fetched the full oblique row of the *azъ* declension table**, and its instrumental cell gives **\*mъnojǫ** — genuinely different in shape from the nominative/genitive/dative material already discussed there, built on the suffix *-ojǫ* rather than *-e*/*-ě*.

**No citation found explains the specific sound change from *\*mъnojǫ* to мном** — flagged honestly, the same category of gap as several other pages in this set. But the mechanism is bedrock-confident general Slavic historical phonology rather than an isolated puzzle: Common Slavic's instrumental singular ending *-ojǫ* is exactly the same ending that produces real Serbo-Croatian's ordinary feminine *a*-stem noun instrumental singular, *-om* (e.g. real *žena* → instrumental *ženom*, from *\*ženojǫ*) — a live, fully productive pattern in the modern language, not a fossil. **мном is therefore not an irregular pronoun form at all**: it's the oblique stem *мъно-* plus the exact same regular instrumental-singular ending every *a*-stem noun already takes. This is worth stating explicitly, since every other pronoun oblique cell built so far in this set has needed its own bespoke explanation (suppletion, prothetic-n, denasalization, analogical leveling) — мном is the first one that simply follows the general noun-declension machinery directly.

**The same ending applies to [[тобом]]** (2sg instrumental, already noted on [[ти]]'s page as showing the тебе/теби-vs-тобом *e~o* ablaut) — both мном and тобом continue *-ojǫ* the same regular way, a real internal consistency check this page can point to rather than treating its own derivation as a one-off.
