# нама (nama)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Thirtieth individual pronoun entry — the dative/instrumental cell of [[ми]]'s paradigm, and the first pronoun page in this set covering a genuine, real dative=instrumental syncretism rather than the genitive=accusative pattern seen everywhere else so far.

## Meaning — A Different Case-Pairing Than Every Prior Syncretism

Every syncretism built so far in this set has been genitive=accusative ([[мене]], [[тебе]], [[њега]], [[нас]], [[вас]], [[њих]]). нама is the first **dative=instrumental** pairing — "to us" and "with us" share one form, per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table, a genuinely different case-pair than any prior page.

## Etymology

**[[нас]]'s own page already fetched the relevant Proto-Slavic forms**, and they're genuinely distinct: dative **\*nàmъ** and instrumental **\*nàmi** — different endings (*-ъ* vs. *-i*), not two spellings of one thing. So нама's dative=instrumental syncretism, unlike every genitive=accusative case built so far, isn't inherited directly; it's a real merger of two originally-separate Proto-Slavic cells.

**Neither form obviously produces нама's own shape by a simple regular sound change, and this page flags that honestly rather than asserting a clean derivation.** The [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] would reduce *\*namъ* to a bare **нам** (matching the short clitic [[нам]] exactly — see below), not to a stressed form ending in *-a*. Instrumental *\*nami* doesn't produce an *-a* ending either. So нама's full/stressed *-a* shape isn't the direct regular reflex of either historical cell on its own.

**The most plausible account, offered as recalled general Slavic comparative morphology rather than a fresh citation:** real South Slavic pronoun plural oblique forms show a documented tendency to be reshaped by analogy with the highly productive feminine *a*-stem noun plural dative/instrumental ending, **-ама** (e.g. real Croatian *žena* → dative/instrumental plural *ženama*) — the same ending family, generalized onto the pronoun paradigm. This is a real, recognized type of pronoun-to-noun analogical leveling in Slavic historical morphology, but it isn't confirmed by any citation found, so it's presented as the most likely explanation rather than an established fact.

**A real, cited precedent for dative/instrumental convergence existing in Slavic generally, worth naming even though it doesn't directly prove this specific form:** Common Slavic's own **dual** number already had dative and instrumental syncretized onto a single ending (from PIE *\*-me*/*\*-oH*) before the dual was even lost — meaning dative=instrumental merger isn't a novel idea invented for нама, it's a recurring pattern already present elsewhere in real Slavic grammatical history. This is offered as supporting context for why such a merger is plausible, not as the specific mechanism behind нама's own -a vowel.

**The short form [[нам]] is comparatively simple and well-grounded**, by contrast: it continues **\*namъ** directly via the ordinary [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] (loss of the weak final jer), the same universal sound law used throughout this project — no special pleading needed, unlike the full form.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Dative | Instrumental |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | намъ (namŭ) | нами (nami) — kept distinct |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | nama | nama — merged |
| South Slavic | Slovene | nam | nami — kept distinct |
| East Slavic | Russian | нам (nam) | нами (nami) — kept distinct |
| West Slavic | Polish | nam | nami — kept distinct |

**A real branch tension worth flagging plainly:** Serbo-Croatian is the *only* branch checked here where dative and instrumental actually merged into one form — every other branch, including South Slavic's own Slovene, kept them distinct. Sisician's нама follows the Serbo-Croatian outlier specifically, the same kind of unresolved-but-acknowledged branch choice already made for [[њој]], not a pan-Slavic default.
