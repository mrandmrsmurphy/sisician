# к (k)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** A fossil preposition: no longer productive, replaced by [[../Words/код|код]] + dative. Survives only in fixed phrases, mostly before vowel-initial words. See [[../Grammar/Case Government|Case Government]] for the system and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

Formerly "toward, to" — goal of motion, typically a person: the spatial extension of dative's recipient sense. That job now belongs to **[[../Words/код|код]] + dative** ("to X's place"), the fourth case of код's within/beside system (see [[../Grammar/Case Government|Case Government]] § код: Within vs. Beside).

## Why It Fell Out of Use

- **Clusters.** A bare к fuses with the next word into an initial consonant cluster — [kt-], [kp-], [ks-], or the old **к њему** [kɲ-]. To Sisician ears these sound overly Slavic, the same kind of marker [[../Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman|Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman]] describes being avoided in prestige speech.
- **A ready replacement.** код is itself к + од, so код + dative could be heard as a fuller form of к + dative. Replacing a short preposition with a longer compound of itself is a common path (standard example: French *dans*, from Latin *de intus*, replacing *en* in most spatial uses; not separately cited).

**Where it survives:** in fixed phrases, especially where the governed word begins with a vowel. There к simply becomes the onset of the next syllable, and no cluster arises. Which phrases these are is not yet decided.

## Etymology

Common Slavic **\*kъ(n)** (standard, not separately cited). The weak jer drops ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]); its old final -n (\*kъn) is one source of the prothetic н- on њега/њему/њим — see [[../Words/он|он]] § Etymology. The н-forms outlived к itself: they now follow any preposition, код included (**код њему**).

## Register

Archaic: fixed phrases only. Closed-class grammatical vocabulary, outside [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]]' three-layer model.
