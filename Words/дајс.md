# дајс (dajs)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Thick, dense," filling [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 30, and the first Swadesh item where the **Romance strand wins outright**, at the user's direction, from the Swadesh table's Dalmatian entry *dais*. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Thick, dense": **дајс лес** "a dense forest," **дајса магла** "a thick fog," **дајси домови** "closely packed houses." It covers thickness as density and compactness (of fog, forest, soup, hair), the sense of Latin *densus* and English *dense*, and also plain thickness of an object (below).

**Also the measurement sense (user's decision):** "thick" of a slab, a rope, a branch, with no Sisician example yet because the nouns aren't coined. This is the thick-vs-thin axis, so дајс is the word that item 35 "thin" will contrast with. "Fat" (of a person or animal) is **not** claimed; the native words (PS \*tъlstъ, \*debelъ, \*tukъ) are not coined and nothing here displaces them yet.

**Adverbial use: the masculine singular, unchanged**, per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § Adjective to Adverb: **дајс** is also "densely, thickly."

## Forms

A hard-declension adjective, gender and number agreement only, like every adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | дајс | дајса |
| Plural | дајси | дајсе |

No case and no definiteness. A noun that is old-neuter-plural takes the feminine plural, by the neuter merger. **Stress is root-anchored on -ај-** throughout (ˈdajs, ˈdajsa, ˈdajsi, ˈdajse), the same place real Vegliote *dais* stresses.

## Etymology

**Dalmatian dais**, the Swadesh table's entry for "thick" ([Appendix:Dalmatian Swadesh list](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Appendix:Dalmatian_Swadesh_list)). The Latin source is not given there (the table's Vulgar Latin column lists \*grossu and \*spissu for this item). The user supplied **Latin *densus*** "dense, thick," a first/second-declension adjective; it is the same word as French *dense* and Italian *denso*.

**Latin → Dalmatian, my reconstruction (the Dalmatian side is not worked in [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]):**

1. **Vulgar Latin \*densu.** The ending -us → -u, as for the other Vulgar Latin forms in [[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Sources/Vulgar Latin]].
2. **Nasal loss before s, with compensatory lengthening: \*densu → \*dēsu.** This is the point where I depart from the plain route. The straight route would be short ĕ in a closed syllable (dĕns-), which [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s diphthongization model sends to *-ja-*, and which the real Vegliote word *diant* "tooth" (< *dentem*, same ĕ, same closed syllable; Swadesh item 77) confirms: that route would predict something like \*dians, not *dais*. But *dais* has the **ai** that real Vegliote *monaita* (< MONĒTA) shows for **ē**. So *dais* fits a long ē, and the way to get ē from *densus* is the ordinary Romance loss of /n/ before /s/ with the vowel lengthened (as in Italian *mese* and French *mois* < *mensem*, and Classical *cōsul* beside *cōnsul*). I'm recalling that pattern from general Romance knowledge; I have not fetched a source for it, and the vault has no entry on it yet.
3. **ē → ai**, the *monaita* outcome ([[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Additional Diphthong Outcomes), not the page's ē→ie/ja model. This is the second word with this outcome, so it adds a second real data point for ē→ai without resolving what conditions it.
4. **Final vowel drops, final -s stays**: \*dēsu → **dais**.

This is exactly the kind of unmodeled Dalmatian vowel data point that page already records, and I have added no rule to it.

**Sisician adaptation**, no new mechanism:

1. **ai → ај**, the same respelling as [[../Words/монајта|монајта]] and [[../Words/денајр|денајр]].
2. **s → с**, final, unchanged.
3. **Stress** stays on the diphthong syllable, the pre-c. 1300 loan rule ([[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Prosody): **ˈдајс**, same place as денајр's.
4. **Intervocalic lenition** (§ 12) does not apply: there is no б, д or г in the word.

**Phonotactics check.** The final **-јс** is a glide + obstruent coda, sonority-falling like денајр's -јр and кертиојн's -јн ([[../Phonology/Phonotactics|Phonotactics]]). It is the first such coda ending in an obstruent.

**Feminine and plural forms.** I build them from the Sisician citation form by adding the hard-declension endings, so the с stays [s] (дајса). In Dalmatian itself an intervocalic /s/ would voice to [z] ([[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Intervocalic /s/ Voices to [z], as in *kaza* → [[../Words/каза|каза]]). Here the s follows the glide, not a vowel, in the Sisician spelling, so I left it voiceless; whether дајса should be [ˈdajza] is open.

## Register

Dalmatian-formal, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer II. Unlike most Layer II words, this one is basic vocabulary, a core Swadesh item, so it is a case of the Romance strand taking a slot in the everyday lexicon, the way English took *face* and *use* from French.

## Open Points

- **The Latin route** (steps 2–3) is my inference, flagged above: it explains why *dais* has ai and not the ĕ-route result, but no source confirms it.
- **Native doublets** (\*tъlstъ, \*debelъ) aren't coined. дајс now covers both "dense" and the measurement "thick," so a native word would only compete for "fat" or for a displacement story, neither built.
- **The [s] vs [z]** of дајса.
- **The comparative** and **an abstract noun** ("density, thickness") aren't built.
- **The opposite**: Swadesh item 35 "thin" will need its own word, now contrasting with дајс across both senses.
