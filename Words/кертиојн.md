# кертиојн (kertiojn)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Certain," filling [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 19 together with [[../Words/неколико|неколико]] "some." The user's design: Sisician splits English *some* the way English splits *some* from *certain*, and this is the **specific** half, built on the Swadesh table's Dalmatian entry. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"A certain": a particular referent the speaker has in mind but does not name or identify. **кертиојн дом** "a certain house," **кертиојни домови** "certain houses." Where [[../Words/неколико|неколико]] gives a loose quantity with no particular ones in view, this picks out particular ones without saying which. It is the sense of English *a certain man* and French *une certaine femme*, which Wiktionary glosses "of indefinite, unknown, or unmentioned identity."

The other English sense, "sure" (*it is certain that…*), is **not** claimed for this word here.

## Forms

A hard-declension adjective with gender and number agreement only, like every adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | кертиојн | кертиојна |
| Plural | кертиојни | кертиојне |

No case and no definiteness. A noun that is old-neuter-plural takes the feminine plural, by the neuter merger. **Stress is root-anchored on -ој-** throughout (kerˈtiojn, kerˈtiojna, kerˈtiojni).

## Etymology

**Dalmatian certioin**, the Swadesh table's Dalmatian entry for "some" ([Appendix:Dalmatian Swadesh list](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Appendix:Dalmatian_Swadesh_list), where it is a red link with no page of its own). The Latin source is not given there. I take it from the French cognate: [Wiktionary *certain*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/certain#French) says "Inherited from Old French certain, from Vulgar Latin unattested form \*certānus," an "extended form of Latin certus ('fixed, resolved, certain')." That identification is my inference from the match in meaning and shape, not something the Dalmatian entry states.

**The Latin→Dalmatian derivation isn't worked.** The stressed -ā- of \*certānus giving the -ioi- of certioin doesn't follow from [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s models. It adds a third vowel outcome beside *cituot*'s a→uo and *monaita*'s e→ai, and is recorded as another unmodeled data point, not forced into a rule.

**Sisician adaptation**, no new mechanism:

1. **c before e stays /k/** ([[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Velars Preserved Before /e/): cer- → **кер-**.
2. **The -ti- is just t + i.** Nothing here is a TJ cluster (no yod), so it does not affricate: **-ти-**.
3. **oi → ој**, the same respelling as [[../Words/монајта|монајта]]'s monaita → -ајт- and [[../Words/денајр|денајр]]: **-ој-**.
4. **Final -n stays.** Words that entered before c. 1300 keep the donor's Latin-descended stress ([[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Prosody), and the stress here is on the diphthong syllable (the stressed -ā- of \*certānus), so **кертиˈојн**.
5. **Intervocalic lenition** (§ 12) does not apply: the only stop, т, is voiceless.

**Phonotactics check.** The final **-јн** is a glide+nasal coda, sonority-falling like [[../Words/денајр|денајр]]'s -јр ([[../Phonology/Phonotactics|Phonotactics]]). This is the second coda cluster of the kind, and the first one ending in a nasal.

## Register

Dalmatian-formal, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer II. Specific-but-unnamed reference is the flavor of notarial and legal Dalmatian prose (*a certain person*), which fits the layer.

## Open Points

- **With the article or један.** How кертиојн combines with [[../Grammar/Definiteness|the article]] or **један** ("one") isn't decided. French uses it after the indefinite article.
- **A native doublet.** Whether the prestige loan displaces a native word is not worked out, because no native specific-indefinite word has been coined.
- **The "sure" sense** is unbuilt.
