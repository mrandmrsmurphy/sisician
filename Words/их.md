# их (ih)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Twenty-first individual pronoun entry — the short/clitic counterpart to [[њих]], and — unusually for this corner of the paradigm — the *more* conservative of the two forms, not a reduction of the full one.

## Meaning

The unstressed second-position clitic covering genitive and accusative for masculine/mixed "them," per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table. Shared historically with feminine until the ЊЕХ innovation split it off (see [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] § Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate and [[оне]]'s own page) — их/[[ех]] today are the masculine-specific pair, distinct from feminine's own [[ех]].

## Etymology — их Is the Base Form, њих Is the Innovation

**[[њих]]'s own page already established a clean generalization: prothetic n- appears only on full/stressed forms, never on clitics, because it arose from resegmenting a preposition — an environment clitics don't occupy.** их is the direct, worked example of that principle, not just a case it happens to be consistent with: **их's bare shape isn't a reduction of њих; it's the *unmodified* continuation of the pre-prothetic form, and њих is what's actually innovated relative to it.**

**Direct textual confirmation, not just reconstruction:** fetched Old Church Slavonic's own "Declension of the third personal pronoun" table specifically for its plural masculine row, and it gives **genitive ихъ (ixŭ)** — attested, not reconstructed — already without any n-, alongside a **genuinely distinct accusative, ѩ (ję)** confirming (via real attested OCS text, not just the *jь* reconstruction) exactly the same genitive/accusative split [[њих]]'s page already established from the reconstruction alone. Modern **их** continues OCS **ихъ** directly via the [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] (loss of the weak final jer *-ъ*) — the same universal sound law already applied to [[дом]] and every other word in this project, not a special exception. The old accusative-specific **ѩ** didn't survive, exactly paralleling [[нас]]'s *ny* and [[тебе]]/[[мене]]'s partial parallel — except here, unlike [[мене]]/[[тебе]], the *lost* form was the accusative-specific one and the *surviving* clitic is genitive-descended, matching [[њих]]'s own finding rather than the singular's.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | ихъ (ixŭ, genitive); ѩ (ję, accusative — lost) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | ih |
| South Slavic | Slovene | jih |
| East Slavic | Russian | их (ix) |
| West Slavic | Polish | ich |

**No branch tension found** — every branch continues genitive *ixъ*, none preserve *ję* as a living clitic.

## Register — Clitic Placement

Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], их is a true second-position (Wackernagel) clitic. Full [[њих]] is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; их takes the ordinary second-position slot otherwise.

## Related

- [[њих]] — the full/stressed form, historically the *innovated* member of this pair (n- added), not их's own ancestor.
- [[они]] — the nominative this whole paradigm belongs to.
- [[ех]] — the feminine-specific counterpart, split off from их by the ЊЕХ innovation rather than a shared inheritance.
- [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] — the ordinary sound law deriving modern их from attested OCS ихъ, the same law used throughout this project.
- [[нас]] — the closest structural parallel: a real accusative form existed and was lost, genitive generalized to cover both slots.
