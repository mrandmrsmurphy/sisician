## Etymology

**Deep root:** Proto-Slavic **\*(j)ãzъ** "I" ← Proto-Balto-Slavic **\*ēź-** ← PIE **\*éǵ(h₂)** (the same root behind Latin *ego*, Greek ἐγώ, Sanskrit *aham*, and — through a separate Germanic path — English *I*). The exact internal reconstruction is genuinely disputed among Slavicists, not settled: Kapović treats *ja* and *jazъ* as two forms from two different PIE bases (*éǵ(h₂)* vs. *eǵh₂óm*); Kortlandt reconstructs one ancestral form (*\*ēˀźun*, from *eǵh₂óm*) and treats *ja* as secondary to it; Olander derives both *jȃzъ* and *já* from a single *\*ˈēzu*. This page doesn't need to adjudicate that dispute — all three routes converge on the same PIE root, they just disagree on the internal Slavic-period derivation.

**Real Slavic comparanda**, by branch:

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | азъ (azŭ) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | ја̑ / jȃ |
| South Slavic | Slovene | jȁz |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian | аз (az), яз (jaz) |
| South Slavic | Macedonian | јас (jas) |
| East Slavic | Russian | я (ja); archaic/religious аз (az) |
| East Slavic | Old East Slavic | ꙗзъ (jazŭ), ꙗ (ja) |
| West Slavic | Polish | ja |
| West Slavic | Czech | já |

**Decided (2026-09-19): јаз, matching the conservative outcome that kept the old -zъ ending** — Old Church Slavonic азъ, Bulgarian аз/Macedonian јас, and specifically **Slovene jȁz** — rather than the innovated bare form (standard Serbo-Croatian, Czech, Slovak, Polish) originally used here.

**Why the change:** the word was first filed as bare "ja" simply because that's what real Croatian gave (the user's own words: "I got ja from Croatian"). But [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]] had already established Kajkavian's *closer affinity to Slovenian* than to standard Neo-Shtokavian Serbo-Croatian — and Slovene is exactly the branch that kept the -z. Bare "ja" was therefore pulling against a profile the vault had already committed to; **јаз** pulls with it instead. This is a real instance of the project's own governing method working as intended: a comparative etymological check surfaced a latent inconsistency, and it got fixed rather than left standing because "it came from a real language" — Croatian being real doesn't make it the *right* real source when a closer-matching one (Slovene) was already on record.

**The oblique stem is unaffected.** мене/мени/мном descend from an entirely different PIE stem than the nominative does (the same general nominative/oblique suppletion pattern found across Indo-European 1st-singular pronouns — compare English *I*/*me*, Latin *ego*/*me*), so the nominative's -z-retention question doesn't touch them at all. The specific Wiktionary reconstruction page for *azъ consulted here doesn't itself trace the oblique stem's etymology in detail; that suppletion is separately well-established Indo-European background knowledge, not sourced to this particular citation.

## Register

Pronouns don't carry the Slavic/Dalmatian/Greek lexical stratification that content words do ([[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s three-layer model doesn't apply here) — this is closed-class grammatical vocabulary, not a word that could plausibly have been replaced by a prestige loan. More relevant: per [[../Grammar/Verbal Agreement|Verbal Agreement]]'s canonical pro-drop, јаз is **usually absent** in ordinary speech — person/number agreement on the verb already identifies the subject, so the overt pronoun surfaces mainly for emphasis or contrast ("*јаз* sam to uradio" — "**I** did that," as opposed to someone else).
