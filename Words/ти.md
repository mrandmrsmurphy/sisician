# ти (ti)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Second individual pronoun entry, following [[јаз]]'s lead in [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]]'s now-wikilinked paradigm. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"You" — 2nd person singular, **informal/familiar register**. Real Slavic languages generally distinguish familiar singular *ти* from formal/polite address, which borrows the 2nd person **plural** [[ви]] instead (the same T–V pattern as French *tu/vous*, Russian *ty/vy*) — worth recording here since [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] doesn't yet document this distinction explicitly.

## Paradigm

One cell of the closed paradigm documented in full at [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]]. ти's own forms across the five living cases:

| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| Nominative | ти |
| Genitive | [[тебе]] ([[те]]) |
| Dative | [[теби]] ([[ти]]) |
| Accusative | [[тебе]] ([[те]]) |
| Instrumental | [[тобом]] |

**A real, inherited homophony worth flagging, not a typo:** the dative short/clitic form is **ти** — identical to the nominative. This is the same kind of ambiguity [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] already notes for [[ми]] (1pl nominative / 1sg dative clitic), now showing up a second time in the same paradigm's neighbor. Resolved by context/position in speech, not something to engineer around.

**A real, inherited stem alternation, also not an error:** тебе/теби share the stem **теб-**, but the instrumental **тобом** shifts to **тоб-** — a genuine e~o ablaut alternation within one paradigm, inherited directly from Common Slavic and present in real Croatian exactly this way (*tebe/tebi* vs. *tobom*). Noting it explicitly so it reads as inherited texture, not a future correction target.

## Etymology

**Deep root:** Proto-Slavic **\*ty** "you (sg.)" ← Proto-Balto-Slavic **\*tūˀ** ← PIE **\*túh₂** (cognate with Latin *tū*, Sanskrit *tvam*, and — through a separate Germanic path — English *thou*).

**Real Slavic comparanda**, by branch:

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | тꙑ (ty) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | ти̑ / tȋ |
| South Slavic | Slovene | tȋ |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian | ти (ti) |
| South Slavic | Macedonian | ти (ti) |
| East Slavic | Russian | ты (ty) |
| East Slavic | Ukrainian | ти (ty) |
| West Slavic | Polish | ty |
| West Slavic | Czech | ty |

**Checked for the same kind of split that hit [[јаз]] — found none.** Unlike the 1st singular (where South Slavic split sharply between Slovene's conservative *jaz* and Serbo-Croatian's innovated *ja*), the 2nd singular is remarkably stable across the entire Slavic family. The only variation at all is *y* vs. *i* (OCS/East Slavic/West Slavic *ty* vs. South Slavic *ti*), which is just the general, regular Common-Slavic-to-South-Slavic *y*→*i* vowel merger applying uniformly across the whole lexicon — not a special pronoun-specific isogloss the way *az*/*ja* was. Sisician's **ти** already matches the South Slavic outcome by construction (our 5-vowel inventory has no /y/ at all — see [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]]), so there's nothing here to reconsider. Worth doing the check anyway rather than assuming stability from memory.

## Register

Same as [[јаз]]: closed-class grammatical vocabulary, no Slavic/Dalmatian/Greek stratification ([[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]] doesn't apply). Per [[../Grammar/Verbal Agreement|Verbal Agreement]]'s pro-drop, ти is usually absent in ordinary speech, surfacing mainly for emphasis/contrast. The T–V distinction (§ Meaning above) is the one piece of social/register information this specific pronoun carries that [[јаз]] doesn't — choosing ти over [[ви]] is itself a register decision, familiar vs. formal.

## A Second Word, Same Spelling: ти as Demonstrative Masculine Plural

**A separate word, not a second sense of this pronoun.** [[../Grammar/Demonstrative Pronouns|Grammar/Demonstrative Pronouns]] builds a "this/that" system where the masculine plural of [[тај]] ("that") is also **ти** — continuing Proto-Slavic *\*ti* (masc.nom.pl. of the demonstrative *\*tъ*) directly, an entirely different root from this page's *\*ty* "thou." Same accepted category of coincidence already established for [[../Words/ми|ми]] (1pl "we" / 1sg dative clitic) and this very page's own nominative/dative-clitic homophony above — a third instance, filed here as a note (per the filename-per-citation-form convention) rather than a separate page. See [[тај]] for the demonstrative paradigm this second ти belongs to.
