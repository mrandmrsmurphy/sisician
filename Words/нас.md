# нас (nas)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** The genitive/accusative cell of [[ми]]'s paradigm, and a genuinely *third* distinct history among the genitive/accusative pronoun pairs, not a repeat of either [[мене]]/[[тебе]]'s pattern or [[њега]]'s.

## Meaning — A Third Kind of Genitive/Accusative Story

Fetched the Proto-Slavic *\*my* declension table specifically for its oblique row, and it turned up a real, distinct arrangement worth laying out against the two patterns already established:

1. **[[мене]]/[[тебе]]'s pattern:** a dedicated accusative-only enclitic existed (*\*mę̃*/*\*tę̑*) and **survived** as the modern short clitic ([[ме]]/[[те]]), while genitive's full form stayed untouched.
2. **[[њега]]'s pattern:** no dedicated accusative shape ever existed at all (*\*jь*'s accusative was just its own nominative), so genitive had nothing to compete with and simply took the job outright.
3. **нас's pattern — different from both:** a dedicated accusative-only form *did* exist, **\*ny̑** — but unlike [[мене]]/[[тебе]]'s *\*mę̃*/*\*tę̑*, it **didn't survive into the modern standard language.** Genitive **\*nàsъ** generalized to cover accusative's job as well, the same *outcome* as [[њега]]'s (one form doing both), but reached by **losing** a real competitor rather than by never having one.

**Sisician's [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] table already reflects this exactly**: нас has no parenthesized short/clitic form at all, unlike [[мене]]/[[тебе]]/[[њега]] — because there's no surviving *\*ny*-descended clitic to list. нас itself is short enough to serve both stressed and unstressed contexts, a real, independently-attested modern Slavic outcome, not a gap in this project's coverage.

## Etymology

**Genitive \*nàsъ** — per [[ми]]'s own page, this continues a separate suppletive oblique root from nominative *\*my*, itself not traced to a specific PIE morpheme by the citation used there (an open gap, not filled here either).

**Accusative \*ny̑** — a genuinely distinct short form in Proto-Slavic, confirmed by a direct fetch of its own reconstruction entry, which also states it did *double duty as a short dative* alongside genitive's own separate dative form **\*nàmъ** — meaning Proto-Slavic's plural accusative and dative clitics were **already the same word**, *\*ny*, well before Sisician existed to decide anything about it. This is a real point of contrast with the singular: [[мене]]/[[тебе]] kept accusative and dative clitics *separate* ([[ме]] vs [[ми]], [[те]] vs [[ти]]), but the plural's short accusative/dative pronoun was already unified at the Proto-Slavic stage. Sisician's [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] table keeps genitive/accusative нас separate from dative/instrumental [[нама]] ([[нам]]) — following the modern outcome (below), not the Proto-Slavic short-form system directly.

**What actually happened to \*ny:** it didn't survive into the modern standard South Slavic languages as a living clitic. Instead, genitive **\*nàsъ** generalized outward to cover accusative (and, in most of these languages, locative too) — the real modern outcome, not a Sisician simplification of a still-living Proto-Slavic three-way split.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Genitive/Accusative | Dative |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | насъ (gen.); ны (short acc./dat.) | намъ |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | nas (single form, gen.=acc.=loc.) | nama |
| South Slavic | Slovene | nas | nam |
| East Slavic | Russian | нас (gen.=acc.=loc.) | нам |
| West Slavic | Polish | nas | nam |

**No branch tension found** — every branch checked shows the same generalization (genitive-shaped нас covering accusative), so Sisician's table matches a pan-Slavic outcome, not a South-Slavic-specific choice.

## Register

Same closed-class status as [[ми]]. Since there's no separate clitic, нас itself occupies whatever position [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]]'s template calls for — worth flagging as an open question there (see § Related) rather than assumed identical to [[ме]]/[[те]]/[[га]]/[[је]]'s clitic behavior, since a form that's simultaneously the only stressed and unstressed option may not slot into that template the same way a true short/full pair does.

## Related

- [[ми]] — the nominative this word is suppletive with.
- [[нама]], [[нам]] — the dative/instrumental counterpart, where Proto-Slavic's *\*ny* actually left a living descendant, distinguishing нас from нама far more sharply than the мене/тебе-style genitive/dative distinction does.
- [[мене]], [[тебе]] — structurally different history: their old accusative enclitics survived; нас's own equivalent (*\*ny*) didn't.
- [[њега]] — same modern outcome (one form for genitive and accusative) reached by a different route (never had a competing accusative shape at all, rather than losing one).
- [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]] — where нас's lack of a dedicated clitic form should eventually be addressed explicitly, not yet done.
