# маног (manog)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** The adverb "much, a lot," the one surviving singular form of the adjective behind [[../Words/њоги|њоги]] "many". See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Much, a lot, greatly," modifying verbs and adjectives (*it rains a lot*). Never a determiner: it does not combine with a noun the way њоги does, and the user ruled that њоги is not used with mass nouns, so "much water" is not expressed here yet.

## Why It Exists

[[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s adverb rule makes an adjective's adverb its masculine singular form, unchanged. њоги has only plural forms, so its masculine singular survives only in that adverbial role, the way a lone fossil does, and it is the whole singular paradigm.

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*mъnogъ** (see [[../Words/њоги|њоги]] for the citation). This is the masculine singular, with **two** jers:

1. **Fall of the Jers** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): counting from the end, the final ъ is weak and drops, which makes the first ъ strong, so it becomes **a** (the same alternation as сан/сна): \*mъnogъ → **маног**.
2. The mn- cluster never forms, so [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 14 does not apply here. Compare њоги, the plural, where the only jer is weak and the cluster does form.

The г is word-final, so lenition applies only if a vowel-initial word follows. The result is a real contrast between маног and њоги in the same root, the kind of jer-driven alternation the vault already has in сан/сна.

## Register

Native, unmarked, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer I.
