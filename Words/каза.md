# каза (kaza)

**Status: exploratory/tentative — see note at bottom.** Part of the [[дом]] / [[каза]] / [[скинија]] / [[икос]] set illustrating the [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|three-layer lexical model]] for "home/house." See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page. **This page is the template other `Words/` entries should follow** — see [[../AIOS/memory/reference_word_entry_format|reference_word_entry_format]] for the standing convention it establishes.

## Meaning

Dwelling, building, residence — a more formal, architectural word than [[дом]]. Erudite the way Latinate vocabulary feels elevated in English (*residence* vs. *home*), but purely secular — no religious or theological coloring at all (contrast [[скинија]]).

## Declension (e-declension, feminine)

Following real Croatian e-declension (*žena/žene/ženi/ženu/ženom*) exactly — see [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]] for the case system this reflects.

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | каза /ˈkaza/ | казе /ˈkaze/ |
| Genitive | казе /ˈkaze/ | каза /ˈkaza/ |
| Dative | кази /ˈkazi/ | казама /ˈkazama/ |
| Accusative | казу /ˈkazu/ | казе /ˈkaze/ |
| Instrumental | казом /ˈkazom/ | казама /ˈkazama/ |

Note the syncretisms are inherited directly from real Croatian, not artifacts of this specific word: nom.sg. **каза** is homophonous with gen.pl. **каза**; gen.sg. **казе** is homophonous with nom.pl. and acc.pl. **казе**; dat.pl. and instr.pl. are both **казама** (a third historical syncretism-partner, locative plural, would also have been *казама* had the locative survived).

**Stress: root-anchored** (the general noun rule, decided 2026-09-19 — see [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Modern Inventory]] § Prosody). казама keeps stress on the root каз- (**ˈka.za.ma**) rather than migrating onto the penult (†ka.ˈza.ma) as the case ending lengthens the word — this word was in fact the first evidence for the general rule, since every other form here is two syllables, where root-anchored and mechanical-penultimate stress happen to coincide.

## Etymology

Vulgar Latin/[[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Vulgar Latin]] **casa** "cottage, small house" (in Classical Latin, actually the *humbler* word next to the grander *domus* — that internal Latin register doesn't carry over here; Sisician's register comes entirely from Dalmatian being the elite-prestige source language, not from what the word meant back in Latin). Via [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]: no diphthongization trigger (short *a*, not stressed ē/ō); no intervocalic stop to preserve-or-lenite; the one relevant change is **intervocalic /s/ → [z]**, paralleling real Italian's identical *casa* → [ˈkaza] — see [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § Decided: Intervocalic /s/ Voices to [z]. **casa → kaza**, and Dalmatian hands this form to Sisician essentially unchanged (no further adaptation currently proposed, though this hasn't been rigorously checked against a full Phase 3 chain).

This resolves the placeholder in [[../Grammar/Design Principles|Grammar/Design Principles]] § Do Not Romance-ify the Morphology, which used "kaza" as a hypothetical illustration of Romance nouns taking native Slavic declension — that placeholder is now this actual word, and per [[../Grammar/Declension Classes|Grammar/Declension Classes]] it's confirmed to fall in the **e-declension** — the class most Dalmatian a-final loans are expected to join.

## Register

**Dalmatian, formal/erudite, secular.** Belongs to [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s "Dalmatian Romance" layer — architecture, urban life. Forms a register doublet with [[дом]] the way English *ask/question* or *house/residence* do (see [[../Etymology/Methodology|Etymology/Methodology]] § Romance-Slavic Doublets), without either word being "more correct" — they simply differ in warmth vs. formality. Per [[../Grammar/Word Order and Register|Word Order and Register]], expect каза to appear more often in freer-word-order, fronted-for-emphasis constructions than [[дом]] does, since that syntactic register is itself coded as Dalmatian/erudite.
