# икос (ikos)

**Status: exploratory/proposed — see note at bottom.** The fourth member of the "home/house" worked example alongside [[дом]], [[каза]], and [[скинија]] — see [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]] for the three-layer model this illustrates, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

Household, community — specifically the community-of-the-faithful sense, not a building and not (directly) the mystical presence-of-God sense that [[скинија]] carries. The New Testament grounding: **Ephesians 2:19**, "members of the household of God" (Greek οἰκεῖοι τοῦ θεοῦ), and **1 Timothy 3:15**, "the household of God, which is the church of the living God." Where [[скинија]] is about God dwelling *with* people, **икос** is about people belonging *to* one another and to God — a family/congregation sense.

## Etymology

Greek **οἶκος** (*oikos*) "house, household" — note this is a genuinely different path from [[скинија]]'s. There is no clean, directly-attested Church Slavonic loan of οἶκος in this "household/community" sense to build on the way скиния exists for σκηνή: Slavic already had strong native vocabulary for "house/household" (домъ and its derivatives), so OCS translation practice generally calqued rather than borrowed here. (The one real, directly-attested Byzantine/Church-Slavonic borrowing of this exact word, "икос," is a narrow technical term in Orthodox hymnography for a specific type of hymn-stanza in the Akathist/Kontakion structure — a different, narrower sense than "household/community.")

Given that gap, the proposed path is **direct from Greek, not routed through OCS** — legitimate because [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]] already distinguishes "Greek-derived" from "Church-Slavonic-derived" domains as separate channels, and [[../Culture/History|Culture/History]] § 1167 (the Byzantine tax-farming period) and [[../Culture/Religion|Culture/Religion]] both establish direct Greek contact independent of the Church Slavonic liturgical pipeline. By the relevant period, Byzantine Greek had undergone iotacism (οι had monophthongized), so **οἶκος** would have been pronounced roughly **[ˈikos]** — which happens to match the real Church-Slavonic "икос" spelling exactly, even though that word's attested sense is the narrower hymnographic one rather than this one. Proposed: **οἶκος [ikos] → Sisician икос**, entering with minimal phonological change (all four sounds — и, к, о, с — are already native Sisician phonemes, nothing to adapt), the same "resists reshaping" pattern [[скинија]] shows for prestige Greek-layer loans.

## Register

**Greek, direct (not via Church Slavonic), theological-communal.** A fourth register alongside [[дом]] (warm/native), [[каза]] (formal/secular), and [[скинија]] (mystical-presence/theological) — this one is about belonging rather than either coziness, architecture, or divine presence.

## Related

---

**Note on status:** the most speculative of the four so far, more so even than [[скинија]]. Unlike скинија (which has a clean real OCS precedent, скиния, in essentially the same sense), икос's real precedent (Byzantine hymnographic "икос") is in a *different* sense than the one wanted here — the "household/community" meaning is a proposed semantic choice built on the real Greek word's own NT usage, not on an attested Slavic loan carrying that sense already. Flagging this gap explicitly rather than presenting it as equally solid.
