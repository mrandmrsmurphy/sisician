# она (ona)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Fifth individual pronoun entry, the feminine counterpart to [[он]]. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — the Contrast With он Is the Point

"She" — and, unlike [[он]], **она does *not* absorb any old-neuter referents in the singular.** [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger sends old-neuter-singular nouns to *masculine* agreement, not feminine — so она stays purely personal/feminine in the singular, while [[он]] alone carries the double "he/it" burden. The two pronouns aren't symmetric, and that asymmetry is a direct, visible consequence of a design choice made three pages ago, not an accident.

## Etymology

**Same paradigm as [[он]], not a separate root.** Per the Proto-Slavic *onъ reconstruction (consulted 2026-09-19 for [[он]]), masculine *onъ, feminine ***\*ona***, and neuter *ono are explicitly documented as one shared demonstrative paradigm — all descend together from the same PIE demonstrative root ultimately behind [[он]]'s *onъ. она isn't a separate coinage; it's the feminine agreement-form of the identical pronoun.

**Oblique stem ње/њој/њу/њом** comes from the same displaced anaphoric pronoun *jь as [[он]]'s oblique forms, via the identical prothetic-n mechanism already explained on [[он]]'s page (prepositions ending in *-n* resegmented onto the pronoun, then generalized to all oblique contexts) — not re-derived here, since it's the same historical process applying across genders, not a feminine-specific development.

**A real ambiguity Sisician's own design happens to avoid, worth noting explicitly:** real Croatian's feminine singular nominative *ona* is **homophonous with the neuter plural nominative**, also *ona* — a genuine, attested source of ambiguity in Croatian, usually resolved only by verb agreement (singular vs. plural). [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s decision to send old-neuter-*plural* referents to **[[оне]]** (feminine plural) rather than to она itself means Sisician never inherits this specific collision — a real design payoff from a decision made for unrelated reasons (the masculine-singular/feminine-plural split), not something engineered on purpose to dodge this problem, but worth recording as a benefit that fell out of it.

**Comparative aside, flagged as recalled rather than freshly verified this session:** most Slavic languages keep the *on/ona/ono* series for 3rd person, but Bulgarian's modern colloquial system (той/тя/то) draws on a different, though related, demonstrative stem (the *tъ*/*ta* "that" series) rather than continuing *onъ/ona/ono* directly. Not confirmed against a citation this time — noting it as a plausible comparative fact to verify later rather than asserting it with the same confidence as the cited material above.

## Register

Same as [[он]]: closed-class, no lexical stratification, pro-drop-eligible but needing more restatement than 1st/2nd person in practice. No T–V interaction (that's 2nd person only).
