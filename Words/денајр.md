# денајр (denajr)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** The word that actually takes over Old English *feoh*'s displaced slot — see Etymology below and [[../Words/монајта|монајта]] for the narrower, concrete "coin/cash" word coined alongside it. See [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] § "Decided: /rj/ Metathesizes to a Diphthong" for the sound-change this word introduces, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Money" — the general, abstract sense (wealth, currency as a concept), distinct from [[../Words/монајта|монајта]]'s concrete "a coin/cash." Real cross-Romance precedent for exactly this "specific coin denomination → money in general" broadening: Spanish *dinero*, Portuguese *dinheiro*, Italian *denaro* all independently show it, from the same Latin DENARIUS (a specific Roman silver coin, originally worth ten *asses* — from *dēnī* "ten each" + *-ārius*). Not invented for Sisician; inherited as an already-real semantic shift.

**The sole modern word.** The native candidate it displaces, **\*skotъ**, is real Proto-Slavic for "cattle" (Croatian/Russian/Polish *skot*) — but real Old Novgorod dialect and Old Polish independently show it *also* meaning "money, tribute, tax" (via Old Norse contact reinforcing an already-live cattle-as-wealth sense), a genuine, cited, real secondary meaning, not invented for the parallel below. Not built as its own `Words/` page here — the plain "cattle" sense would need a separate word if one is ever wanted (real Common Slavic has other candidates, e.g. *govędo*), not resolved on this page.

## The Real English Parallel

Old English **feoh** meant "livestock, cattle; movable property; possessions in livestock, goods, or money; riches, treasure, wealth" — from Proto-Germanic *fehu*, ultimately PIE *\*peku-* "cattle," the same root behind Latin *pecū* "cattle" and *pecunia* "money, property" (Cicero himself still felt *pecunia* as literally "wealth in cattle"). *Feoh* was fully displaced as a word by Latin-derived **money** (via Anglo-Norman); *fee* survives today, but as a separate borrowing of a Germanic *cognate* back through French (*fief*), not as *feoh*'s own direct continuation. **The Sisician parallel is unusually exact**: \*skotъ shows the identical PIE *cattle → wealth/money* semantic path (independently — Proto-Slavic *\*skotъ* and Germanic *\*fehu* are not the same root, just the same cross-linguistically common metaphor, cattle being humanity's original portable wealth almost everywhere livestock was kept), and, per this page, gets displaced by a Dalmatian loanword the same way *feoh* was.

## Declension (a-declension, masculine, hard stem)

Following [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s hard-stem a-declension pattern — **the first bisyllabic (non-monosyllabic) masculine a-declension word built**, which matters directly for [[../Grammar/Declension Classes|Declension Classes]]'s own long-flagged open question about the *-ов-* plural extension's exact conditioning (previously confirmed only for monosyllabic roots, via дом itself). **Resolved here, tentatively**: денајр *does* take the extension despite being bisyllabic — real Croatian generally extends most masculine nouns this way regardless of syllable count (monosyllabic roots aren't the only trigger, just the one already tested) — so "monosyllabic" is provisionally widened to "the default, unless a specific reason blocks it," rather than narrowed further. Flagged as a real but tentative resolution, not a fully independent citation.

## Etymology

Latin **DENARIUS**, accusative **DENARIUM**, stressed **dē-NĀ-ri-um** (antepenult — Classical Latin's own rule: the penult *-ri-* is light, short *i*, open syllable, so stress falls one syllable further back, on the long *ā*). Drop final -M (general Vulgar Latin): **denariu**, unchanged stress.

**The new step, and the answer to "can we do anything fun with the -rj-": glide metathesis, not a new consonant.** The unstressed *-ri-u* sequence is a real consonant+yod cluster (/rj/, from intervocalic *i* + following vowel). Rather than palatalizing /r/ into some new sound — real Romance data shows /r/ resists that, unlike /n l s z t k d g/ — Vulgar Latin's own real, documented development is **glide metathesis**: /rj/ → transitional [rʲ] → the glide jumps *before* the /r/ instead of after it, giving [i̯r]. Real, cited example of exactly this mechanism: Classical Latin **PARIA** (neuter plural of *par* "equal") → Vulgar Latin *parʲa* → **[pai̯ra]** → Old French **paire** "pair." Applying the identical mechanism to *denariu*: the yod jumps from after the /r/ to before it, landing on the preceding stressed vowel and forming a diphthong — **de-NA-ri-u → de-NAI-ru**.

Dalmatian's own already-established final-vowel apocope (real, general Vulgar-Latin/Dalmatian tendency, already implicit in [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s *mōrte→mwart*, where the unstressed final *-e* simply drops) removes the remaining unstressed final *-u*: **denairu → denajr** (writing the diphthong's own /j/-offglide with Sisician's established **ј**, per [[../Words/тај|тај]]'s own [aj]-type spelling precedent (matching real Serbian Cyrillic conventions like *мај*/*крај*, not yet independently built as `Words/` pages here), rather than a vowel-hiatus "ai"). No further Dalmatian-internal change needed — masculine gender (DENARIUS is masculine, matching real Italian *denaro*, Spanish *dinero*, Portuguese *dinheiro*) lands directly on Sisician's own a-declension masculine class, and the consonant-final shape (денајр) needs no further adaptation.

**Evidentiary honesty**: the /rj/-metathesis mechanism itself is real and well-cited (a documented Vulgar Latin process, not invented for this word). The specific application to *denariu*, the final-apocope step, and the -ов- plural-extension resolution are this page's own proposed extensions of already-established or newly-cited general mechanisms — reasonable, motivated, but not independently attested for this exact word the way *paire* is attested for *paria*.
