# ви (vi)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Seventh individual pronoun entry. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — One Word, Two Real Functions (Not a Coincidence, Unlike [[ми]])

ви does two jobs, but **this isn't a homophone collision the way [[ми]]'s two senses were.** It's a single grammatical item carrying a real, well-documented, cross-linguistically common extension:

1. **Genuine 2nd person plural, "you all."**
2. **Formal/polite singular address** — using the plural to address one respected individual. This is the T–V distinction already fully documented at [[../Culture/Formal Address (ти vs ви)|Culture/Formal Address]] and [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] § T–V Distinction: ви is the formal counterpart to [[ти]]'s familiar singular, the same plural-for-polite-singular pattern as French *vous*, Russian *вы*, and (via a different route, 3rd-plural rather than 2nd) German *Sie*.

**Both functions are the same word, not two words that happen to look alike** — this is the actual, single most important thing to keep straight about ви versus [[ми]]'s situation.

## Etymology

**Deep root:** PIE **\*yúHs** ← Proto-Balto-Slavic **\*jūˀs** ← Proto-Slavic **\*vy**. Worth flagging a genuinely different historical mechanism than every pronoun built so far: per this fetch, Proto-Balto-Slavic *\*wōns* (the immediate ancestor of *vy*) was itself originally the **accusative** case-form of *\*jūˀs*, not a separate demonstrative root the way [[он]]'s *onъ*/*jь* split was, or a separate ancient PIE suppletive stem the way [[јаз]]'s *ja*/*mene* was. ви's own history is a **case-leveling event**: an oblique (accusative) form generalized to take over the nominative slot, within Balto-Slavic specifically — a different *kind* of irregularity-origin than any pronoun documented so far, not just another instance of the same pattern.

**The oblique stem is, once again, a separate suppletive root:** вас/вама continue Proto-Slavic **\*vasъ**/**\*vamъ**/**\*vami**, distinct from ви itself — the same general nominative/oblique split shown by every pronoun in this set, even though ви's *own* nominative has the unusual accusative-generalization history just described.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | вꙑ (vy) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | vȋ |
| South Slavic | Slovene | vȋ |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian, Macedonian | vie (real innovation — see below) |
| East Slavic | Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian | вы / ви (vy) |
| West Slavic | Polish, Czech, Slovak | wy / vy |

**Checked for branch tension — found the same East-South-Slavic split as [[ми]], and it's just as irrelevant here.** Bulgarian and Macedonian innovated *vie*, structurally the same move as their *ние* replacement of *my* — a real, symmetric pattern across both 1st and 2nd plural in that branch specifically. It doesn't touch Serbo-Croatian or Slovene (both *vȋ*), so Sisician's Kajkavian-based ви needs no reconsideration, same as [[ми]].

## Register — the Formal Half of the T–V System

**See [[../Culture/Formal Address (ти vs ви)|Culture/Formal Address (ти vs. ви)]] for the full social treatment** — the default with strangers (answering the phone, the door, any formal introduction), used until an invitation to switch to [[ти]] is extended, modeled on German *Sie*/*du* practice, including the proposed wine-sharing invitation ritual. This page just establishes the word itself; the social rule governing *when* to use it lives on that page.
