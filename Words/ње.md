# ње (nje)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Sixteenth individual pronoun entry — the feminine genitive cell of [[она]]'s paradigm, and the first pronoun page in this whole set where genitive and accusative are **not** the same word.

## Meaning — Where the Feminine Paradigm Genuinely Diverges from Masculine

Every oblique pronoun built so far — [[мене]]/[[тебе]]/[[њега]] — has had genitive and accusative sharing one form. **ње breaks that pattern, and it's not a Sisician-specific choice: it directly continues a real Proto-Slavic distinction.** Fetched the *jь* declension table specifically for its feminine singular row (having already used the masculine/neuter and plural rows for [[он]]/[[она]]/[[они]]/[[оне]]/[[њега]]) and found genitive **\*jeję̇** and accusative **\*jǫ** listed as two genuinely separate forms — unlike masculine, where accusative was simply identical to nominative *\*jь*. So the masculine paradigm's genitive-absorbing-accusative story ([[њега]]'s page) was never available to the feminine paradigm in the first place: there was no third slot for genitive to expand into, because feminine accusative already had its own distinct shape from the start. ње (genitive) and [[њу]] (accusative) staying separate in Sisician isn't an asymmetry invented for variety — it's the default outcome of just not disturbing what Proto-Slavic already kept apart.

## Etymology

**The prothetic-n mechanism is [[он]]'s own** (see that page), applied here to the feminine oblique stem the same way [[она]]'s page already applied it without re-deriving it. This page's contribution is the case-specific detail: **\*jeję̇** (genitive), with prothetic n-, is ње's direct ancestor — a separate formation from masculine *\*jego*, not a gendered spelling variant of the same ending.

**A genuinely interesting connection, worth stating explicitly:** the ending underlying *\*jeję̇* — a pronominal/demonstrative genitive singular feminine formation, historically related to the ending seen in Slavic demonstrative *\*toję* ("of that," fem.) — is the same family of pronominal ending that [[../Grammar/Definiteness|Definiteness]] and [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] already say got fused onto adjectives to build the long/definite declension. [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s own table already has a feminine genitive singular ending, **нуве** (renamed 2026-09-20 from нове, see [[../Words/нув|Words/нув]]) — not a coincidence of spelling, but the *same historical ending family* ње itself continues, showing up in two different paradigms (pronominal and adjectival) for the structurally identical reason: Slavic's adjective genitive singular feminine ending *is*, historically, this same demonstrative pronoun's genitive ending, suffixed onto the adjective stem. This is general comparative-Slavic morphological knowledge, not sourced to the *jь* citation specifically, but it's a real, well-established fact connecting two pages that were built independently of each other.

**Real Slavic comparanda (genitive):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | неѩ (neję) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | nje |
| South Slavic | Slovene | nje |
| East Slavic | Russian | её (jejo) |
| West Slavic | Polish | jej |

**No branch tension found** — every branch keeps a *jeję*-type reflex distinct from its own accusative reflex, so the genitive/accusative split isn't a South-Slavic-specific feature either; it's pan-Slavic.

## Register

Same closed-class status as [[она]]. Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], full ње is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; [[је]] takes the ordinary second-position clitic slot otherwise — noting the same real, inherited homophony already flagged elsewhere in this paradigm: short genitive **је** is not distinct in shape from [[Biti (To Be)|biti]]'s own 3sg present clitic је ("is"), a genuine ambiguity resolved by syntactic position, the same category of fact as [[ми]]/[[ти]]'s earlier-flagged homophonies.
