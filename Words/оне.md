# оне (one)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Ninth individual pronoun entry, the feminine counterpart to [[они]] — and, with this word filed, the third-person pronoun set (он/она/они/оне) is now complete. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Where the Neuter Merger *Actually* Finishes Landing

"They" (feminine referents) — but exactly parallel to how [[он]] is the true landing site for old-neuter-*singular* reference, **оне is the true landing site for old-neuter-*plural* reference**, not [[они]]. [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger sends old-neuter-plural nouns to feminine agreement (matching the real Romanian pattern this project deliberately follows), so any formerly-neuter noun, once pluralized, takes оне as its pronoun — not они. This was already flagged as an open consequence on both [[он]]'s and [[они]]'s pages; оне is where it's finally made concrete rather than just promised.

**A real, attested collision this design specifically avoids** was already noted on [[она]]'s page: real Croatian's feminine singular *ona* and neuter plural *ona* are homophonous. Sisician's neuter-plural referents go to оне (distinct from она) instead, so that particular ambiguity never arises here — a benefit that fell out of the masculine-singular/feminine-plural split made for unrelated reasons, not something engineered to dodge this specific problem.

## Paradigm

| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| Nominative | оне |
| Genitive | [[њех]] |
| Dative | [[њима]] ([[им]]) |
| Accusative | [[њех]] |

Genitive and accusative use the innovated feminine-specific **ЊЕХ** (see [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] § Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate) — the one place оне's paradigm actually diverges from [[они]]'s. Dative and instrumental remain the shared, ungendered ЊИМА.

## Etymology

**Same root as [[они]], not a separate coinage — this is the fem.pl. cell of the identical *onъ demonstrative paradigm.** Per the Proto-Slavic *onъ reconstruction's own declension table (fetched for [[они]]'s build and consulted again here), the plural nominative row gives **masc. \*oni** vs. **fem. \*ony** as two distinct, already-differentiated forms sitting side by side in Proto-Slavic itself — exactly mirroring how [[он]]/[[она]]'s singular *onъ/\*ona split was never something invented for this project.

**A genuine surface complication, flagged rather than smoothed over:** the raw reconstructed form is *\*ony* — with a **-y**, not the **-e** that оне's spelling shows. This isn't an error; it reflects a real, well-established Slavic development, general historical-linguistics knowledge rather than something confirmed by this specific citation: the old pronominal/adjectival plural ending *-y regularly merges with *-ě (yat) in this exact morphological slot across most of South Slavic, surfacing as **-e** in ekavian-type reflexes. This is the *same* development already baked into [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s hard-declension feminine plural ending (**нуве** as of the 2026-09-20 нов→нув rename, but -е either way — not *novy*/*nuvy*) — оне's **-е** and нуве's **-е** are the same historical merger showing up in two different paradigms (pronominal and adjectival), not two coincidentally similar endings. Real attested Serbo-Croatian and Slovene both show exactly this: modern *one* (fem.pl. "they"), not *ony*.

**Oblique stem њех/њима** — dative/instrumental њима is the plural of the same *jь-derived oblique stem already established for [[они]] (see that page's etymology for the *jixъ/*jimъ citation); ЊЕХ specifically is Sisician's own innovation on top of that inherited material, not a separate historical form — already fully derived on [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] via the ЊЕ:ЊЕГА::ЊЕХ:ЊИХ proportional analogy, not re-derived here.

**Real Slavic comparanda (nominative):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | оны (ony) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | òne |
| South Slavic | Slovene | óne |
| East Slavic | Russian | they merged into a single genderless они |
| West Slavic | Polish, Czech, Slovak | largely merged/simplified, no living fem.pl. reflex parallel to оне |

**No branch tension found for South Slavic itself** — Serbo-Croatian and Slovene both keep the masculine/feminine plural nominative distinction (*oni*/*one*) that Sisician's они/оне pair continues directly; the merger visible in East Slavic and most of West Slavic is a separate, later simplification that doesn't touch Sisician's Kajkavian-based branch.

## Register

Same closed-class status as every pronoun so far. Per [[../Grammar/Verbal Agreement|Verbal Agreement]]'s pro-drop, оне can be omitted once a referent is established, but — as already noted on [[он]]/[[они]]'s pages — 3rd-person reference needs restating more often than 1st/2nd in ordinary discourse. No T–V interaction; that system is 2nd person only.
