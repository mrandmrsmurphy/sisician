# насуштни (nasushtni)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Daily" — the [[../Texts/Pater Noster|Pater Noster]]'s own adjective: **хлеб наш насуштни** "our daily bread." A genuine Layer III artifact: a Church Slavonic compound borrowed whole as a liturgical adjective. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Daily" — but specifically the liturgical, epiousios sense: bread "needful for being," not merely "everyday." The Slavonic word it descends from is itself a calque of Greek **ἐπιούσιος** (the famously untranslatable adjective of the Lord's Prayer), built as на- + сѫщь ("being," present participle of сѫти "to be") + -ьн-: roughly "subsistent, pertaining to sustenance." Sisician's vernacular "everyday" (a native formation, not yet coined) is a different, plainer word; насуштни is the prayer's word.

## Forms

Gender and number agreement only, like every modern adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | насуштни | насуштна |
| Plural | насуштни | насуштне |

**The masculine singular keeps the Slavonic -и ending whole** — a learned loan that entered as a unit and was never re-analyzed through the native short-form machinery (which would have produced a mobile-a shape, тежак-type). Real BCS has the same kind of frozen -ни adjectives in this register (*dnevni*, *svakodnevni*). Consequence, flagged: **masculine singular and masculine plural are identical** (насуштни). No case, no definiteness, per the modern system.

## Etymology

**Old Church Slavonic насѫщьнъıи** — the prayer's own word (хлѣбъ нашъ насѫщьнъıи). **Nativization, four steps:**

1. **ѫ→u** ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 2): насѫщьнъıи → насущьнъıи.
2. **Jers drop** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): насущьнъıи → насущнъıи → the suffixal ь and ъ both weak.
3. **-ъıи → -и**: the hard adjective ending reduces to the modern -и.
4. **щ → шт**: the modern inventory has **no щ phoneme** ([[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Modern Inventory]]). Real Serbian renders Church Slavonic щ as шт in exactly this kind of learned loan (свѧщеникъ → *свештеник*), and the same treatment gives **насуштни**. (The alternative, шч — matching [[../Words/сишчани|сишчани]]'s own native cluster — was considered and set aside in favor of the real Slavonic-loan treatment.)

**Flagged:** the form approved in the design conversation was "насущни," before the щ question was checked against the inventory; насуштни is the nativized outcome, recorded here with the deviation made explicit.

## Register

**church-slavonic-liturgical**, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer III — the prayer's clearest single example of the "Slavonic/Greek for heaven" channel, a word that exists *only* because the liturgy needed it.
