# га (ga)

## Meaning

The unstressed second-position clitic covering both genitive and accusative, exactly as [[њега]]'s page already established — see that page for why the syncretism itself (genitive doing accusative's job, via the general Slavic animate-accusative-uses-genitive rule) doesn't need re-deriving here.

## Etymology — An Honest Account of What Wasn't Found, Not Just What Was

**[[њега]]'s page already flagged that га's precise derivation is the weakest-sourced claim in the pronoun set.** Rather than let that stand as a vague admission, this page went back and fetched further, specifically to see if a clean account exists anywhere reachable — and it mostly still doesn't, but turned up one genuinely useful new fact worth recording.

**New fact, directly quoted:** the Old Church Slavonic masculine singular accusative wasn't a single form — it had **two**, *и* (i, the bare continuation of *\*jь*, identical to the nominative) **and *нь* (nĭ)**, listed as alternates in the same declension table. **нь is real, direct textual evidence of the prothetic-n mechanism operating on the accusative specifically** — the same *-n* resegmentation already cited on [[он]]'s page for the oblique cases generally, caught here in its accusative-specific form rather than just asserted to apply everywhere by extension. Genitive in that same table is a single form, **его** (jego), with no separate short/clitic alternate shown alongside it.

**Why this doesn't cleanly solve га's derivation:** neither OCS accusative variant, *и* nor *нь*, is an obvious phonological ancestor of modern **га** — there's no regular sound change that turns either one into *ga*. This is a real, structural difference from [[мене]]/[[тебе]]'s situation: those short forms ([[ме]]/[[те]]) descend cleanly from a *dedicated accusative enclitic* (*\*mę̃*/*\*tę̑*) via a well-understood regular sound change (ę-denasalization). No comparably clean path from any attested OCS 3rd-person short form to **га** turned up here. The most likely real account — general comparative Slavic handbooks (not accessed directly this session) — is that **га** continues a reduced/clipped form of genitive **его** itself (loss of the unstressed initial syllable, *(j)ego* → *go*, further shifting to *ga*), which would make га, unlike ме/те, a genitive-side form all along, consistent with [[њега]]'s finding that genitive is what's doing the structural work in this paradigm. But that specific vowel-shift step (*o* → *a* in this clitic) is **not confirmed by any citation reached in this session** — flagging it as a plausible, structurally-consistent hypothesis, not an established fact.

**Real Slavic comparanda:**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | и / нь (accusative only; genitive его shows no distinct short form in the table consulted) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | ga (genitive and accusative clitic, both) |
| South Slavic | Slovene | ga |
| East Slavic | Russian | no comparable short clitic in the standard language (full его/него used) |

## Register — Clitic Placement

Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], га is a true second-position (Wackernagel) clitic, exactly like [[ме]]/[[те]] — not an informal variant of [[њега]]. Full њега is required after a preposition and for contrastive emphasis; га takes the ordinary second-position slot otherwise.
