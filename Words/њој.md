## Etymology — An Honest Gap at the Vowel Level, Not Just the Whole-Word Level

**The prothetic-n mechanism is [[он]]'s own** (see that page), applied here the same way [[она]]'s and [[ње]]'s pages already applied it to the feminine oblique stem.

**Fetched the *jь* feminine singular declension (already used for [[ње]]'s build) for its dative cell specifically:** it gives **\*jeji**, and — checked directly against real attested Old Church Slavonic text via the same "third personal pronoun" table already used for [[му]]'s build — OCS's own actual spelling is **ѥи** (jei), confirming the reconstruction rather than contradicting it.

**Neither of those forms obviously becomes њој by a documented regular sound change, and this page says so rather than papering over it.** *\*jeji*/*jei* has an *e*-vowel throughout; real modern Serbo-Croatian **njoj** has an *o*-vowel instead. Three separate fetches (this word's own Wiktionary entry, the *jь* reconstruction's usage notes, and a direct question about a *-eji→-oj* sound law) turned up no source explaining that vowel shift. This is a **genuine, real branch split worth stating as evidence rather than glossing over:** Slovene's own reflex is **njej** — an *e*-vowel outcome, much closer to what *\*jeji* would predict — while Serbo-Croatian specifically shows the innovated *o*. Sisician's [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] table already commits to the Serbo-Croatian-style њој (matching the already-chosen [[њом]] instrumental, which shows the same *o*), so this page inherits that decision rather than reopening it — but the *o*'s specific historical origin remains an open question, not a solved one.

**A plausible (not cited) contributing factor, worth naming without overclaiming it as an answer:** dative *\*jeji* and instrumental *\*jejǫ* are the two cells that ended up *o*-colored in the modern outcome (њој, њом), while genitive [[ње]] and accusative [[њу]] didn't — raising the possibility of analogical leveling *between* dative and instrumental specifically, rather than a regular phonological rule affecting the vowel on its own. This is offered as a reasonable hypothesis given the pattern, not a documented mechanism the way [[он]]'s prothetic-n citation is.

**Real Slavic comparanda (dative):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | ѥи (jei) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | njoj |
| South Slavic | Slovene | njej |
| East Slavic | Russian | ей (jej) |
| West Slavic | Polish | jej |

**A real branch tension, not resolved by picking a side, just acknowledged:** Slovene's *njej* and Russian/Polish's *jej* all preserve the expected *e*-vowel; Serbo-Croatian's *njoj* is the outlier. Sisician follows the Serbo-Croatian outlier (matching the vault's general Kajkavian/Serbo-Croatian lean elsewhere), but unlike [[јаз]]'s resolved *az/ja* tension, this one isn't being resolved by an argued case for which branch fits better — it's simply the form already on record in [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table, flagged now rather than silently assumed correct.
