# друж (druž)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** "Other," filling [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 21, from the Proto-Slavic adjective \*drugъ at the user's direction, with the user's own softening of the final г to **ж**. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"Other, another, different": **друга каза** "another building," **други домови** "other houses." Real Slavic \*drugъ also means "second" and, as a noun, "friend, companion"; Sisician takes only "other" here. The noun "friend" and the ordinal "second" aren't coined.

**Adverbial use: the masculine singular, unchanged**, per [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § Adjective to Adverb: **друж** is also "otherwise, differently."

## Forms

Gender and number agreement only, like every adjective ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | друж | друга |
| Plural | други | друге |

**Only the masculine singular has ж.** In every other form the г stands between vowels, so it lenites to [ɣ] ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 12): /ˈdruɣa/, /ˈdruɣi/, /ˈdruɣe/. The result is a consonant alternation inside the paradigm, **ж ~ г**, of the same kind as the vowel alternation in сан/сна. No case and no definiteness. A noun that is old-neuter-plural takes the feminine plural, by the neuter merger. Stress is root-anchored on the first syllable, **дру-**, throughout.

## Etymology

**Proto-Slavic \*drȗgъ**, an adjective "other, second" and a noun "friend, companion," from Proto-Balto-Slavic \*draugás and PIE \*dʰrewgʰ- "to accompany, be loyal" ([Wiktionary \*drugъ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/drugъ)). Descendants listed there: OCS дроугъ, Serbo-Croatian дру̑г (noun) and дру̏гӣ (adjective), Slovene drȗg "other" and drūgi "second."

**Sisician derivation:**

1. **Fall of the Jers** ([[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]): the only jer is word-final and weak, so it drops: \*drugъ → *друг*, with no mobile *a* needed.
2. **The stem alternant \*druž-.** The same page lists the vocative singular \***druže** and the derived \***družiti** "to be friends" and \***družina** "company." The g-to-ž change is the first palatalization before a front vowel; the page does not name it, but it fits. So the root already has a real, inherited **druž-** form next to **drug-**.
3. **Leveling, a design choice.** The palatalized stem **druž-** is generalized into the one cell where the stem stands bare, the masculine singular, giving **друж**. Everywhere else the plain stem **друг-** is kept. This step is not cited: no Slavic language does it, and a vocative-based form replacing the nominative is unusual. I chose it because it lets the user's softening rest on a real alternation and not on a new sound law that would also change [[../Words/маног|маног]], the only other г-final word.

**Spelling note, g stays elsewhere.** I'm assuming the plural \*drugi stays g and doesn't show the second-palatalization alternation (\*drudzi), the same leveling choice made for [[../Words/њоги|њоги]]. That is a choice, not a citation.

## Register

Native, unmarked, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer I.

## Open Points

- **The leveling story** (step 3) is a proposal; if you want a more natural route, a categorical word-final г → ж rule was considered and set aside because it would change маног.
- **The noun друг "friend" and the ordinal "second"** aren't coined; the noun would not share this ж form if the leveling is adjective-only.
- **The comparative** and **"other" as a pronoun** ("the other one") aren't built; the article would carry the substantivized use ([[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]] § Substantivized Adjectives).
