# мене (mene)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** Tenth individual pronoun entry, and the first oblique-case pronoun page — every entry before this one has been a nominative. See [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Grammar/Personal Pronouns]] for the full paradigm and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning — Genitive and Accusative, Genuinely Syncretic

мене covers **two cases at once**, and this isn't an approximation — [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table already shows genitive and accusative sharing the identical form мене (short form [[ме]]) for every singular pronoun, not just this one. This is real, inherited syncretism, not a Sisician-specific collapse: the Proto-Slavic declension table itself (fetched below) already lists a single **\*mȅne** for genitive with no separate accusative-genitive distinction at that slot, alongside a *third*, genuinely different accusative-only form, ***mę̃*** — which Sisician doesn't inherit at all (see § A Form Not Carried Forward, below).

## Etymology

**A separate suppletive stem from the nominative, exactly as [[јаз]]'s own page already flagged rather than assumed.** Fetched the full Proto-Slavic *\*azъ* declension table specifically to check what it says about the oblique forms, since [[јаз]]'s page had explicitly noted the etymology section there covers only the nominative *az-/ja-* stem and leaves the oblique men-/mъn- stem unexplained. Confirmed: that page's etymology section still only traces the nominative; **it doesn't derive мене's own root**. So the account below is general Indo-European background knowledge, not sourced to that specific citation — flagged honestly, the same practice used throughout this project.

**The oblique stem \*men-** continues PIE's own separate oblique root for "I," conventionally reconstructed as **\*me-** (contrasting with the nominative's *\*h₁eǵ(h₂)*) — the same ancient nominative/oblique suppletion pattern visible across Indo-European generally: Latin *ego*/*mē*, Greek *ἐγώ*/*ἐμέ*, Sanskrit *aham*/*mā*, English *I*/*me*. Slavic's *mene* specifically extends that bare *\*me-* with a further suffix (the *-ne* seen also in the dative *\*mьně*), a real and old formation but not one this project has traced to a specific PIE source morpheme — left as an open gap rather than a guessed answer.

**A form not carried forward: \*mę̃.** The fetched declension table shows Proto-Slavic actually had a *third*, distinct accusative-only enclitic, **\*mę̃** (nasal vowel, the direct source of Old Church Slavonic **мѧ** and modern Serbo-Croatian short accusative *me* as a separate form from genitive *mene*) — genuinely different from both мене and its own short form ме. Sisician's paradigm, per [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s table, doesn't preserve this third form: accusative simply reuses genitive мене/ме rather than keeping *mę̃* alive as a separate accusative-specific shape. This is a real simplification relative to the reconstructed Proto-Slavic system (and relative to Old Church Slavonic, which kept *мѧ* distinct) — flagged explicitly as a design point rather than presented as if Sisician just mechanically inherited the modern Serbo-Croatian outcome, since Serbo-Croatian *did* keep a formally separate accusative clitic (*me*) alongside genitive *mene*, distinguished at least in the short/clitic form even where the full forms coincide.

**Real Slavic comparanda (genitive/accusative full form):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | мене (mene, gen.); мѧ (mę, short acc.) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | mène (gen./acc.); short acc. clitic me, distinct from genitive clitic |
| South Slavic | Slovene | mêne |
| East Slavic | Russian | меня (menja) |
| West Slavic | Polish, Czech | mnie / mě |

**No branch tension found** — every South Slavic language keeps a reflex of *mene* for the genitive/accusative full form; the only real point of variation is exactly the short-clitic question already flagged above (whether accusative keeps its own *mę-derived* clitic or merges with genitive's), not a nominative-style branch split like [[јаз]]'s.

## Register

Same closed-class status as [[јаз]]. Per [[../Grammar/Clitic Ordering|Clitic Ordering]], the short form [[ме]] is a true second-position clitic, not just an unstressed spelling variant of мене — see that page for placement rules. The full/stressed form мене is used for emphasis or contrast, or wherever clitic placement rules block a second-position clitic (e.g. after a preposition — real Slavic generally requires the full form there, never the clitic).
