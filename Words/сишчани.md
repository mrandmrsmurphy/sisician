# сишчани (siščani) — the people

**Status: exploratory/tentative — the second name derived from Sisician's own sound-change chain, and a real correction to the user's own guessed *Сисачани*.** Answers [[../Culture/Founding Myth and Naming|Culture/Founding Myth and Naming]] § Naming's "name of the people" question. See [[../Words/сисачки|сисачки]] for the endonym this word is a close sibling of, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Meaning

"The Sisicians" — the people as a collective, the usual citation form for a Slavic ethnonym (compare real *Hrvati* "the Croats," not the singular *Hrvat*, as the ordinary way to name a people). Individual members: **сишчанин** (a Sisician man), **сишчанка** (a Sisician woman).

## The Real Correction

**The user's own first guess, Сисачани, doesn't survive contact with the real record — checked via search before building anything.** The actual attested Croatian demonym for a Sisak resident is **Siščanin** (masc.), **Siščanka** (fem.), plural **Siščani** — not *Sisačanin*/*Sisačani*. The reason isn't arbitrary: this word is built with a **different suffix and a different sound-change environment** than [[../Words/сисачки|сисачки]], and the two happen to diverge in a genuinely interesting way rather than one simply being "wrong" — see Etymology.

## Declension

A real, distinct minor Slavic declension class, not ordinary a-declension: the singular carries a **singulative** suffix **-ин** ("one member of X") that the plural simply drops, taking a bare **-и** directly on the stem — the same pattern real Croatian *građanin* "citizen" (sg.) / *građani* "citizens" (pl.) shows, not the ordinary *-ovi/-evi* plural extension [[../Words/дом|дом]]-type nouns take.

## Etymology

**Same root as [[../Words/сисачки|сисачки]] (Сиск-, from the real toponymic chain Segestica→Siscia→Siscium→Сисак), but a genuinely different suffix produces a genuinely different outcome — not an inconsistency to paper over.**

сисачки attaches the suffix **\*-ьskъ**, whose trigger segment is a **front yer** (a vowel-like segment) immediately following the root's final consonant — an ordinary single-consonant first palatalization (к→ч), affecting only that one consonant.

**сишчани attaches a different real, extremely productive Common Slavic suffix, \*-aninъ** ("one belonging to a place," a singulative built on an old *-an-* collective plus *-inъ* "one" — the same suffix behind real Croatian *seljanin* "villager" from *selo* "village," *građanin* "citizen" from *grad* "town"). Its trigger segment is the **glide /j/** (a full consonant, not a vowel), and when /j/ follows a whole **consonant cluster** ending in /k/ — here, the root's own **-sk-** — the *cluster as a unit* undergoes its own distinct, real, cross-Slavic-attested development: ***skj → šč*** (confirmed via search — South Slavic *stj/skj* clusters converge on *šč*, later reduced further to *št* only in Bulgarian and the eastern BCS dialects; western South Slavic, including Croatian, keeps *šč*, matching real *Siščanin* directly and matching Sisician's own established preference for the western/BCS side of this exact kind of fork elsewhere in the vault).

So: **Сиск-** (bare root) + **-aninъ** → cluster jotation, *skj→šč* → **Сишчан-** + final grammatical yer (weak, drops per ordinary [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]]) → **сишчанин** (masc. sg.). Feminine **-ка** (the ordinary demonym-forming feminine suffix, real Croatian *Splićanka*, *Zagrepčanka*) gives **сишчанка**. Plural drops the singulative **-ин** per the real *građanin/građani* pattern, giving **сишчани**.

**Why this diverges from сисачки, stated plainly**: сисачки's trigger is a single front *vowel* acting on a single consonant (к alone palatalizes, the preceding с is an uninvolved bystander); сишчани's trigger is a *glide* acting on the whole preceding cluster (ск together become шч as a unit). Same root, two real, independently-attested, mechanically distinct Common Slavic sound laws — not the same process applied inconsistently.

## Register

Native, unmarked, [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]] Layer I — the people's own name for themselves stays in the native Slavic layer for the same reason [[../Words/сисачки|сисачки]] does: self-designations overwhelmingly stay native cross-linguistically, even in a culture as thoroughly hybridized as this one.
