# нув (nuv)

**Status: exploratory/tentative.** The first adjective word entry — and already the worked example on [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Grammar/Adjective Agreement]], which documents its full hard-declension paradigm (this page doesn't repeat that table, just points to it). See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

**Renamed 2026-09-20 from "нов" to "нув"** — see § Etymology for why; this isn't a new coinage, it's a vowel correction to an already-built word, following the same kind of revision [[../Words/јаз|јаз]] went through earlier (bare "ja" → "јаз").

**This word is [Swadesh item 183](../Etymology/Swadesh%20List.md#quality-adjectives-181–200), "new"** — see [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] for the full comparative row across Proto-Slavic, OCS, Serbo-Croatian, Slovene, Vulgar Latin, Dalmatian, and Sardinian. That row is exactly what motivated this rename: Dalmatian's own cell there already reads **nuv**.

## Meaning

"New."

## Declension

Full paradigm — long form (attributive), short form (predicate), hard declension — is documented at [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Grammar/Adjective Agreement]], not repeated here. нув is a **hard-stem** adjective (its final consonant в isn't in the soft-stem trigger set ц дз ч џ ш ж љ њ ј), so it takes the plain *-ога/-ому* endings, not the soft *-ега/-ему* variants — unaffected by the vowel change, since the stem-final consonant that decides hard/soft is untouched.

## Etymology

**Deep root:** PIE **\*néwos** "new" ← Proto-Balto-Slavic **\*náwas** ← Proto-Slavic **\*nòvъ**. The same root also gives Proto-Slavic's comparative form **\*novějь** ("newer"), for whenever comparative degree gets built. This part of the etymology is unchanged by the rename below — Proto-Slavic *novъ is still нув's real ancestor; only the modern Sisician reflex's vowel has moved.

**Why о became у: a real, motivated convergence, not an arbitrary respelling.** This word's own earlier page (when it was still spelled нов) had already flagged something worth watching for: Latin *novus* — ancestor of whatever Dalmatian's own "new"-word would turn out to be — descends from the *exact same PIE root* as native нов/нув, making the two genuine cognates rather than a coincidental look-alike (contrast [[../Words/дом|дом]]/[[../Words/каза|каза]]'s real false-friend pair). Building out [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] confirmed the prediction directly: Dalmatian's actual reflex is **nuv** — not from an arbitrary vowel shift, but the regular output of [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s own already-documented diphthongization of stressed Latin ō (*nŏvus* → Vulgar Latin *\*nuovo*, shown in that same Swadesh row, → Dalmatian *nuv*). So Sisician ended up with two genuinely cognate words for "new," inherited by two different routes (native Slavic *novъ* and Dalmatian-mediated Latin *novus*), landing one vowel apart. **The decision here is that they converged rather than staying formally distinct**, with the Dalmatian vowel winning out in the merged form — a real, attested type of contact effect between languages in sustained contact (convergence/leveling between near-homophonous cognates), not a regular Slavic sound law: no other Slavic *o* systematically becomes у in Sisician, this is a lexically-specific merger driven by this one word's unusually tight double ancestry.

**Real Slavic comparanda (pre-convergence forms, for the historical Slavic side specifically):**

| Branch | Language | Form |
|---|---|---|
| South Slavic (liturgical) | Old Church Slavonic | новъ (novŭ) |
| South Slavic | Serbo-Croatian | но̏в / nȍv |
| South Slavic | Slovene | nȍv |
| South Slavic | Bulgarian | нов (nov) |
| East Slavic | Russian | но́вый (nóvyj) |
| East Slavic | Ukrainian | новий (novýj) |
| West Slavic | Polish | nowy |
| West Slavic | Czech | nový |

**No jaz-style branch tension on the Slavic side** — checked the same way as the pronouns, and this one is stable across the whole family; the only variation is the expected long-form-vs-bare-stem citation difference between languages (Russian cites the long-form *nóvyj*, Sisician's own citation form here is the bare/short stem, matching how [[../Grammar/Declension Classes|дом]] and [[../Words/каза|каза]] are cited). The tension that actually mattered here wasn't cross-Slavic-branch at all — it was Slavic-vs-Dalmatian, resolved by convergence rather than by picking a branch.

**Note on IE cognates beyond Balto-Slavic/Germanic:** the original Wiktionary fetch (Proto-Slavic *novъ page, consulted 2026-09-19) confirmed the PIE root and its Germanic descendants (English *new*) directly, but didn't itself list Latin/Greek/Sanskrit cognates. Those cognates (Latin *novus*, Greek *νέος*, Sanskrit *náva-*) are extremely well-established comparative Indo-European facts, but flagging that this particular citation didn't confirm them, rather than presenting all of it as equally sourced.

## Register

Native Slavic in origin, but no longer purely native in *form* — this is now the one lexical item in the vault whose surface shape was directly reshaped by contact with Dalmatian rather than by regular sound change alone. Still basic core vocabulary, not competing with a separate Dalmatian or Greek loan for this concept (see [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s Native Slavic layer) — there's no rival дом/каза-style doublet here, because the two ancestries merged into one word instead of surviving as two registers.
