**2026-09-26: the user flagged that Sisician reads as "too similar to Croatian and Slovenian"** — the lexical three-layer register model (Slavic/Dalmatian/Greek) was working as designed, but grammar and phonology were mostly adopted from real Croatian/Slovene wholesale, with no Sisician-specific innovation layered on top (recent coinages like [[../../Words/кто|кто]], [[../../Words/тамо|тамо]], [[../../Words/все|все]] all cite the nearest real Slavic form directly). The user's own comparandum: **Portuguese deliberately sought to distinguish itself from Spanish** — a *conscious*, elite-driven distancing project, not organic accident.

**Two fronts opened, in this priority order (user's own call):**

1. **A productive definite article — [[../../Grammar/Definiteness|Grammar/Definiteness]], decided and under construction as of 2026-09-26, corrected the same day.** First pass modeled the revived article as *postposed and fused* (Balkan-Sprachbund-style, reasoning from Dalmatian's geographic proximity to Romanian/Bulgarian) — **the user caught a real typological error**: Dalmatian is actually Italo-Dalmatian/Western Romance (confirmed via search), the same family as Italian/Venetian, so its article was always ordinary preposed **el/la** (sg.), **li/le** (pl.), a separate free-standing word placed *before* the noun, not a suffix. Corrected mechanics resolve the vowel-hiatus problem the fused version had left open (a preposed word never touches the noun's own case ending) and are better-grounded than the retired version, not just different. The *original* 2026-09-19 -l fossil also had to be rebuilt, since it depended on the same wrong postposed premise — replaced with article-noun univerbation as a **prefix** (real parallel: Arabic *al-* → Spanish/Portuguese loanwords, Spanish *el-* → English "alligator"), now gender/number-distinct (ел-/ли-/ла-/ле-) rather than a single eroded fossil. Coexists with [[../../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s existing long-form/short-form adjective definiteness system for centuries; **user specified the article only fully displaces the adjective system's definiteness function in the 1800s** — recent, not immediate, with a leading (unresolved) hypothesis for what the adjective forms repurpose toward. **Lesson for future grammar work on this thread**: check genetic/typological classification before reaching for a geographically-plausible-sounding analogy — this error was caught by the user, not self-caught, and cost a full rebuild.
2. **Categorical (not just register-conditioned) phonological erosion of "no-Latin-pathway" sounds — discussed 2026-09-26, not yet built. Plus a third, related mechanism, decided and built the same day: intervocalic lenition, /b, d, g/ → [β, ð, ɣ] between vowels, spelling unchanged** — see [[../../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Modern Inventory]] § Intervocalic Lenition. Modeled on real Spanish (same rule, same "one letter, two allophones" spelling relationship), explicitly *not* claimed as inherited from Dalmatian (Italian, Dalmatian's actual close kin, doesn't do this — claiming otherwise would repeat the [[feedback_classification_before_analogy]] mistake) — framed instead as a natural, cross-linguistically common, independently-arising lenition, reinforcing the same prestige pressure as [ç]/no-syllabic-r without needing a genetic/contact source. Applies unconditionally (every register, native and loan vocabulary, across word boundaries too) — already affects a dozen-plus existing words' pronunciation (када, кога, тебе, теби, тога, чега, њега, овога, седам, тобом, један, одовде) — **user asked for the `ipa:` frontmatter to be updated retroactively anyway, reversing the initial "phonemic, not allophonic, leave it alone" call** — all twelve now show the leniated pronunciation (када→/ˈkaða/, кога→/ˈkoɣa/, тебе→/ˈteβe/, etc.) directly in frontmatter, not just described centrally. The only two sounds flagged in the vault as having zero Vulgar Latin/Dalmatian pathway are /x/ (no velar/uvular fricative in VL/Dalmatian at all) and syllabic /r/,/l/ (no Latin syllable-nucleus-consonant pattern) — both currently just *register variation* ([[../../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Modern Inventory]]'s [ç]/[x]/[χ] three-way spread; [[../../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]]'s eye-spelling colloquial-vs-careful split). User wants these turned into a real *completed* historical sound change over ~200 years: standard Modern Sisician stabilizes at [ç] only (not a spectrum) and always uses the epenthetic vowel (not colloquial-only) — old forms surviving solely in a genuine non-standard/rural dialect plus the mockery-performance register, not as live standard-register options. **Not started — this is the next task once the article's own mechanics settle.**

3. **в/ф revised from labiodental to bilabial, [β]/[ɸ] — decided 2026-09-26, same day as the lenition rule that prompted it.** User noticed the consequence directly: since б already lenites to [β] intervocalically (front #2 above), making в *itself* bilabial (rather than labiodental [v]) means б and в become phonetically identical between vowels, while staying distinct everywhere else (б=[b] stop vs в=[β] continuant elsewhere) — a real, deliberate **partial neutralization**, the same general phenomenon as English's own intervocalic /t/~/d/ flapping (though narrower than Spanish's own b/v, which is a full historical merger in all positions, not just one). Well-grounded independently of that consequence too: в's own etymology (a hardened Common Slavic glide *v < PIE *w, per [[../../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]]) makes bilabial the *smaller* articulatory step from a bilabial glide than labiodental would be — the same fork Vulgar Latin's own /w/ took into real Spanish (bilabial β) as opposed to French/Italian (labiodental v). ф's case is weaker (no glide-hardening story; ф is historically a Greek-loanword letter, and whether Greek φ was bilabial or labiodental at the point of contact isn't resolved here) — made bilabial anyway for symmetry with в, flagged honestly as the weaker half of the decision. Updated the consonant chart (Labiodental column dropped entirely) and retroactively updated all 24 в-words' and the 1 ф-word's `ipa:` frontmatter (ви→/ˈβi/, фамаља→/ɸaˈmaʎa/, etc.), same "update the IPA anyway" preference as front #2.

**Why:** the user's explicit standard is that "basically every sentence" should read as visibly non-Croatian/non-Slovene, not just individual vocabulary items — a structural/grammatical distancing, not another lexical layer.

**How to apply:** treat this as a standing lens on future grammar/phonology work, not a one-off task — check whether a new grammar or phonology decision could lean into this distancing direction rather than defaulting to the nearest real Croatian/Slovene form, the same way [[../../Words/все|все]]'s own Kajkavian citation should now prompt asking "is there a *more* distinctive option" rather than stopping at the first real comparandum found. Don't retrofit this onto already-published lexicon retroactively without being asked — this governs new grammar-level decisions and the two open fronts above, not a mandate to revise all ~30 already-built Words/ pages.
