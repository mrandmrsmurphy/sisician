A pervasive, systematic phonemic pairing of every consonant into a plain/palatalized ("hard"/"soft") twin — marked in the orthography via a soft sign, iotated vowels, etc. — is a hallmark of **East Slavic** phonology, Russian above all (Polish has a related but not identical system). It is **not** how South Slavic phonology works, and Serbo-Croatian descriptions correctly don't foreground it.

South Slavic languages instead have the **fossilized output of Common Slavic's historical palatalizations** (the velar palatalizations, and iotation of dentals/labials/velars): a fixed, closed set of palatal/postalveolar phonemes — /ʎ/, /ɲ/, /tʃ dʒ/, /ʃ ʒ/, /j/ (plus /tɕ dʑ/ in Serbo-Croatian proper) — that are independent phonemes, not one half of a live plain/palatalized alternation running across the whole consonant inventory.

**Why:** [[reference_construction_workflow|Phonology/Design Principles.md]] originally described Sisician's palatalization as a Russian-style "hard vs. soft consonants" system with a speculative parallel palatalized-stop series (/k/:/kʲ/, /g/:/gʲ/, etc.). The user caught this on 2026-09-18 after reading up on Serbo-Croatian and not finding it — correctly, since the framing had leaked in from the earlier, abandoned Jewish-Slavic ("Yiddish-in-reverse") conlang possibility, where it would have made more sense (Yiddish has Ashkenazi/Polish contact). It doesn't transfer to a South Slavic base. Both `Phonology/Design Principles.md` and `Phonology/Modern Inventory.md` have been corrected.

**How to apply:** Never describe Sisician's /ɲ ʎ tʃ dʒ ʃ ʒ/ series as a "hard/soft" opposition or propose a parallel palatalized-stop series (/kʲ gʲ tʲ dʲ/) for it — that's the wrong Slavic branch's phonology. Frame it as the standard South Slavic outcome of historical palatalization instead. This applies to any future phonological-history work (Phase 1/3 of [[reference_construction_workflow]]) deriving these sounds from the pre-founding Slavic base.
