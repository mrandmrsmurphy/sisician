Sisician is Slavic grammar underneath, but it should carry itself the way English does relative to French: a Slavic language that idolizes its own Romance-derived vocabulary and pronunciation as prestigious, cultured, and sophisticated, while treating its own native Slavic sounds (or a stand-in "othered" system) as comparatively unrefined by contrast.

**Why:** stated directly by the user (2026-09-20), while discussing Dalmatian's own end-stage phonology: *"We still idolize French, with such phrases as je ne sais quoi, or the strong pronunciation of denouement. We make fun of German for being saying /ix/ (I) and hate /x/. We want a Slavic language that fancies itself a Romance language, like English is a Germanic language that fancies itself a Romance language."* This sharpens [[../../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]]'s own already-stated Core Idea (the English/French analogy is the project's founding premise) into a concrete, actionable mechanism, rather than leaving it as background flavor. It also directly explains why [[../../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s own end-stage phonology (c. 1300) was designed to converge *heavily* toward Slavic rather than staying "clearly Dalmatian" — Dalmatian itself is allowed, even expected, to die out completely and get absorbed, precisely because its prestige and flavor survive independently, inside Sisician's own register system, the same way Anglo-Norman French died out as a living language in England while permanently reshaping English's own prestige-pronunciation habits.

**How to apply:**
- When coining new words or deciding register (see [[../../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Etymology/Semantic Domains]]'s existing layer system), remember that Dalmatian/Romance-derived vocabulary isn't just "one lexical stratum among several" — it should carry active social prestige, the way French loanwords in English often do (formality, elevated register, association with courtliness/sophistication).
- Look for opportunities to have Sisician speakers deliberately retain a "foreign-flavored," non-nativized pronunciation for specific high-prestige Dalmatian-derived words or phrases, the way English keeps French-ish stress/vowel quality in words like *denouement* or fixed phrases like *je ne sais quoi* — this is a real, concrete design tool, not just an abstract theme.
- **Resolved 2026-09-20, see [[../../Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman|Culture/Accent and the Slavic Boogeyman]] for the full design:** the mockery target is real, living, neighboring Croatian/Serbian/Bosnian speakers specifically — an intra-family class snobbery (closer to Anglo-Norman-vs-English-peasant than to England-mocks-Germany), not distant foreigner-mockery, since Sisician is demonstrably Slavic itself underneath the veneer. Three concrete markers: (1) /x/ pushed to an exaggerated uvular [χ], the mirror image of prestige speech's own softening of /x/ toward [ç] (see [[../../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]]); (2) an exaggerated singsong pitch-accent impression, mocking real Neo-Shtokavian BCS's genuine tonal melody, which Sisician's own Kajkavian-descended prosody never had; (3) deliberate reversion to real bare syllabic /r/ (vrt, smrt) against Sisician's own standardized epenthetic-vowel-corrected forms (vart, smart — see [[../../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] § The "Eye-Spelling" Tradition).
- This principle governs **Sisician's own register system** — it is explicitly distinct from and should not be confused with [[../../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s own internal death trajectory (which is allowed to converge heavily toward Slavic and lose its own distinctiveness entirely, per that page's End-Stage Phonology section) — the prestige lives on independently in the surviving language, not in the dying one.
