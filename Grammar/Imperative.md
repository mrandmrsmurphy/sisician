# Imperative

Closes the category [[Verbal Categories]] flags as "confirmed, no forms yet." Copied directly from real Croatian's own three-way split (synthetic core, jussive periphrasis, suppletive negative) — the same "adopt the whole real system" approach already taken for [[Biti (To Be)|biti]] and [[Personal Pronouns]]. See [[Verb Paradigm (Sketch)]] for the conjugation-class stems this builds on, [[Negation]] for не/нон, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Synthetic Core: 2nd Person (sg./pl.) and 1st Person Plural

**Real BCS's own imperative is only synthetic for three cells — 2sg, 1pl, 2pl — not the full six-cell person paradigm.** 1st plural isn't a periphrasis here; "let's do X" genuinely takes the same imperative-ending set as 2nd person in real Croatian (*idimo* "let's go," not a *neka*-construction), and Sisician inherits that as-is.

**Formation**: take the present-tense stem (3rd plural minus its ending, per [[Verb Paradigm (Sketch)]]'s three classes), then add **-и** (2sg) / **-имо** (1pl) / **-ите** (2pl) if the stem is consonant-final, or **-ј** / **-јмо** / **-јте** if the stem is vowel-final (a-type only — e-type and i-type present stems are already consonant-final, so the alternation never arises there). This -и/-ј split is inherited directly from Croatian's own imperative-marker alternation, not a Sisician-specific hiatus rule — though it happens to sit comfortably alongside [[../Phonology/Phonotactics|Phonotactics]]'s own V+/j/ coda mechanism, a reinforcing coincidence rather than the actual derivation.

### A-type: гледати (vowel-final stem гледа-)

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | гледа́ј | гледа́јте |
| 1st | — | гледа́јмо |

### E-type: нести (consonant-final stem нес-)

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | не́си | не́сите |
| 1st | — | не́симо |

### I-type: радити (consonant-final stem рад-)

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | ра́ди | ра́дите |
| 1st | — | ра́димо |

**Worth flagging as a nice, unforced coincidence, not a new rule**: i-type ра́ди is spelled identically to the ordinary 3rd-singular present ра́ди — true in real Croatian too (*radi* "he works" / "work!" are homographs, disambiguated only by context), not a Sisician-specific collision.

**A concrete loanword-verb data point**: [[../Words/респонџати|респонџати]] (a-type, stem респонџа-) gives **респонџа́ј / респонџа́јмо / респонџа́јте** ("answer!" / "let's answer" / "answer! [pl.]"), the same vowel-final -ј pattern as гледати.

## Jussive: 1st Singular and 3rd Person (sg./pl.) — нека + Present

**Real BCS has no synthetic imperative at all for these three cells** — it reaches for a jussive particle, **нека**, plus the ordinary finite present tense (already fully built per [[Verbal Agreement]] and [[Verb Paradigm (Sketch)]]'s three conjugation-class tables). No new verb morphology is needed here — this whole cell of the paradigm is "free" once нека exists as a word, since it just governs an already-existing finite form:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | не́ка ра́дим | *(not idiomatic — 1st plural is synthetic, see above)* |
| 3rd | не́ка ра́ди | не́ка ра́де |

**Etymology of нека — real, pan-Slavic, not invented.** Continues Common Slavic **\*nexati/\*nechati** "to let, allow" — a real LET-verb whose imperative grammaticalized into a jussive particle independently across Slavic: Polish **niech**, Ukrainian **нехай** (nechaj), Czech **nechť**, and BCS **neka** itself are all the same development. Sisician's нека inherits this directly, the same evidentiary tier as [[Biti (To Be)|бити]]'s own core paradigm — a whole real category adopted wholesale, not reconstructed from scratch.

**Deliberately kept distinct from да** ([[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]), despite both governing a finite clause: да is the general subordinating complementizer (descended from a PIE demonstrative), нека is specifically the jussive/optative particle (descended from a LET-verb) — different real Slavic words with different real histories that happen to converge on a similar "particle + finite present" surface shape. Keeping them separate rather than collapsing нека into да matches how real BCS itself keeps them separate.

## Negative Imperative: немој(-мо/-те) — a Suppletive Fossil, Not a Simple не + Imperative

**The synthetic 2sg/1pl/2pl cells above cannot simply take не- the way an ordinary indicative verb does** — real Croatian famously can't say *не остани* for a perfective verb; negating the synthetic imperative requires a separate, defective auxiliary:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | немо́ј (да ра́диш) | немо́јте (да ра́дите) |
| 1st | — | немо́јмо (да ра́димо) |

**немој + да + present**, not + infinitive — the modern-period да-complementation pattern ([[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]) applies here the same way it does everywhere else a control-type complement is needed; an archaic/literary register could plausibly still show немој + (dying) infinitive, mirroring that page's own gradual-recession timeline, but the productive modern construction is да-based.

**Etymology, real and worth citing carefully**: real Croatian **немој** is itself historically **не** (the ordinary verbal negator, per [[Negation]]) fused with a fossilized old imperative of ***mošti/moći*** ("to be able, may") — the same real-Slavic modal-verb root that would underlie any future "may/might" coinage (see the open modal-verb question this page doesn't resolve). немој is adopted as a single **defective, fossilized negative-imperative auxiliary**, the same status [[Biti (To Be)|биti's]] own бих enjoys as a fossilized aorist rather than a live present-tense form — it only ever appears in this one function, with no independent living *moći* paradigm needed to justify it.

**The jussive cells need no such suppletion**: не simply negates the present tense normally inside a нека-clause (**не́ка не ра́ди** "let him not work"), since нека governs an ordinary indicative present, not the irregular synthetic imperative — negation only gets weird where the synthetic imperative itself is involved.

## Aspect: Perfective vs. Imperfective Imperative — the Ancient Greek Parallel, Realized Differently

**No new endings are needed for this distinction — it's a free consequence of aspect being inherited bedrock** ([[Verbal Categories]] § Aspect), not a separate imperative-specific mechanism. But it's worth being precise about *how* the parallel to Ancient Greek actually works, since the two languages realize it through different machinery:

**Real Ancient Greek marks the aspectual split morphologically on the imperative itself** — a single verb has both a present-stem imperative (imperfective: λῦε "be releasing / keep releasing," a general precept or ongoing command) and an aorist-stem imperative (perfective: λῦσον "release! [once, as a completed act]"), built from two stems of the *same* lexeme.

**Slavic — and Sisician — don't do this.** Aspect isn't a stem-alternation within one verb; it's a lexical property distinguishing two *separate*, usually formally related, verbs (an imperfective/perfective pair, e.g. real Croatian *sjedati*/*sjesti* "to be sitting down" / "to sit down [once]"). So the Ancient-Greek-style contrast surfaces here as: **conjugate whichever member of the aspectual pair you mean, using the identical synthetic/jussive endings above** — no separate "perfective imperative ending" exists, because the perfectivity already lives in the verb you picked, not in the imperative morphology layered on top of it. **Сједни!** (perfective, "sit down! [one bounded act]") vs. its imperfective partner's own imperative (ongoing/habitual "keep sitting," "be seated [as a state]") would be the worked example — but Sisician doesn't yet have an actual coined aspectual pair to demonstrate this with concretely; [[Verb Paradigm (Sketch)]]'s own "Aspect pairs" item is still open, and per this vault's standing policy against inventing vocabulary unprompted, no pair is coined here just to fill this example. The mechanism is fully decided; a concrete Sisician sentence pair is the next real gap once an aspectual pair exists.
