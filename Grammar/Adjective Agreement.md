# Adjective Agreement

Decided 2026-09-19. See [[Personal Pronouns]] and [[../Words/он|Words/он]] for the *jь etymology this connects to, [[Nominal Morphology]] for the case/gender system this reflects, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## The Real Pattern This Maps Onto: Long Form vs. Short Form

What was described — attributive adjectives taking full gender/number/case agreement, predicate adjectives taking only gender/number — isn't a new invention. It's the real, well-documented Slavic **long-form (definite, historically "pronominal") vs. short-form (indefinite, historically "nominal")** adjective distinction, still fully productive in Serbo-Croatian today. Sisician keeps it, mapped directly onto function:

- **Attributive → long form.** Full agreement in gender, number, *and* case — no shortcuts, matching whatever declension class the modified noun belongs to ([[Declension Classes]]).
- **Predicate → short form.** Agrees in gender and number only; no case distinction.
- **Substantivized adjectives are fully productive, and default to the long form** — nominalizing an adjective ("the young [one]," "the sick [one]") inherently implies a definite referent, which is exactly the long form's own native semantic territory. This isn't a separate rule; it falls directly out of the long form already being the "definite" one.

**Decided (2026-09-19): the short form is genuinely caseless by design, not just unused.** Real modern Serbo-Croatian technically retains a full case-declined indefinite paradigm (genitive, dative, etc. all exist in a dictionary entry like Wiktionary's *bled*), but in living speech those oblique indefinite forms are essentially moribund — everyday attributive use in any oblique case defaults to the long form regardless of logical definiteness, and the short form survives productively only in the nominative (predicate position). Sisician goes one step further and actually **loses** the old indefinite oblique case forms structurally, rather than letting them survive as unused fossils the way real BCS does. This is the same *kind* of process already seen everywhere else in this project — categories don't go dormant, they disappear — matching [[Nominal Morphology]]'s locative and neuter losses, [[Verbal Categories]]'s aorist/imperfect, and [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]'s infinitive. The short form's *entire* paradigm is now just: singular (masc./fem.) and plural (masc./fem.) — nominative-shaped, gender/number only, full stop.

## The Etymology: Long Form = Short Form + *jь

**Confirmed via Wiktionary's Proto-Slavic appendix for *novъ "new" (consulted 2026-09-19), not assumed.** The indefinite/short declension and the definite/long declension are given as genuinely separate paradigms, and the long form is constructed by suffixing **\*-jь** (the same anaphoric pronoun already behind [[../Words/он|он]]'s own oblique stem њега/њему/итд. — see that page's Etymology) onto the short form: indefinite nominative masculine *novъ* becomes definite *novъjь*. The historical logic is transparent once stated: "new" + "that [one]" = "the new one" — a demonstrative pronoun literally cliticized onto an adjective to make it definite, the same general strategy behind definite articles in many other language families, just accomplished through suffixation here instead of a separate word.

**This means attributive Sisician adjectives are, at their etymological root, adjective-plus-pronoun compounds** — which is *why* they inherited a full case paradigm: they're built from the same material as [[Personal Pronouns]]'s own fully-declining oblique forms, not from the short form's more limited nominal declension.

## Consequence: No Separate Neuter, Same as Everywhere Else

Adjectives agree with [[Nominal Morphology]]'s two living genders, not three. An adjective modifying an old-neuter-singular noun takes **masculine** agreement; modifying an old-neuter-plural noun takes **feminine** agreement — the identical mechanism already established for nouns ([[Declension Classes]]) and pronouns ([[Personal Pronouns]]). Not a new decision, just consistency.

## Two Declensions: Hard and Soft (2026-09-19)

Real Slavic adjective declension splits into **hard** and **soft** types depending on the adjective's final stem consonant — a genuinely different phenomenon from the Russian-style systematic hard/soft consonant *pairing* already rejected for Sisician's phonology in general (see [[../Phonology/Design Principles|Phonology/Design Principles]] § Palatalization). This is narrower and real regardless: a small, closed set of stem-final consonants conditions an *-o-* → *-e-* shift in certain endings (genitive/dative/accusative-animate singular masculine).

**Confirmed trigger set for Sisician** (adapted from real Croatian's own soft-stem consonants, minus ć/đ, which Sisician doesn't have — see [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]]): stems ending in **ц дз ч џ ш ж љ њ ј** take the **soft** endings (*-ега, -ему*); everything else takes the **hard** endings (*-ога, -ому*). No lexical soft-stem adjective exists yet to illustrate this concretely — the rule is decided, an example is pending.

## The Two Paradigms: нув "new"

**Renamed 2026-09-20 from "нов" to "нув"** — see [[../Words/нув|Words/нув]] § Etymology for the full reasoning (a real, motivated convergence with Dalmatian's own cognate *nuv*, confirmed via [[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] item 183, not an arbitrary respelling). Every form below reflects the vowel change; the underlying case/gender machinery this page describes is completely unaffected — only the root vowel moved.

### Indefinite (Short Form) — Caseless

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | нув | нува |
| Plural | нуви | нуве |

That's the whole paradigm. No genitive, dative, accusative, or instrumental indefinite forms exist — not omitted for space, structurally absent. This is the entire short-form system: two genders, two numbers, no case.

### Definite (Long Form) — Full Case Paradigm, Hard Declension

| Case | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative sg. | нуви | нува |
| Genitive sg. | нувога | нуве |
| Dative sg. | нувому | нувој |
| Accusative sg. | нуви (inanim.) / нувога (anim.) | нуву |
| Instrumental sg. | нувим | нувом |
| Nominative pl. | нуви | нуве |
| Genitive pl. | нувих | нувих |
| Dative/Instrumental pl. | нувим(а) | нувим(а) |

No locative row — per standing convention, dead cases aren't mentioned even in a merged header. Nothing is lost by leaving it out: real Croatian's adjective locative plural was already identical to dative/instrumental, the same "costs nothing" pattern already seen with nouns and pronouns.

**The accusative masculine singular depends on the head noun's animacy, not on any property of the adjective itself** — an attributive adjective modifying an inanimate masculine noun (like [[../Words/дом|дом]]) takes нуви; modifying an animate one takes нувога, mirroring [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s own established animacy rule exactly.

**Soft declension endings** (no lexical example yet): genitive singular masculine **-ега** in place of -ога; dative singular masculine **-ему** in place of -ому. Everything else (nominative, accusative, instrumental, all feminine and plural forms) is unaffected by the hard/soft split — it's specifically an *-o-*/*-e-* alternation localized to those two cells.

**Note the plural forms are identical between the two tables** (нуви/нуве) — this isn't a mistake; it's the real, already-noted fact that the short/long opposition never lived in the plural to begin with, so the "definite" plural row above and the "indefinite" plural row above are the same forms doing double duty, not two independent paradigms that happen to coincide.
