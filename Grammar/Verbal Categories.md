# Verbal Categories

Decided 2026-09-19 — categories and their fates only, no endings yet (that's the next step). See [[Biti (To Be)]], [[Verbal Agreement]], and [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]] for pieces this builds directly on. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Aspect: Perfective/Imperfective — Inherited Bedrock, Not a Decision

**Yes, confirmed** — but it's worth being precise about what kind of fact this is. Grammatical aspect (perfective vs. imperfective) isn't a design choice the way the case losses or the neuter merger were; it's a defining structural feature of every Slavic language without exception, inherited straight from Common Slavic. [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]] already presupposes it (its whole aspectual-split claim only makes sense if aspect exists). Retaining it isn't optional under [[Design Principles]]'s "not a creole, robust Slavic grammar" constraint — losing it would be the actual creole-like move.

## Finite Agreement: Person + Number — Already Decided

Confirmed, already settled in [[Verbal Agreement]]: person and number agreement, inherited from Common Slavic, licensing pro-drop. Nothing new here.

## Imperative — a Must, Uncontroversial

Confirmed, and not a design tension: every living Slavic language has a productive imperative mood. **Built 2026-09-25, see [[Imperative]]**: synthetic 2sg/1pl/2pl (per [[Verb Paradigm (Sketch)]]'s three conjugation classes), jussive 1sg/3sg/3pl via нека (a real pan-Slavic \*nexati-descended LET-particle) + present, немој(-мо/-те)-suppletion for negating the synthetic cells (немој itself a fossilized \*mošti/moći imperative, structurally the same category as [[Biti (To Be)]]'s own бих), and a perfective/imperfective imperative contrast realized the Slavic way (via the aspectual verb pair itself, not a Greek-style stem alternation) rather than invented from scratch.

## Tense/Mood System: Mapping Your Guesses to the Real BCS Inventory

Your guessed list (present, past, two futures, pluperfect, "something irrealis") maps cleanly onto real BCS's actual living categories — and several of them turn out to be exactly why earlier decisions were made the way they were:

| Category | Real BCS formation | Status here |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | finite present | Confirmed — [[Verbal Agreement]]'s paradigm |
| **Past** | Perfect: [[Biti (To Be)]]'s short/clitic present + l-participle | Confirmed as the *main* past — see § Aorist/Imperfect below for what else is competing for this slot |
| **Future I** | че-clitic + infinitive **or** че-clitic + да + present | Confirmed — and this is a direct consequence of [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]: the future is exactly the kind of control context да was already established to invade, so Future I's construction should follow the same historical timeline (infinitive-based in Old/Middle Sisician, да-based by the modern period) |
| **Future II (Futur Exact)** | [[Biti (To Be)]]'s **будем**-paradigm + l-participle | Confirmed — this is *why* будем was built in the first place; used in subordinate temporal/conditional clauses |
| **Pluperfect** | biti's own past + l-participle of the main verb | Confirmed, as named — **and now actually buildable (2026-09-25)**: [[Biti (To Be)]]'s own l-participle (био/била/били/биле) was the missing piece; e.g. *био сам радио* "I had worked" |
| **Conditional ("irrealis")** | бих-series (fossilized aorist, **built 2026-09-25 in [[Biti (To Be)]]**) + l-participle | This is what "something irrealis" is reaching for — real BCS's actual irrealis category is the Conditional, not a separate mood family; e.g. *радио бих* "I would work" |

**Consequence: the l-participle is now a real priority, not a side note.** Four of these six categories (Perfect, Pluperfect, Future II, Conditional) all need it. [[Biti (To Be)]] already flagged the l-participle as unbuilt and specifically noted it's where the neuter merger (masculine singular / feminine plural) has to apply directly — this is no longer a someday task, it's blocking most of the tense system.

## The Aorist and Imperfect — a Category You Didn't Mention, With a Good Home

Real Common Slavic (and real BCS, which uniquely retains vestiges among modern Slavic languages) had two more finite past tenses beyond the Perfect: the **aorist** (simple perfective past) and the **imperfect** (simple imperfective past). In living modern BCS these are archaic/literary — largely fallen out of ordinary speech, surviving in narrative/literary registers. **Real Old Church Slavonic, by contrast, uses the aorist and imperfect as its ordinary narrative past tenses** — Church Slavonic biblical/liturgical prose is built on them, much like Ancient Greek narrative prose.

**Decided (2026-09-19): register-conditioned, dying gradually like the infinitive.** Dead in the vernacular by the modern period (replaced by the Perfect), alive as a marked, archaic, liturgical-register feature — directly mirroring real OCS's own aorist/imperfect-heavy narrative style. This extends [[Word Order and Register]]'s syntax-level register marking into the tense system itself: aorist/imperfect narration reads as unmistakably liturgical/elevated, the effect [[../Culture/Religion|Culture/Religion]] already wants from that register.

**One real-world grounding point worth being precise about:** aorist/imperfect loss is *not* a general Balkan Sprachbund trend the way case loss and infinitive loss are — Bulgarian and Macedonian actually *kept* a fully living, everyday aorist/imperfect distinction; it's specifically the **western South Slavic** (Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian) branch that marginalized them into literary/archaic status. Since Sisician's vernacular base is Kajkavian — part of that same western branch, not the Bulgarian/Macedonian one — following the Serbo-Croatian trajectory (marginalization) rather than the Bulgarian/Macedonian one (full retention) is the linguistically consistent choice, not an arbitrary one.

**Timeline**, run alongside (not copied from) [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]'s dates, since these are related symptoms of the same broad analytic-simplification wave but not identical processes:

| Stage | Aorist/imperfect status |
|---|---|
| Old Sisician (c. 1000–1150) | Fully living and productive in the vernacular, as in Common Slavic |
| Middle Sisician (c. 1150–1350) | Receding from ordinary vernacular speech, increasingly confined to narrative/formal registers even outside the liturgy; meanwhile fully reinforced in the liturgical register by direct OCS influence |
| Early Modern Sisician (c. 1350–1650) | Effectively dead in ordinary vernacular speech, replaced by the Perfect; survives only as a marked liturgical/literary feature |
| Modern Sisician (c. 1650–present) | Purely liturgical/archaic-literary — using aorist/imperfect in ordinary speech reads as deliberately theatrical, poetic, or religious in register, the same way real modern Croatian speakers perceive aorist forms as literary or dialectal today |

## Supine — Already Gone Before Sisician's Story Even Starts

**Confirmed, and further back than you might expect.** The supine (a distinct Common Slavic/Proto-Slavic verb form used specifically after motion verbs to express purpose, e.g. "I go [to] hunt") was already merging into the infinitive well before the historical period — even real Old Church Slavonic shows it only as a marginal, vestigial category. This isn't a Sisician-specific loss to explain; it's inherited as **already-lost** from [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] itself, the same way дом inherits an already-completed Fall of the Jers.

## The Infinitive's Nominal Functions — Real Slavic Deverbal Nouns, Not Invention

**Confirmed, and there's a specific, well-attested mechanism, not just "something else."** [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]] only covers the infinitive's *verbal complement* function (control contexts — "I want to do X"), which да takes over. The infinitive's separate *nominal* function (using a verb like a noun — "reading is fun," "the act of doing X") is a different job, and real Slavic languages that lost the infinitive (Bulgarian, Macedonian) don't reinvent anything for it — they use the ordinary, already-extremely-productive **Common Slavic deverbal noun suffix** (*-nje/-će*-type, e.g. real Croatian *čitanje* "reading" from *čitati* "to read"). Sisician should do the same: **да replaces the infinitive's verbal-complement role; the native *-nje/-če* deverbal noun replaces its nominal role** (-če, not real Croatian's -će: [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]]'s ć→č merger applies here too, since this suffix's -će allomorph is itself a *tj-derived affricate, the same historical category as свеча/noč). No new invention needed on this point either.
