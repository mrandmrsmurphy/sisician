# Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality

Decided 2026-09-19 — the first genuinely novel Sisician-specific innovation, not a straight adoption from Croatian or Dalmatian. See [[Biti (To Be)]], [[Verbal Categories]], and [[Clitic Ordering]] for what this builds on. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Starting Point: The Conservative Periphrastic Past

Real Croatian forms its past tense (the Perfect) with [[Biti (To Be)]]'s short/clitic present + the **l-participle**, which agrees in gender and number — *radio* (masc.), *radila* (fem.), *radilo* (neut.), plural *radili/radile/radila*. The auxiliary (сам/си/је/смо/сте/су) carries person/number; the participle carries gender/number.

**Applying [[Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger here for the first time** (flagged as pending since [[Biti (To Be)]] and [[Verbal Categories]]): the participle has no separate neuter form. Old-neuter-singular referents take the masculine participle (радио); old-neuter-plural referents take the feminine participle (радиле). Same mechanism as nouns and pronouns, now applied to participles:

| | Masculine (sg.) | Feminine (sg.) | Masculine (pl.) | Feminine (pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| l-participle | радио | радила | радили | радиле |

## The Innovation: Clitic Fusion Splits Into Two Divergent Outcomes

**је is phonologically weak** — unstressed, proclitic, carrying no information beyond 3rd person singular (which is also its most semantically "empty" auxiliary slot: no distinctive person content beyond "not 1st, not 2nd," and no gender content of its own — gender lives entirely on the participle). A clitic that weak, under centuries of contact-era phonetic erosion, doesn't have to survive unchanged. Two different things can happen to it, and Sisician does both, in different functional niches:

### Outcome 1 — Fusion: gender/number agreement moves onto the verb complex itself

The auxiliary univerbates with the participle rather than staying a separate word: **је радио → ЈРА̀дио** (masc. sg.), **је радила → ЈРА̀дила** (fem. sg.), and — extending the same logic to 3rd plural **су** — **су радили → СРА̀дили** (masc. pl.), **су радиле → СРА̀диле** (fem. pl., also covering old-neuter-plural per the merger). The old auxiliary reduces to a bound (or semi-bound) element fronting a single verb form. This becomes the **new default, unmarked past tense** — the ordinary way of reporting something witnessed/known directly.

**Stress stays on the participle's own root, not the fused element** — a fused clitic doesn't retroactively gain stress just by univerbating; it stays prosodically weak, the way clitics do. So the root-anchored stress principle still lands on **-ра-** (the lexical root of *raditi*), not on the fused ј-/с- prefix: ЈРА̀дио, not †ЈРАдио with stress elsewhere.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Fused past, singular (witnessed) | ЈРА̀дио | ЈРА̀дила |
| Fused past, plural (witnessed) | СРА̀дили | СРА̀диле |

### Outcome 2 — Drop: the bare participle becomes a hearsay/reportative evidential

In a separate set of contexts, the auxiliary doesn't fuse — it **vanishes entirely**, leaving the bare participle standing alone: **радио**, **радила**, **радили**, **радиле**, with no auxiliary trace at all (singular and plural both lose their auxiliary the same way). This is exactly the mechanism real Bulgarian and Macedonian used to grammaticalize evidentiality (the "renarrated mood"): dropping the auxiliary from the perfect reanalyzes the construction as reportative/hearsay ("apparently he did," "they're said to have done") rather than witnessed fact. **This is the piece you specifically liked** — auxiliary-loss becomes a real, grammaticalized hearsay marker, not just an occasional colloquial elision, and its natural home is exactly where real reportative/hearsay evidentials cross-linguistically live: **storytelling, legend, myth, and reportage** — narrating events the speaker didn't personally witness. A Sisician folktale or legendary narrative would run in this form throughout, the way a Bulgarian folk tale conventionally runs in the renarrated mood.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Bare participle, singular (hearsay/reportative) | радио | радила |
| Bare participle, plural (hearsay/reportative) | радили | радиле |

## The Result: A Three-Way Evidential Contrast From One Starting Construction

| Form | Auxiliary status | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| је/су радио.../радили... | Conservative, unfused (older / more formal-literary alternative) | Plain past, no evidential marking |
| ЈРА̀дио / СРА̀дили (etc.) | Fused, bound | **Witnessed/direct** past — the new ordinary default |
| радио / радили (etc.) | Dropped entirely | **Hearsay/reportative** past — the register of stories, legends, myths, and reportage |

All three trace back to the identical real Croatian source construction — the divergence is purely in what happened to one weak clitic, in three different directions, which is exactly the kind of single-cause-many-outcomes story the project has valued throughout (compare [[Nominal Morphology]]'s locative loss redistributing into three different case slots from one cause).

## Evidentiary Honesty: What's Cited vs. What's Extended

**The general mechanism is real and well-documented**: clitic-to-bound-morphology fusion is cross-linguistically common (Romance future formation from Latin *habere* is the textbook case), and Balkan Slavic specifically already shows this tendency repeatedly — [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]]'s да, the ће-future, and the бих-conditional are all former independent words now bound or semi-bound. Real Bulgarian/Macedonian aux-drop-for-evidentiality is also real and directly cited (this was already the basis of the evidentiality proposal). **What's a Sisician-specific extension, not a direct citation**: the exact resulting fused form (*јрадио*-type univerbation) isn't attested in real Bulgarian or Macedonian dialectology as far as this page claims — it's a novel application of a real general tendency, which is precisely what was asked for ("room for novel stuff that might arise naturally").

## Scope: Decided — 3rd Person Only (Singular *and* Plural)

**Confirmed (2026-09-19): this system is 3rd person only** — је (singular) and су (plural) both participate; сам/си/смо/сте (1st/2nd person, both numbers) do not fuse or drop, and stay as ordinary conservative periphrastic auxiliaries always. This is a realistic, well-motivated person-asymmetry, not an arbitrary restriction: evidentiality is naturally exercised talking about third parties ("he/she/they reportedly did") — you don't normally need a hearsay marker for your own or your addressee's actions, since you'd know those firsthand. Extending to 3rd plural (су) alongside 3rd singular (је), rather than singular-only, follows directly from the functional domain this serves: legends, myths, and folk narrative constantly need "they" (peoples, gods, groups), not just "he/she."

## Timeline (proposed, following the same gradual-change shape as [[Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation]] and [[Verbal Categories]]'s aorist/imperfect split)

| Stage | Status |
|---|---|
| Old Sisician (c. 1000–1150) | Conservative periphrastic only (је радио/је радила), as inherited |
| Middle Sisician (c. 1150–1350) | Both fusion and drop begin emerging as competing colloquial variants, alongside the conservative form |
| Early Modern Sisician (c. 1350–1650) | Fusion generalizes as the ordinary default past; drop grammaticalizes specifically as reportative/hearsay, no longer just casual elision |
| Modern Sisician (c. 1650–present) | Fully grammaticalized three-way contrast; the unfused conservative form survives only as an archaic/literary/liturgical register option (paralleling the aorist/imperfect's own fate) |
