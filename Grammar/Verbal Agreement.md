# Verbal Agreement and Subject Marking

See [[Nominal Morphology]] for the number system this agrees with, and [[Word Order and Register]] for the discourse-level pattern this directly enables. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb agrees with its subject in **person** (1st/2nd/3rd) and **number** (singular/plural, per [[Nominal Morphology]]'s two-number system). This isn't new invention — it's straightforwardly **inherited from Common Slavic**, which already has a full, real present-tense person/number agreement paradigm (the source of every modern Slavic language's verb conjugation). No sound-change work is needed to justify this; it comes for free with the [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] ancestor.

## Pro-Drop, Not Subject Clitics

**Decided: canonical pro-drop**, not a French/Northern-Italian-style subject clitic system. Rich person/number agreement alone is sufficient to identify the subject; independent subject pronouns are optional, appearing only for emphasis, contrast, or disambiguation — exactly like every real Slavic language, and like Greek.

**Why this over subject clitics — both source languages agree, though with different strength of evidence:**

- **Slavic side (strong):** every Slavic language, including Kajkavian, is uniformly pro-drop. There is no Slavic subject-clitic tradition anywhere to draw on.
- **Dalmatian side (typological inference, not direct attestation):** subject clitics are a real Romance phenomenon, but specifically associated with the *innovative* Gallo-Romance zone — French, and northern Italian dialects like Venetian and Piedmontese (often arising to compensate for eroded, less phonologically distinctive verb endings). Standard Italian, Sardinian, and Romanian are pro-drop instead, like Slavic. [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] has already been built as *conservative/peripheral* Romance — preserving intervocalic voiceless stops, preserving velars before front vowels, patterning with Sardinian rather than French/Northern Italian. Subject-clitic-doubling belongs to the opposite (innovative Gallo-Romance) typological package, so for internal consistency, Dalmatian more plausibly stayed pro-drop too. **Caveat, stated plainly:** unlike the phonology decisions, this isn't backed by direct Vegliote/Ragusan syntactic evidence — real Dalmatian is barely documented syntactically at all (Bartoli's material is overwhelmingly phonological/lexical). This is inference from Dalmatian's already-established conservative character, not a citation.

## Resolves an Open Dependency

[[Word Order and Register]] flagged that the "Greek discourse" pattern (subject stated once, never repeated across an extended stretch) needs verb agreement rich enough to license long-distance subject omission. **This is now resolved**: canonical pro-drop, inherited directly from Common Slavic, is exactly that mechanism — no additional machinery needed. This also reinforces the Balkan-Sprachbund framing already used for the case system ([[Nominal Morphology]] § Locative): Greek itself is pro-drop for the same reason (rich agreement), so Sisician's Slavic-inherited pro-drop and its Greek-influenced discourse register aren't in tension — they're the same mechanism doing double duty.
