# Personal Pronouns

Based directly on the real Croatian paradigm. See [[Nominal Morphology]] for the case/gender system this reflects, [[Verbal Agreement]] for why these are used only for emphasis/contrast (pro-drop is the default for subjects), and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## The Paradigm

Only the five living cases appear (locative and vocative are gone, per [[Nominal Morphology]] — and this loses nothing extra here: real Croatian's pronoun locative forms are already identical to the dative in every cell, so dropping the row doesn't collapse anything that used to be distinct; pronouns never had vocative forms to begin with).

Parenthesized forms are the short/enclitic (unstressed) variants, kept alongside the full/stressed forms exactly as in real Croatian — see § Clitics below for what this actually commits us to.

**Split into separate 3sg-m/3sg-f and 3pl-m/3pl-f columns** — no reason to keep masculine and feminine mashed into one slashed cell. Originally the 3pl-m and 3pl-f columns were identical in every oblique case, matching real Croatian (see § Only Two Genders below) — **genitive and accusative plural feminine have since been given their own innovated form, ЊЕХ, distinct from masculine ЊИХ** (see § Why Genitive and Accusative, Not Dative and Instrumental below). Dative and instrumental plural remain genuinely identical between genders, matching real Croatian.

## Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate, and Dative/Instrumental Don't

**The root cause: the neuter merger removed the pronoun system's one disambiguation escape-hatch.** Real Croatian's oblique pronoun cases were *never* gender-marked to begin with — masculine and neuter were already identical in every singular oblique case (genitive *njega* for both, etc.), and all three genders were already identical in every plural oblique case (*njih*, *njima* for masculine, feminine, *and* neuter alike). [[Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger didn't touch any of that; it only removed the **nominative** *on/ona/ono* → *on/ona* and *oni/one/ona* → *oni/one* distinctions. But that nominative was the *only* place in the whole paradigm where "person" and "thing" were ever kept apart — so losing it doesn't just simplify the nominative, it removes the system's last remaining resource for disambiguating a referent as animate/personal vs. formerly-neuter/inanimate, anywhere in the paradigm. That's real, systemic pressure, even though no oblique case was ever structurally different to begin with.

**Where that pressure resolves is a functional question, not a phonological one.** Proportional analogy (see below) explains *how* a new form gets built once pressure exists, but it doesn't explain *why only some cells actually adopt it* — dative and instrumental had an equally good phonological model available (singular *njoj*/*njom* both carry the same *-o-* vowel color that *nje* carries for genitive) and still didn't spread it. The deciding factor is **functional load**: accusative objects and genitive possessors are exactly the argument positions where speakers most need to track *who acted on what* and *whose it is* — high referential/disambiguation traffic in ordinary discourse. Datives (recipients) and instrumentals (means) are comparatively peripheral arguments, rarely the locus of referential ambiguity worth resolving. A real, recognized principle in functional/historical linguistics: **the availability of an analogical model isn't sufficient for a change to actually catch on — it also needs enough communicative payoff to be worth speakers adopting.** Dative/instrumental had the model and lacked the motivation; genitive/accusative had both.

**The mechanism, once motivated:** proportional (four-part) analogy, the standard historical-linguistics model for paradigm-internal innovation (the classic "A is to B as C is to D" formula). Feminine singular genitive is already the bare form **ЊЕ**, contrasting with masculine's extended **ЊЕГА** — a live "feminine = bare/е-flavored, masculine = extended stem" contrast already sitting in the paradigm, not invented for this purpose:

> ЊЕ (fem.sg.gen) : ЊЕГА (masc.sg.gen) :: **ЊЕХ** (fem.pl.gen) : ЊИХ (masc.pl.gen)

**Accusative joins genitive, rather than getting its own separate analogy, because they were already historically syncretic** — real Croatian's genitive and accusative plural are identical for every gender (both *njih*). The syncretic pair innovates *together*: both genitive and accusative plural feminine become **ЊЕХ**, preserving the existing gen=acc pairing structure rather than breaking it — a cleaner result than treating genitive and accusative as independent cells.

**The short/clitic form follows the full form here, deliberately.** If the whole point is that speakers need this disambiguation *in actual production*, that need applies at least as much to the high-frequency clitic (arguably more, since clitics are what's actually said in ordinary fast speech) as to the full form. So where the functional motivation is strong enough to justify the change at all (genitive/accusative), it applies fully, both stressed and clitic form (**их → ех**). Where the motivation is absent (dative/instrumental), *nothing* changes — no stressed/clitic split needed, because neither form moves.

## Only Two Genders in the Third Person — the Neuter Merger Applied

**Real Croatian's third-person pronouns are masculine/feminine/neuter** (nom.sg. *on/ona/ono*, nom.pl. *oni/one/ona*), **but Sisician doesn't have a living neuter** — [[Nominal Morphology]] merged it into masculine (singular) and feminine (plural). So the pronoun paradigm above has only two genders per number, not three, dropping *ono* and the neuter *ona* entirely, not just relabeling them.

**This turns out to be nearly free**, the same way the noun declension merger was ([[Declension Classes]]): real Croatian's masculine and neuter pronouns are *already* identical in every oblique case — genitive *njega* for both, dative *njemu* for both, accusative *njega* for both, instrumental *njim* for both. The only place they ever differed was the nominative singular (*on* vs. *ono*), which Sisician's merger simply erases (old-neuter referents just use *он*). Symmetrically, real Croatian's plural oblique cases (*njih*, *njima*) never distinguished gender *at all*, for any of the three genders — the only place plural gender showed up was nominative (*oni/one/ona*), and Sisician's merger erases that too (old-neuter-plural referents use **оне**, not a separate form). Two independent structural mergers, both landing on "the only place the distinction existed was the nominative, and now it doesn't" — a genuinely clean result, not a coincidence being forced.

**True for dative and instrumental, not for genitive/accusative:** § Why Genitive and Accusative Differentiate above gives genitive *and* accusative plural feminine their own new form (ЊЕХ), so "plural oblique cases never distinguish gender" doesn't hold across the board — it still holds for dative and instrumental (low functional load, no motivation to innovate), and no longer holds for genitive/accusative specifically (high functional load, where the neuter-merger's systemic pressure actually resolved).

## Clitics — What Keeping the Short Forms Actually Commits Us To

Keeping ме/те/га/је/му/јој/ју/нам/вам/их/им alongside the full forms is more than a spelling choice — Serbo-Croatian's short pronouns are true clitics, with their own placement rules (the famous Slavic "second-position"/Wackernagel-type clitic slot, fixed relative ordering when multiple clitics stack, interaction with the *biti* auxiliary clitic in compound tenses, the reflexive *se*). None of that syntax has been designed yet — this page adopts the *forms*, not yet the *placement rules*. Worth noting the thematic fit, though: [[Verbal Agreement]] already established pro-drop (minimal/zero subject reference by default); clitic object pronouns are the same "minimize overt pronominal weight in unmarked speech" principle applied to objects instead of subjects. The two decisions reinforce each other rather than being unrelated.

**Note on a real, inherited homophony, not a flaw:** **ми** serves double duty, as 1pl nominative "we" and as the short dative form of "јаз" ("to me") — this ambiguity exists in real Croatian too and is resolved by context/position, not something to fix here. The same thing happens one column over: 2sg dative short **ти** is identical to 2sg nominative **ти** — see [[../Words/ти|Words/ти]].

## T–V Distinction

Real Slavic languages generally mark formal/polite address by using the 2nd person **plural** (ви) to address a single person, rather than the familiar singular (ти) — the same pattern as French *tu/vous* or Russian *ty/vy*. Confirmed for Sisician: **ти** = familiar singular, **ви** doing double duty as both genuine 2nd plural and formal/polite singular address. **See [[../Culture/Formal Address (ти vs ви)|Culture/Formal Address (ти vs. ви)]]** for the fuller treatment: why this feature is doubly-motivated (Slavic areal diffusion *and* direct Dalmatian-elite reinforcement, not simple inheritance) and the social rule governing when to use which, modeled on German *Sie*/*du* practice.
