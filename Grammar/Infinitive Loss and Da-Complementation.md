# Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation

Decided 2026-09-19, resolving the open question raised in [[Biti (To Be)]] § Open Question. See [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] for the periodization this timeline runs alongside, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## The Trajectory

Sisician's infinitive doesn't disappear all at once — it erodes over roughly 650 years, following the real, well-documented Balkan pattern of infinitive replacement (see Joseph 1983, *The Synchrony and Diachrony of the Balkan Infinitive*, for the classic real-world account of exactly this kind of gradual, context-by-context erosion across Balkan Slavic and Balkan Romance): spreading first through **control contexts** (same-subject complements, especially after modal and aspectual verbs) and only much later through **event-type complements** (phasal verbs like "begin," "learn to do").

| Date | Nearest stage in [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]]'s periodization | Infinitive status |
|---|---|---|
| c. 1000 | Old Sisician begins | Fully productive |
| c. 1200 | Middle Sisician | Mixed: **да** begins invading control contexts (same-subject complements), especially after modal and aspectual verbs |
| c. 1400 | early Early Modern Sisician | **да** dominant in speech; infinitive survives only in event-type complements ("begin to do," "learn to do") |
| c. 1650 onward | Modern Sisician | **да** throughout; infinitive no longer productive |

The 1200 and 1400 milestones don't land exactly on Sociolinguistic History's own period boundaries (1150, 1350) — expected, not a discrepancy to fix. Individual grammatical changes run their own timelines; they don't have to snap to the broader periodization.

## The Aspectual Split

**Imperfective verbs kept the infinitive much longer than perfective verbs, which trended toward да earlier.** Flagging the evidentiary status honestly: the control-vs-event-type conditioning above has a direct real-world Balkan parallel; this aspectual refinement is the user's own design addition on top of it — linguistically coherent and worth keeping, but presented as a Sisician-specific choice rather than a cited fact.

## Etymology of да

Reconstructed to a PIE demonstrative root ***do-***, originally a temporal-sequential particle ("then," "at that point") that developed modal force before grammaticalizing into a full subordinating complementizer. This matches a well-attested general pathway — demonstratives commonly become complementizers cross-linguistically (clearest parallel: English *that*, a demonstrative pronoun that became a complementizer: "I know [that he left]"). Real Slavic *da* itself independently supports a deictic origin, living a dual life as a temporal/purposive particle ("so that, in order to") and as an affirmative "yes" in several Slavic languages.

## Consequence for Biti (To Be)

**Resolves that page's open question.** Sisician's infinitive is not a stable, permanently-living category the way Croatian's is — it's a receding category across the language's documented history: real in Old and Middle Sisician, vestigial by Early Modern Sisician, gone by the modern period. [[Biti (To Be)]]'s бити (and any future infinitive citation form) should be understood as **historically real but synchronically dead** in Modern Sisician — a fossil, the same status [[Nominal Morphology]] already gave the vocative case. Worth deciding, once [[../Words|Words/]] entries carry period-specific citation forms, whether a Modern-Sisician-period word should list an infinitive at all, or only a да-construction.

## Consequence for Hteti and the Future

**Future I is a direct, concrete instance of this page's own timeline, not just an abstract consequence.** [[Hteti (To Want) and the Future]] (built 2026-09-25) gives Future I an actual paradigm (че/чеш/че/чемо/чете/че) and shows both stages of the recession side by side rather than just the endpoints: **сунце че сијати** (Old/Middle, infinitive-based) and **сунце че да сија** (Modern, да-based). Real BCS itself still keeps both patterns alive today rather than having fully completed the shift — direct, living evidence that this kind of centuries-long coexistence isn't a Sisician-specific invention.
