# Definiteness

Decided 2026-09-19; substantially extended 2026-09-26, then **corrected the same day** after the user caught a real typological error in the extension's own premise (see § A Real Correction below) — the postposed/fused mechanics built earlier on 2026-09-26 are retired, not just revised. See [[Adjective Agreement]] for the historical productive system this page's history feeds into (and is later displaced by, per § Coexistence, Then Displacement), and [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] for the source-language framework this extends. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Two Systems, One Convergent Story

Sisician's ancestors approached definiteness marking from both sides, using the same raw material — a demonstrative/anaphoric pronoun descended from Latin *ILLE* — but implementing it differently. The Slavic side **fuses** it, as a suffix (*jь* onto the adjective, per [[Adjective Agreement]]). The Dalmatian side kept it as a genuinely **separate, preposed word** — an ordinary article, the same as every one of its real Romance relatives — which mostly disappeared as a live system, surviving only as scattered fossil material (see § A Real Correction and § Where the Article Prefix Survives below). Sisician runs on the Slavic system for most of its history; a revived version of the Dalmatian one eventually displaces it (§ Coexistence, Then Displacement).

## A Real Correction (2026-09-26): the Article Was Preposed, Not Postposed

**The original 2026-09-19 decision on this page, and this page's own same-day 2026-09-26 extension, were both built on a wrong typological premise, caught by the user directly.** The reasoning had been: a postposed article (attached *after* the noun) is a real Balkan Sprachbund feature — Romanian's *-ul*, Bulgarian's *-ът*, Macedonian, Albanian — and Dalmatian sits geographically inside that zone, so it seemed plausible Dalmatian's own article grammaticalized the same way. **That reasoning reached for the wrong relative.** Dalmatian is standardly classified as **Italo-Dalmatian**, grouped with **Western Romance** as "Italo-Western Romance" ([Wikipedia: Dalmatian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatian_language); [Wikipedia: Italo-Dalmatian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Dalmatian_languages)) — the postposed enclitic article is specifically an **Eastern Romance** innovation, shared by contact with Bulgarian/Macedonian/Albanian, and it is *not* found anywhere in the Italo-Western group Dalmatian actually belongs to. Every one of Dalmatian's real relatives — Italian, Venetian, every other Italo-Romance variety — uses the ordinary Western Romance pattern: a **preposed, free-standing article**, placed *before* the noun, from *ILLE/ILLA/ILLI/ILLAE*: Italian *il/la, i/le*, Venetian *el/la*.

**Genetic classification is the stronger evidence here than geographic proximity, and this page should have led with it the first time.** Real Dalmatian syntax remains essentially undocumented (per [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]), so this is still an extension, not a direct citation of an attested Vegliote article paradigm — but it's now grounded in what Dalmatian demonstrably *is* (a Western Romance sister of Italian and Venetian) rather than in where it happens to sit on a map.

**What this fixes for free:** Channel 2's entire fusion/case-attachment mechanic — built on 2026-09-26 to handle a *postposed suffix* — simply isn't needed. A preposed, free-standing article doesn't touch the noun's own case ending at all, which retires the genitive/dative singular vowel-hiatus problem that section used to flag as unresolved. **What this reopens:** the original -l fossil (2026-09-19) was derived by eroding this same wrong postposed premise down to a bare final consonant. That mechanism no longer applies — a preposed free word doesn't erode into a suffix on the *back* of the noun. Rebuilt below (§ Where the Article Prefix Survives) around a different, better-attested mechanism: article-noun univerbation as a **prefix**, the same process behind real Spanish/Portuguese *aldea*, *azúcar*, *alcalde* (all carrying Arabic's own fossilized *al-* article) and English "alligator" (Spanish *el lagarto*, swallowed whole).

## Channel 2: The Antiquarian Revival — el/li/la/le, Preposed and Free-Standing

**The historical story from 2026-09-26 is unchanged; only the mechanics are corrected.** Real Dalmatian's spoken erosion and its *written* legal/notarial register didn't move at the same speed — the same asymmetry already established for Latin itself ([[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]]'s "Latin survives... in diplomatic/foreign-trade/family-document contexts," the exact channel [[../Words/куи|куи]] and [[../Words/абкле|абкле]] already came from). Starting in Early Modern Sisician (c. 1350–1650) — the same period and social class as the "Eye-Spelling" tradition ([[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]] § The "Eye-Spelling" Tradition) — literate, Dalmatian-philic elites went looking in Dalmatian's own written record for a more authentically Dalmatian way to mark definiteness than the "merely Slavic" long-form adjective they'd inherited, found the ordinary preposed article **el/la** (sg.) / **li/le** (pl.), and began generalizing it productively — placed *before* the noun, exactly where it always belonged. **This is a conscious, elite-driven revival project, not organic drift** — the same kind of deliberate linguistic self-distancing the user's own Portuguese-vs-Spanish comparison points at.

**Real grounding, flagged honestly:** el/la/le are exactly the shape of real living Venetian/ordinary-Italian articles (Venetian *el* for masc.sg. specifically; *la/le* match ordinary Italian fem. sg./pl.); *li* matches a real attested Old Italian/Tuscan masculine-plural variant (modern standard Italian *gli*). This is a plausible reconstruction for the Adriatic Dalmatian dialect area, not a directly attested Vegliote primary source — the same evidentiary tier as [[../Words/ној|ној]]'s own proposed NOVEM-erosion.

### Gender/Number Assignment — Falls Out of the Neuter Merger for Free

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | **ел** | **ли** |
| Feminine | **ла** | **ле** |

**No new agreement machinery needed.** [[Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger already sends every old-neuter-singular referent to masculine and every old-neuter-plural referent to feminine — so an old-neuter-singular noun simply takes **ел**, an old-neuter-plural noun simply takes **ле**, the identical mapping already used for [[../Words/он|он]], [[../Words/тај|тај]], and [[../Words/овај|овај]]. This slots directly into a category that already exists rather than requiring a new one.

### Mechanics: Preposed, Free-Standing, Invariant Across Case

**The article is a separate word placed before the noun and agrees only in gender/number — it does not decline for case at all**, matching every one of its real Western Romance relatives directly (Italian/Spanish/French articles are all case-invariant) rather than the German-style pattern (a preposed article *can* case-decline, when the host language keeps living case) that Sisician could otherwise have reached for. This is now the *better*-grounded choice, not just the simpler one: it matches what Dalmatian's actual closest relatives do, rather than splitting the difference between two unrelated typologies the way the retired Bulgarian/Romanian framing did. The noun keeps its own full, ordinary case declension completely untouched — the article sits in front of it and never interacts with its ending.

**Worked example: [[../Words/дом|дом]], masculine, across its full paradigm — no complications anywhere, singular or plural, because the article never touches the noun's own case endings.**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | ел дом | ли домови |
| Genitive | ел дома | ли домова |
| Dative | ел дому | ли домовима |
| Accusative | ел дом | ли домове |
| Instrumental | ел домом | ли домовима |

**This retires last turn's genitive/dative vowel-hiatus problem entirely — it was an artifact of the wrong (fused/postposed) mechanic, not a real problem with these forms.** A small, genuinely minor word-boundary question remains open rather than resolved: **ла**/**ле** end in a vowel, so a vowel-initial noun immediately after them (e.g. a hypothetical *ла + икос*) creates an ordinary cross-word vowel hiatus — real Western Romance languages sometimes elide this (French/Italian *l'ami*, not *le ami*) and sometimes just tolerate it. Not decided here; a much smaller and more optional question than the retired fusion problem, since plenty of real languages simply leave word-boundary hiatus alone.

**Expected NP word order, noted but not deeply worked out:** article first, then any attributive adjective, then the noun — **ел нуви дом** ("the new house") — mirroring the real Italian/Spanish/French article-adjective-noun order rather than anything invented for Sisician specifically.

## Where the Article Prefix Survives — Rebuilt 2026-09-26

**The old -l fossil (kinship/body-part nouns, toponyms/hydronyms, family names, fixed oaths, all eroding to one gender-neutral bare consonant) is retired along with the postposed premise it depended on.** The replacement mechanism is univerbation, not erosion: a handful of Dalmatian-derived loanwords were borrowed into Sisician **as a whole article-plus-noun phrase**, not as a bare noun stem — the article was already sitting in front of the noun in the source language, so nothing needed to jump position for it to end up as a prefix. Over time the boundary between article and noun stopped being felt as a boundary at all, the same way real Arabic *al-* froze onto borrowed Spanish/Portuguese nouns (*aldea*, *azúcar*, *alcalde*) and Spanish *el-* froze onto an English one (*el lagarto* → "alligator").

**This changes which categories plausibly carry the fossil, and the change is a tightening, not a loosening.** The mechanism specifically requires the *host word itself* to be a Dalmatian loan borrowed as a fixed phrase — which rules out the old list's "kinship and body-part nouns" category outright: per [[../Etymology/Semantic Domains|Semantic Domains]], core kinship vocabulary is Layer I, native-Slavic-dominant almost without exception ([[../Words/фамаља|фамаља]] is the one flagged crossover, and even that isn't a whole-phrase borrowing). The categories that *do* fit, and fit tightly, are exactly the ones already established elsewhere in the vault as real vectors for Dalmatian-derived proper-name material ([[../Culture/Founding Myth and Naming|Founding Myth and Naming]]'s own Vitalis Madius de Corinio thread):

- **Toponyms and hydronyms** — place names are routinely borrowed as a single referentially-fixed unit, article included; this is a real, common, cross-linguistic pattern for exactly this reason (compare French place names retaining a fused *Le-*/*La-*, or foreign exonyms borrowed with their own article attached).
- **Family names and bynames** — a byname like "[the] of the Mount" is exactly the kind of phrase-as-name unit that freezes whole (compare real Italian surnames like *Lo Bianco*, *Del Monte*).
- **Fixed expressions and oaths** — a borrowed Dalmatian phrase carrying its own article, memorized as one chunk, immune to the analogical leveling that would otherwise strip the article back out.

**Reversed finding, stated honestly rather than smoothed over: the prefix is gender/number-distinct, not a single neutral fossil.** The 2026-09-19 "-l regardless of gender" resolution was a *consequence* of the old erosion story (the vowel carrying the masc./fem. distinction eroded away before the fossil ever formed) — that story is retired, so its consequence doesn't carry over. Under the univerbation mechanism, the prefix is simply whatever the article's own shape was at the moment of borrowing, gender and number intact: **ел-**/**ли-** for masculine hosts, **ла-**/**ле-** for feminine ones. A toponym borrowed from a Dalmatian feminine noun phrase would carry **ла-**, not a bleached **л-**.

## The Historical Productive System: Slavic Long-Form/Short-Form Marking

**This is where definiteness lived in ordinary Sisician speech for most of the language's history** — not the Dalmatian article, but [[Adjective Agreement]]'s already-established long-form (definite) vs. short-form (indefinite) adjective distinction. **This section describes the system as it stood before the Channel 2 revival above went productive** — see § Coexistence, Then Displacement below for what happens to it afterward. Nothing new to build here about the mechanism itself; this page exists to explain *why* that system won out originally and what else definiteness-marking left behind, not to duplicate [[Adjective Agreement]].

## Why the Slavic System Won, and Why Definiteness Felt Natural to Keep At All

**The etymological parallel matters here, corrected slightly for the mechanics fix above.** [[Adjective Agreement]] already established that the Slavic long form is historically short-form-adjective + suffixed ***-jь*** — the same anaphoric pronoun root behind [[../Words/он|он]]'s own oblique stem, fused onto the adjective to mark definiteness. Dalmatian's own article descends from the *identical* ultimate source, Latin *ILLE* ("that one there") — but, per the correction above, it was never itself a fusion mechanism; it stayed a separate word. The real parallel is one level up from "both fuse": **both sides put a demonstrative-descended element next to the modified word to mark that its referent is known/definite** — the Slavic side happens to fuse it, the Dalmatian side kept it separate, and either implementation is the same underlying strategy. **Bilingual speakers in the founding household and its descendants weren't learning an alien concept when they used the Slavic system — they already had the same underlying strategy in their own Dalmatian speech**, just packaged differently. This is a real, well-motivated reason definiteness-marking survived as a robust category into Modern Sisician at all, even though the specific Dalmatian device didn't win out immediately: the concept was reinforced from both sides.

## Coexistence, Then Displacement (Early Modern Through the 1800s)

**The two systems ran side by side for centuries, genuinely redundant on the same noun phrase, before one displaced the other — and only recently.** From the Channel 2 revival's own start (Early Modern Sisician, c. 1350–1650) through most of Modern Standard Sisician, an attributive definite noun phrase would mark definiteness *twice*: once on the adjective (long form, per [[Adjective Agreement]]) and once again with the preposed article (ел/ли/ла/ле). This isn't a design flaw needing a fix — genuine grammatical redundancy during a slow grammaticalization shift is real and well-attested cross-linguistically, and Sisician's own founding premise (a small, prestigious elite reshaping a majority vernacular's grammar over centuries, not an instant switch) predicts exactly this kind of long overlap rather than a clean handoff.

**Decided: full displacement is recent — the 1800s, not the revival's own start.** By the 1800s, the article had been productive, obligatory, and unambiguous for centuries; the adjective long/short-form contrast, by comparison, had never been more than a matching redundancy once the article already carried the same information. Sometime in the 1800s, the adjective system's own definiteness *function* finally gave way — the same slow "a redundant category loses its job to a competitor" pattern already seen elsewhere in this project ([[Adjective Agreement]]'s own indefinite oblique paradigm dying out, [[../Words/абкле|абкле]] displacing native одкле).

**What happens to the long/short-form adjective distinction once it stops meaning "definite/indefinite" — leading hypothesis, not fully resolved:** real modern Serbo-Croatian's own long/short adjective distinction has, in fact, already drifted away from a live definiteness contrast toward something closer to a syntactic-position marker (long form as the default/unmarked attributive shape, short form surviving mainly in predicate position and fixed expressions) — a genuine, independently-attested real-world parallel for exactly the kind of semantic bleaching this page needs. The leading hypothesis is that Sisician's own long/short split undergoes the same repurposing in the 1800s, just triggered by direct competition from the newly-dominant article rather than happening on its own — the long form keeps existing as a form, it just stops being *about* definiteness. **This is flagged explicitly as an open item, not a decision.**

## On "a Complicated Set of Changes by This Point"

The clean story — short-form adjective + *jь* = long-form adjective — is the *origin*, not the current synchronic reality. [[Adjective Agreement]]'s actual endings (*-ога, -ому, -ега, -ему*, etc.) are already the *result* of whatever sound changes and analogical reshaping happened to that original compound over the intervening centuries — they aren't a transparent "adjective + separate pronoun" sequence anymore, and this page doesn't attempt to reconstruct that derivation step-by-step. **Flagging this as a real further task, not a gap being papered over:** a full historical derivation (short-form + *jь* → intermediate stages → the modern endings) could be built out later if wanted, the same way [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s ЊЕХ innovation was derived explicitly rather than just asserted — it just hasn't been done yet for the adjective long form.
