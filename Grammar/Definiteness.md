# Definiteness

Decided 2026-09-19; substantially extended 2026-09-26 with a second article channel that eventually displaces the original system. See [[Adjective Agreement]] for the historical productive system this page's history feeds into (and is later displaced by, per § Coexistence, Then Displacement), and [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] for the source-language framework this extends. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Two Systems, One Convergent Story

Sisician's ancestors approached definiteness marking from both sides, using structurally the *same strategy* — fuse a demonstrative/anaphoric pronoun onto the modified word — and arrived at very different outcomes. The Dalmatian side mostly lost its device, fossilizing in a handful of lexical categories. The Slavic side kept its device and it became the productive system in ordinary use. Sisician runs on the Slavic system today; the Dalmatian one survives only as scattered relic material.

## Dalmatian's Postposed Proto-Article — Extension, Not Citation

**Calibration, stated plainly:** real Dalmatian's syntax is essentially undocumented — checked against Dalmatian's own Wikipedia entry (2026-09-19), which covers phonology and dialectal variation but has no grammatical treatment of articles at all, consistent with [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s own framing that its syntax is a gap in the record, not a documented fact. What follows is a well-motivated creative extension, not a citation.

**The real grounding:** a *postposed* definite article (attached after the noun, not before) is a genuine, well-documented Balkan Sprachbund feature — Romanian's *-ul/-a* (*lup* "wolf" → *lupul* "the wolf"), and the same pattern in Bulgarian, Macedonian, and Albanian, all independently. This is the opposite of Western Romance, which preposes its articles (French *le loup*, Italian *il lupo*). Since Dalmatian sits geographically adjacent to exactly this postposing zone, it's plausible — not attested, but plausible — that Dalmatian was developing (or had) the same postposed pattern, from the same ultimate source as the rest of Romance: Latin **ILLE** ("that"), grammaticalizing from demonstrative to article.

**Decided: Dalmatian had this postposed article, and lost most of it — going further than Romanian did.** Where Romanian kept its article fully productive down to the modern language, Sisician's Dalmatian ancestor's article mostly disappeared, eroding past Romanian's *-ul* all the way down to a bare consonant fossil: **-l**.

## Two Article Channels, Not One — Added 2026-09-26

**Everything below this point, up through "Why the Slavic System Won," describes what happens if you follow Dalmatian's own *spoken* article straight through to its end: it erodes past Romanian's *-ul* all the way to a bare consonant, fossilizing only in a handful of high-frequency categories.** That's still exactly true, and nothing about it is revised here — it's **Channel 1: the organic fossil**, unbroken folk transmission from spoken Dalmatian into Sisician, confined to kinship/body-part nouns, toponyms/hydronyms, family names, and fixed oaths, per § Where -l Survives below.

**Channel 2, decided 2026-09-26: a second, separate, much later revival — the actual mechanism behind Sisician's modern productive definite article.** Real Dalmatian's spoken erosion and its *written* legal/notarial register didn't move at the same speed — the same asymmetry already established for Latin itself ([[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]]'s "Latin survives... in diplomatic/foreign-trade/family-document contexts," the exact channel [[../Words/куи|куи]] and [[../Words/абкле|абкле]] already came from). While spoken Dalmatian's article had already worn down to bare -l by the time the language died, its own written record — charters, notarial deeds, family documents, the conservative register that always lags behind spoken change — still preserved the fuller pre-erosion forms: masculine **el** (sg.) / **li** (pl.), feminine **la** (sg.) / **le** (pl.).

**Starting in Early Modern Sisician (c. 1350–1650) — the same period, the same social class, as the "Eye-Spelling" tradition** ([[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Sociolinguistic History]] § The "Eye-Spelling" Tradition) — literate, Dalmatian-philic elites went looking in exactly those surviving written records for a more authentically Dalmatian way to mark definiteness than the "merely Slavic" long-form adjective they'd inherited, found el/la/li/le, and began generalizing them productively onto ordinary Sisician nouns. **This is not organic drift — it's a conscious, elite-driven revival project**, the same kind of deliberate linguistic self-distancing the user's own Portuguese-vs-Spanish comparison points at: an act of identity, not an accident of contact. **Real grounding, flagged honestly:** el/la/le are exactly the shape of real living Northern Italian/Venetian-type Romance articles (Venetian *el* for masc.sg. specifically; *la/le* match ordinary Italian fem. sg./pl.); *li* matches a real attested Old Italian/Tuscan masculine-plural variant (modern standard Italian *gli*). This is a plausible reconstruction for the Adriatic Dalmatian dialect area, not a directly attested Vegliote primary source — real Vegliote is too sparsely documented to cite an actual article paradigm from it (see [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]), the same evidentiary tier as [[../Words/ној|ној]]'s own proposed NOVEM-erosion.

### Gender/Number Assignment — Falls Out of the Neuter Merger for Free

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | **-ел** | **-ли** |
| Feminine | **-ла** | **-ле** |

**No new agreement machinery needed.** [[Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger already sends every old-neuter-singular referent to masculine and every old-neuter-plural referent to feminine — so an old-neuter-singular noun simply takes **-ел**, an old-neuter-plural noun simply takes **-ле**, the identical mapping already used for [[../Words/он|он]], [[../Words/тај|тај]], and [[../Words/овај|овај]]. This slots directly into a category that already exists rather than requiring a new one.

### Mechanics: Postposed and Fused, Invariant Across Case

**Decided: the article attaches after the noun's own case ending and doesn't itself inflect for case** — Sisician follows the Bulgarian/Macedonian path here rather than Romanian's, even though (unlike Bulgarian/Macedonian) Sisician kept a real 5-case system. This is a genuine, deliberate simplification, not an oversight: a newly-grammaticalizing article doesn't have to replicate the case system it attaches to (Romanian's article does; Bulgarian's doesn't, despite both descending from the identical postposed-demonstrative mechanism), and building only four invariant forms (rather than up to twenty case-crossed ones) is the plainer, lower-risk choice while this system is still being worked out.

**Worked example, the clean half: [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s plural, masculine, takes -ли.** Every plural case form already ends in a vowel, so -ли (consonant-initial) attaches with no complication at all:

| Case | Plural | + Article |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | домови | домовили |
| Genitive | домова | домовали |
| Dative | домовима | домовимали |
| Accusative | домове | домовели |
| Instrumental | домовима | домовимали |

**A genuinely open question, flagged rather than forced: the singular half doesn't attach as cleanly.** дом's own nominative/accusative/instrumental singular forms are consonant-final (дом, дом, домом), so -ел attaches just as cleanly there (домел, домел, домомел) — but the genitive (дома) and dative (дому) are vowel-final, and **-a+-ел** / **-u+-ел** both create a real vowel-hiatus problem with no decided resolution yet. Three candidate fixes, none chosen: **(a)** the case vowel elides before the article's own vowel (дома+ел→домел — which would make genitive-singular-definite identical to nominative-singular-definite, a real case-syncretism-under-pressure, not unprecedented in kind but a genuinely bigger loss than any syncretism accepted so far); **(b)** a hiatus-breaking glide or consonant is inserted (дома+ј+ел→домајел, or similar); **(c)** the two vowels contract/monophthongize (дома+ел→домал or домел by a different route than (a)). **This is exactly the kind of mechanical question that shouldn't be guessed at under time pressure** — left open for its own dedicated pass rather than resolved here.

## Where -l Survives

Not lost everywhere — fossilized specifically in:

- **High-frequency kinship and body-part nouns**
- **Toponyms and hydronyms**
- **Family names and bynames**
- **Fixed expressions and oaths**

**This is a real, well-attested type of pattern, not an arbitrary list.** Grammatical markers that disappear from the productive system regularly survive precisely in these categories cross-linguistically: high-frequency words (kinship, body parts) resist regular change simply through sheer repetition entrenching an older form; proper nouns (toponyms, family names) are referentially fixed and don't get re-analyzed the way ordinary vocabulary does; and fixed expressions/oaths are memorized as whole chunks, immune to the analogical leveling that reshapes freely-generated speech. The same general principle already showed up in this project's own [[../Grammar/Verbal Categories|aorist/imperfect]] and [[../Grammar/Infinitive Loss and Da-Complementation|infinitive-loss]] work — old material survives longest in marked, memorized, or high-frequency corners of the lexicon.

**Resolved (2026-09-19): a single fossil, -l, regardless of gender — not two parallel fossils.** Real Balkan/Romance postposed articles keep masculine and feminine formally distinct by their vowel (Romanian *-ul* from *ILLUM* vs. *-a* from *ILLA*; Bulgarian's *-ът/-a*, Albanian's own gender-marked article all do the same). But that distinction lived entirely in the vowel — and Sisician's own erosion already goes further than any of those real languages, stripping the vowel away entirely. Once the vowel that was the *only* carrier of the masculine/feminine distinction is gone, there's no phonological material left to keep them apart: **-lu** (masc.) and **-la** (fem.) both reduce to bare **-l** by the same general process, not by a separate leveling decision. This isn't an assumption stacked onto the earlier extension — it's the mechanical consequence of the erosion already decided.

**The rare exception the user flagged is worth keeping precise:** in specific situations it may still be recoverable which gender the fossil descends from — not because the article itself preserves any trace, but because the *lost* vowel could leave an indirect footprint on the preceding root (a vowel-harmony effect, or a palatalization of the adjacent consonant triggered by *-e/-a* before it dropped). That's a real, narrow, secondary channel of evidence, not a second living fossil form.

## The Historical Productive System: Slavic Long-Form/Short-Form Marking

**This is where definiteness lived in ordinary Sisician speech for most of the language's history** — not the Dalmatian fossil, but [[Adjective Agreement]]'s already-established long-form (definite) vs. short-form (indefinite) adjective distinction. **This section describes the system as it stood before the Channel 2 revival above went productive — see § Coexistence, Then Displacement below for what happens to it afterward.** Nothing new to build here about the mechanism itself; this page exists to explain *why* that system won out originally and what else definiteness-marking left behind, not to duplicate [[Adjective Agreement]].

## Why the Slavic System Won, and Why Definiteness Felt Natural to Keep At All

**The etymological parallel matters here.** [[Adjective Agreement]] already established that the Slavic long form is historically short-form-adjective + suffixed ***-jь*** — the same anaphoric pronoun root behind [[../Words/он|он]]'s own oblique stem, fused onto the adjective to mark definiteness. That's structurally identical to what Dalmatian's postposed article was doing: fusing a demonstrative/anaphoric element (Latin *ILLE*, itself originally "that one there") onto a word to mark that its referent is known/definite. **Bilingual speakers in the founding household and its descendants weren't learning an alien concept when they used the Slavic system — they already had the same underlying strategy in their own Dalmatian speech.** This is a real, well-motivated reason definiteness-marking survived as a robust category into Modern Sisician at all, even though the specific Dalmatian device didn't: the concept was reinforced from both sides, even as one side's implementation fossilized and the other's became universal.

## Coexistence, Then Displacement (Early Modern Through the 1800s) — Added 2026-09-26

**The two systems ran side by side for centuries, genuinely redundant on the same noun phrase, before one displaced the other — and only recently.** From the Channel 2 revival's own start (Early Modern Sisician, c. 1350–1650) through most of Modern Standard Sisician, an attributive definite noun phrase would mark definiteness *twice*: once on the adjective (long form, per [[Adjective Agreement]]) and once on the noun itself (the -ел/-ли/-ла/-ле suffix). This isn't a design flaw needing a fix — genuine grammatical redundancy during a slow grammaticalization shift is real and well-attested cross-linguistically (e.g. some Balkan languages tolerate demonstrative-plus-article co-occurrence for exactly this kind of extended period), and Sisician's own founding premise (a small, prestigious elite reshaping a majority vernacular's grammar over centuries, not an instant switch) predicts exactly this kind of long overlap rather than a clean handoff.

**Decided: full displacement is recent — the 1800s, not the revival's own start.** By the 1800s, the noun-article had been productive, obligatory, and unambiguous for centuries; the adjective long/short-form contrast, by comparison, had never been more than a matching redundancy once the noun itself already carried the same information. Sometime in the 1800s, the adjective system's own definiteness *function* finally gave way — the same slow "a redundant category loses its job to a competitor" pattern already seen elsewhere in this project (Adjective Agreement)'s own indefinite oblique paradigm dying out, [[../Words/абкле|абкле]] displacing native одкле).

**What happens to the long/short-form adjective distinction once it stops meaning "definite/indefinite" — leading hypothesis, not fully resolved:** real modern Serbo-Croatian's own long/short adjective distinction has, in fact, already drifted away from a live definiteness contrast toward something closer to a syntactic-position marker (long form as the default/unmarked attributive shape, short form surviving mainly in predicate position and fixed expressions) — a genuine, independently-attested real-world parallel for exactly the kind of semantic bleaching this page needs. The leading hypothesis is that Sisician's own long/short split undergoes the same repurposing in the 1800s, just triggered by direct competition from the newly-dominant noun-article rather than happening on its own — the long form keeps existing as a form, it just stops being *about* definiteness. **This is flagged explicitly as an open item, not a decision** — see below.

## On "a Complicated Set of Changes by This Point"

The clean story — short-form adjective + *jь* = long-form adjective — is the *origin*, not the current synchronic reality. [[Adjective Agreement]]'s actual endings (*-ога, -ому, -ега, -ему*, etc.) are already the *result* of whatever sound changes and analogical reshaping happened to that original compound over the intervening centuries — they aren't a transparent "adjective + separate pronoun" sequence anymore, and this page doesn't attempt to reconstruct that derivation step-by-step. **Flagging this as a real further task, not a gap being papered over:** a full historical derivation (short-form + *jь* → intermediate stages → the modern endings) could be built out later if wanted, the same way [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]]'s ЊЕХ innovation was derived explicitly rather than just asserted — it just hasn't been done yet for the adjective long form.
