# Numerals

One through ten, following on from [[../Phonology/Phonotactics|Phonotactics]] settling enough of the sound system to check candidate forms against. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## Method: Croatian-Majority, Occasional Slovene Lean, Grounded Twists

Same standing calibration as [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]]'s own "~85% BCS baseline, occasional Slovene lean" principle — most numerals simply continue whichever form the two languages already agree on, since Croatian and Slovene barely diverge for 1–10 once the shared ę→e denasalization and Fall of the Jers are factored in. Three words get individual treatment: one is a fresh instance of the established "occasional Slovene lean" (not a systemic shift), one gets a real, motivated Dalmatian-prestige reshaping, and one gets an outright Dalmatian-descended replacement — both new members of [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]]'s § Prestige-Driven Lexically-Specific Developments, not a new general rule.

## Nine and Ten: Why These Two Got Special Treatment

Full derivation lives on [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § Prestige-Driven Lexically-Specific Developments — this page only summarizes the *why*, not re-derives the *how*.

**Both numbers are unusually salient in Siscia's own toll/commerce identity** ([[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]]): "ten" is the base of decimal reckoning and toll units; "nine" is the number every market culture's pricing psychology gravitates to (prices/counts ending in nine). That shared salience is the actual historical reason — per the [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|governing prestige-register principle]] — these two specific numbers, and no others in the 1–10 set, became targets for Dalmatian-prestige reshaping.

**They didn't get the same *kind* of treatment, because their native Slavic shapes didn't offer the same opportunity:**
- **десет → [[../Words/дешет|дешет]]**: с sits between two front vowels, the same environment that produces real Campidanese Sardinian *dexi* from Latin DECEM — close enough in shape to reshape *in place* via a one-word assibilation (с→ш), not a borrowing.
- **девет → [[../Words/ној|ној]]**: doesn't share enough shape with any plausible Dalmatian NOVEM-descended form to reshape the same way, so this is a genuine replacement — a Dalmatian word, independently eroding toward the same *noi*-shape real Sardinian and Romanian both show (intervocalic /v/-loss in this specific word is attested in *two* separate Romance branches, not invented for this one case), fully displacing девет rather than coexisting with it.

**Neither is a general sound law.** [[../Words/скинија|скинија]] keeps its с; no other Slavic *e* word loses a consonant to a Dalmatian replacement. Exactly the same "lexically-specific, not systemic" framing already established for [[../Words/нув|нув]]'s own convergence.

## Gender Agreement — One and Two Only

Numbers 3–10 are invariant in real Croatian and Slovene alike, and stay that way here. Only [[../Words/један|један]]/[[../Words/једна|једна]] and [[../Words/два|два]]/[[../Words/две|две]] show gender, and the split follows [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter merger exactly the way [[../Words/он|он]]/[[../Words/оне|оне]] already do: an old-neuter-**singular** referent counted as "one" takes **један** (masculine); an old-neuter-**plural** referent counted as "two" takes **две** (feminine). Not a new rule — the identical mechanism, a third application.
