# Possessive Pronouns

The possessive pronoun paradigm: мој, твој, свој, наш, ваш, његов, њен, њихов. Opened by the [[../Texts/Pater Noster|Pater Noster]] translation (which needed наш and твој immediately), but built as a full system here rather than piecemeal. See [[Personal Pronouns]] for the personal-pronoun paradigm these relate to, [[Adjective Agreement]] for the adjective system they follow, and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## The Set and Its Forms

Each possessive has **four forms** — masculine singular, feminine singular, masculine plural, feminine plural — agreeing with the possessed noun in gender and number:

| Possessor | M. sg. | F. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. | мој | моја | моји | моје | my |
| 2 sg. | твој | твоја | твоји | твоје | thy, your |
| reflexive | свој | своја | своји | своје | one's own |
| 1 pl. | наш | наша | наши | наше | our |
| 2 pl. | ваш | ваша | ваши | ваше | your (pl./formal) |
| 3 sg. m. | његов | његова | његови | његове | his, its |
| 3 sg. f. | њен | њена | њени | њене | her |
| 3 pl. | њихов | њихова | њихови | њихове | their |

**Agreement follows the possessed noun, including the neuter merger**: old-neuter nouns take masculine singular agreement, so **име твој** "thy name" and **царство твој** "thy kingdom" (not †твоје), exactly as adjectives agree ([[../Words/сунце|сунце]] takes светао, not †светло). The prayer's **волја твоја** is genuinely feminine.

**свој** is the reflexive possessive ("one's own"), referring back to the subject — real pan-Slavic (*svojь, the same \*-ojь family as мој/твој), kept distinct from његов/њен/њихов exactly as in the real languages: *он воли свој дом* "he loves his [own] home" vs. *он воли његов дом* "he loves his [someone else's] home."

## The Caseless Decision (Flagged, Reversible)

**Possessives take no case forms — gender and number agreement only, like the modern adjective.** This extends the 1800s adjective decision ([[Adjective Agreement]] § The Modern System: adjectives fossilized in the short forms, case agreement lost, definiteness handed to the article) to the possessives, on three grounds:

1. **Formally, possessives are adjectives** — built with the adjectival \*-jь (мој/твој/свој/наш/ваш) and \*-ov/-en (његов/њен/њихов) suffixes, and declining like adjectives in every real Slavic language.
2. **Systemically, the article now carries case** ([[Definiteness]] § Case on the Article), and the noun itself always carries its own case — so nothing is lost when the possessive stops repeating it: **даожницима наши** "to our debtors" has its dative on the noun, exactly as a caseless adjective would.
3. **Functionally, the old long/short definiteness contrast never applied to possessives anyway** — they were always "definite" in reference, so the adjective system's split had nothing to fossilize *from*; the simplest reading of the 1800s change is that everything adjective-shaped went caseless together.

**Flagged as this session's own call, not a user ruling.** The alternative is live: real BCS keeps possessives fully declining (*moj/moga/momu/mojim*...), and the personal pronouns in this vault *do* keep full case ([[Personal Pronouns]]) — so one could equally argue possessives belong to the pronoun system and should decline. If the user prefers that, the four-form tables above become the nominative row of a full paradigm, and the prayer's **даожницима наши** becomes **даожницима нашим** (matching the Slavonic's own нашим). Recorded here so the choice is visible, not buried.

## Etymology

The 1st/2nd-person and reflexive forms are direct Proto-Slavic inheritances, all in the \*-ojь family:

- **мој** < \*mojь ([Wiktionary \*mojь](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/moj%D1%8C), fetched: PIE \*móyos, "inflected like demonstratives")
- **твој** < \*tvojь ([Wiktionary \*tvojь](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/tvoj%D1%8C), fetched: modeled on \*mojь, PIE \*twós) — see [[../Words/твој|Words/твој]]
- **свој** < \*svojь (the reflexive member of the same set, recalled alongside the two fetched citations)
- **наш** < \*našь ([Wiktionary \*našь](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Slavic/na%C5%A1%D1%8C), fetched: \*nasъ + \*-jь "belonging to") — see [[../Words/наш|Words/наш]]
- **ваш** < \*vašь (the exact 2pl twin of \*našь, same composition, per the fetched \*našь page's own paradigm table)

All five lose the final jer by the ordinary [[../Etymology/Fall of the Jers|Fall of the Jers]] and match real BCS *mȏj / tvȏj / svȏj / nȁš / vȁš* cell for cell — no branch fork anywhere in the set.

The 3rd-person forms are built on the vault's own already-established oblique pronoun stems (per [[Personal Pronouns]] and [[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 5's prothetic-n mechanism), with the real BCS comparanda (*njegov/njen/njihov*) as the model:

- **његов** = њег- (the oblique stem of [[../Words/он|он]], as in [[../Words/њега|њега]]) + -ов "his, its"
- **њен** = ње- (the feminine stem, as in [[../Words/ње|ње]]) + -ен "her" — the -ен- shape copied from real BCS *njen*
- **њихов** = њих (as in [[../Words/њих|њих]]) + -ов "their"

Their four-form paradigms follow the same adjective pattern as the rest (његов/његова/његови/његове, etc.). Note that његов's г lenites intervocalically ([[../Etymology/Sisician Sound Changes|Sisician Sound Changes]] § 12): /ˈɲeɣoβ/.

## Syntax

- **Position**: possessive follows the noun in the prayer's liturgical register (име твој, хлеб наш, matching the Slavonic's own имѧ твоѥ, хлѣбъ нашъ); the everyday unmarked order is possessive-first (наш хлеб), per [[Word Order and Register]]'s homey-SVO default. Both are available; the prayer keeps the Slavonic order throughout.
- **With the article**: a possessed noun phrase is definite by virtue of the possessor, so it does not additionally take ел/ли/ла/ле in the prayer — whether possessives *can* co-occur with the article (ел наш дом?) is an open question, not decided here.
- **Clitic interaction**: none — possessives are not clitics and take no slot in the [[Clitic Ordering]] template.
