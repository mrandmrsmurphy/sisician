# Swadesh List (207-word)

A comparative reference table, not a word-coining exercise. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page and [[Etymology/Methodology|Etymology/Methodology]] for the vault's general etymological conventions.

## Purpose and Column Choices

This page puts the standard 207-item Swadesh core-vocabulary list side by side across every language this project already draws on for citations, plus one deliberate outlier, so that future word-coining has a single place to check real comparanda rather than re-fetching them per word.

**The columns split into two ancestry chains plus one outlier, left to right:**

- **Slavic chain — Proto-Slavic → Old Church Slavonic → (Serbo-Croatian, Slovene as modern comparanda).** These are the same languages already cited throughout every [[../Words|Words/]] pronoun page this session (see [[../Sources/Old Church Slavonic|Sources/Old Church Slavonic]] and [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]] for why Serbo-Croatian/Slovene specifically are the modern reference points rather than, say, Russian or Polish).
- **Romance chain — Vulgar Latin → Dalmatian.** Dalmatian is Sisician's actual Romance ancestor (see [[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Sources/Vulgar Latin]] and [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]); Vulgar Latin is included alongside it the same way Proto-Slavic sits alongside OCS, to show the VL→Dalmatian step directly rather than leaving it implicit.
- **Sardinian — NOT an ancestor of Sisician.** This is the one deliberate outlier, included specifically as a **typological comparandum**: Sardinian (the Logudorese variety here) is the most phonologically conservative living Romance language, largely untouched by the innovations that reshaped Italian/French/Dalmatian, so it's useful as a control for what an unmodified Vulgar Latin outcome tends to look like. **Sisician has no Sardinian ancestry whatsoever** — this column exists for comparison, not genealogy. Flagging this explicitly so a future reader doesn't mistake its presence here for a claimed relationship.
- **Sisician (rightmost)** — cross-referenced to already-built [[../Words|Words/]] pages where one exists for that exact Swadesh item, left blank everywhere else. Per this vault's standing rule (see [[../../AIOS/memory-index|AIOS/memory-index]]) against inventing vocabulary unprompted, this page does not coin new words to fill gaps — it only reports what's already been built, coined one word at a time with its own cited etymology. A blank Sisician cell means "not yet coined," distinct from a blank or "—" cell elsewhere in the table, which means a real attested gap in that language's own source (see § Notes on Gaps).

**One correction made during compilation, flagged rather than silently fixed:** item 71 "hair," an early fetch of the comparative Slavic page gave Proto-Slavic as "*vlosъ," but the OCS standalone appendix's own Cyrillic citation (власъ, unambiguously *vlasъ*) — read together with the regular South Slavic liquid-metathesis pattern already visible one row up at item 72 (*golva* → OCS глава *glava*) — indicates the source form is **\*vòlsъ**, with власъ/*vlasъ* being its regular metathesized OCS reflex, not a form called "*vlosъ*" at all. Corrected here; noted as a cross-check inference from real attested spelling and a real, independently-established sound pattern, not a fresh citation of its own.

## Sisician Column — What's Actually Built

**23 of 207 rows** have an existing Sisician word: the original 7 (personal-pronoun citation forms plus [[../Words/нув|нув]]), item 147 ("sun," [[../Words/сунце|сунце]] — the vault's first genuinely soft-stem noun, and the direct prompt behind [[../Texts/Conlang Syntax Test Cases|Texts/Conlang Syntax Test Cases]] item 1, though that sentence still isn't translatable without a verb), items 7–8 ("this"/"that", from [[../Grammar/Demonstrative Pronouns|Grammar/Demonstrative Pronouns]]), items 9–10 ("here"/"there", [[../Words/овде|овде]]/[[../Words/тамо|тамо]] — locative-adverb siblings of items 7–8's demonstrative pronouns, built by fusing the ов-/та- demonstrative roots with the already-established fossilized suffixes from item 13's [[../Words/кде|кде]] and its own non-Swadesh sibling [[../Words/камо|камо]]), items 11–15 (who/what/where/when/how, from [[../Grammar/Interrogatives|Grammar/Interrogatives]] — a deliberately irregular system, not a correlative chart: who/what share one suppletive paradigm each with its own distinct irregularity, где/камо fossilize old case endings directly into the word rather than declining live, and куи/абкле carry the vault's first vocabulary placed in the written-legal-Latin register — абкле in fact fully displaced its own native predecessor, the same way ној displaced native девет), item 16 ("not," split into [[../Words/не|не]]/[[../Words/нон|нон]] — a genuine Sisician-specific innovation, not an inheritance, see [[../Grammar/Negation|Grammar/Negation]]), and items 22–26 (numbers one through five, from [[../Grammar/Numerals|Grammar/Numerals]] — six through ten also exist in [[../Words|Words/]] but aren't Swadesh items, since this list's own numeral section stops at five). Every numeral entry rests on the same already-established mechanisms (mobile-*a*, Ekavian yat, ę→e denasalization) except штири (a deliberate Slovene-leaning pick, flagged inline above). Every oblique pronoun case built this session (мене, тебе, њега, ње, нас, вас, њих, њех, мени, теби, and their clitics) has **no separate row here** — the Swadesh list only asks for each pronoun's citation/nominative form, not its full case paradigm, so мене/тебе/etc. simply don't correspond to any of the 207 items even though they're fully built words in [[../Words|Words/]]. [[../Words/дом|дом]] and [[../Words/каза|каза]] ("house/dwelling") also don't appear here for the same structural reason — "house" isn't one of the 207 Swadesh items at all (Swadesh lists deliberately exclude built/cultural structures in favor of universal basic vocabulary).

This table is meant to be revisited and filled in gradually, the same one-word-at-a-time, fully-cited way every [[../Words|Words/]] page has been built so far — not completed in one sitting.
