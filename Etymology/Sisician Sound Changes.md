# Sisician Sound Changes

**The third and final piece of the Phase 3 consolidation plan.** [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]] and [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] now each have a consolidated c. 1000 AD (and, for Dalmatian, c. 1300 AD) baseline. This page is the bridge from those baselines to the ~44 modern word forms already built in [[../Words|Words/]] — not new research, but the first time everything discovered piecemeal, one word at a time, gets assembled into one ordered picture. See [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]] for why this was the acknowledged gap (target-first word-coining without a formal Phase 3 chain) and [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

**How to use this page:** every individual [[../Words|Words/]] page already cites its own etymology in full — this page doesn't replace those citations, it organizes them. Each entry below links to the page(s) where the change was first established rather than re-deriving it. New words should check here first before re-fetching a sound change that's already on record.

## The Two Chains, Plus One Convergence Category

Per [[../Conlang Workflow|Conlang Workflow]]'s own Deep Structural Analogy diagram, Sisician descends from two ancestries that meet, not one:

- **Slavic chain** — [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] (c. 1000, still-unresolved jers/yat/nasals) → the sound changes below → modern Sisician grammar and native vocabulary. This is where nearly everything on this page lives, because nearly everything built so far (the entire [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] paradigm, [[../Words/нув|нув]]'s native side) is Slavic-descended.
- **Romance chain** — [[../Sources/Vulgar Latin|Vulgar Latin]] → [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]] (c. 1000 baseline, then heavy convergence to c. 1300) → Dalmatian loanwords entering Sisician. **Already fully consolidated on [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]] itself** (both its own sound-change list and its End-Stage Phonology section) — this page doesn't repeat that material, only points to it. No word has yet been coined through a full regular Dalmatian derivation chain; [[../Words/дом|дом]], [[../Words/каза|каза]], [[../Words/скинија|скинија]], [[../Words/икос|икос]] each cite individual Dalmatian/Greek rules directly on their own pages instead.
- **Cross-ancestry convergence** — a third, genuinely distinct category: cases where the two chains produce independently-cognate forms that then merge into one, rather than one chain simply donating a loanword to the other. Currently one example exists: [[../Words/нув|нув]] (§ Cross-Ancestry Convergence below).

## Slavic Chain — Sound Changes (Chronological)

### 1. Fall of the Jers (Havlík's Law) — traditionally c. 1100–1200

**Full treatment on its own standing page: [[Fall of the Jers]], not repeated here.** South Slavic outcome (both ъ/ь → **a** in strong position, matching Kajkavian rather than the Russian ъ→o/ь→e split); weak jers drop. Applied throughout — [[../Words/дом|дом]] (trivial, single word-final jer), and every short-clitic form in the pronoun paradigm that reduces a full oblique stem: [[../Words/нам|нам]] (*namъ→нам), [[../Words/вам|вам]] (*vamъ→вам), [[../Words/им|им]] (*jimъ→им). Status: **fully resolved, bedrock-confident, real and extremely well-documented.**

### 2. Nasal vowel denasalization — ę→e (front) and ǫ→u (back)

**ę→e**, established on [[../Words/ме|ме]] (and inherited without re-citation by [[../Words/те|те]]): Proto-Slavic's dedicated accusative-only enclitic *\*mę̃*/*\*tę̑* denasalizes to modern ме/те. **Explicitly flagged as recalled general Slavic historical phonology, not freshly cited this session** — repeated fetch attempts didn't return a clean quotable source for the sound law itself, unlike the declension-table facts surrounding it. Polish's real retention of the nasal (*mię*/*cię*) is cited as the standing cross-Slavic control confirming the rule is real, not invented.

**ǫ→u**, established on [[../Words/њу|њу]] as the explicit back-vowel counterpart of the same mechanism: *\*jǫ* → њу. Same evidentiary category as ę→e (recalled, not freshly cited), and cross-checked the same way: Polish's retention of the nasal as *ą* (*ją*) confirms the same branch behaves consistently for both vowels — "a single unified phenomenon (loss of nasal vowels generally, except in Polish), not two unrelated sound changes that happen to pattern alike." **Worth noting as a real asymmetry, not a contradiction:** ę→e got its own individually-flagged citation-strength note on two separate pages before ǫ→u ever needed one; ǫ→u is stated here explicitly, for the first time, as its own line item rather than being folded silently into the instrumental-ending discussion (§ 5 below), where the nasal vowel itself was never individually named until now.

### 3. Liquid metathesis (CoRC → CroC-type)

Established via [[Swadesh List]]'s own compilation-time correction: Proto-Slavic **\*golva** → OCS **глава** *(golva → glava)* is the independently-attested comparandum pattern; cross-checking against it corrected an initial garbled fetch of item 71 "hair" from a spurious "*vlosъ" to the real form **\*vòlsъ**, whose regular metathesized OCS reflex is the real attested **власъ** *(vlasъ)*. One of the strongest-evidenced items on this whole page — a real attested textual form (власъ) plus an independently-confirmed parallel pattern (глава), not a single isolated citation.

### 4. Old pronominal/adjectival plural *-y → -e (merging with yat)

Established on [[../Words/оне|оне]]: the raw reconstruction for feminine "they" is *\*ony* (with *-y*), but modern оне has *-e*. **Flagged as general historical-linguistics knowledge, not confirmed by this specific citation**: Common Slavic's old pronominal/adjectival plural *-y* regularly merges with *-ě* (yat) in this exact morphological slot across most of South Slavic, surfacing as *-e* in ekavian-type reflexes — the same ekavian yat outcome already decided for [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] generally. Cross-checked (not independently confirmed) against [[../Grammar/Adjective Agreement|Adjective Agreement]]'s own hard-declension feminine-plural ending, **нуве** — the same historical ending family, showing up in two different paradigms (pronominal and adjectival) for the same reason, per [[../Words/ње|ње]]'s own connection to this fact. Real attested confirmation: Serbo-Croatian and Slovene both show *one*, not *ony*.

### 5. The prothetic n- mechanism, and its clitic corollary

**The mechanism**, established on [[../Words/он|он]]: Proto-Slavic's old anaphoric pronoun *\*jь* survives only in oblique cases, with an extra **њ-** it didn't originally have. Real, cited source: "following a preposition, a prothetic *n-* is attached to the pronoun in many Slavic languages... through resegmentation of prepositions that originally ended in *-n*" — a phrase like *"vъn jego"* ("in him") resegments as *"vъ njego,"* and the *nj-* generalizes to every oblique context. Applied consistently across every 3rd-person oblique full form: [[../Words/њега|њега]], [[../Words/њему|њему]], [[../Words/њим|њим]], [[../Words/њих|њих]], [[../Words/њима|њима]], [[../Words/њој|њој]], [[../Words/њом|њом]]. Extended to the plural on [[../Words/они|они]]/[[../Words/њих|њих]] "as a reasonable extension of a cited fact, not itself a separately cited fact" — flagged honestly as extension, not fresh citation, each time.

**The corollary, stated for the first time as this project's own synthesis on [[../Words/њих|њих]]**: prothetic *n-* appears *only* on full/stressed forms, never on the corresponding clitics ([[../Words/га|га]], [[../Words/је|је]], [[../Words/их|их]], [[../Words/му|му]], [[../Words/јој|јој]], [[../Words/им|им]], [[../Words/ју|ју]]) — a direct, deductive consequence of the mechanism itself (clitics never occupy the post-preposition environment that triggered the original resegmentation), not a separately sourced fact. Confirmed with the strongest possible evidence on [[../Words/их|их]]: real attested Old Church Slavonic text shows genitive **ихъ** *(ixŭ)* already lacking any *n-*, meaning их is the historically conservative form and њих (with its *n-*) is the actual innovation — inverting the naive assumption that the shorter clitic must be a reduction of the longer one.

### 6. Instrumental singular *-ojǫ → -om (masc./neut. a-stem pattern)

Established on [[../Words/мном|мном]] (*\*mъnojǫ* → мном) and extended to [[../Words/тобом|тобом]] (*\*tobojǫ* → тобом). **Not an irregular pronoun form at all** — the identical ending underlies ordinary Sisician feminine *a*-stem noun instrumental singulars (real Croatian *žena→ženom*, from *\*ženojǫ*), "a live, fully productive pattern in the modern language, not a fossil." No citation reached this session explains the specific *-ojǫ→-om* sound change itself, but it's treated as bedrock-confident general Slavic historical phonology — the same evidentiary category as the *e~o* ablaut in § 8 below, distinguished explicitly from a fresh citation. This makes мном "the first pronoun oblique cell in the whole set that simply follows regular noun-declension machinery" rather than needing its own bespoke mechanism.

**A separate, unrelated masculine 3rd-person instrumental**: [[../Words/њим|њим]] (*\*jimь* → (н)им → њим) does **not** belong to this family — it's a different historical ending entirely (*-ьmь*, not *-ojǫ*), cleanly confirmed via real attested OCS **имь** *(imĭ)*, "no vowel puzzle, no unresolved branch tension, no honestly-flagged gap." Worth stating plainly since the modern *-ом*-looking shapes (мном, тобом, њим) could otherwise be mistaken for one mechanism when they're really two.

### 7. Genitive singular feminine e-vowel (ње), from the same pronominal-ending family as § 4

Established on [[../Words/ње|ње]]: Proto-Slavic feminine genitive **\*jeję̇** is genuinely distinct from accusative **\*jǫ** from the start (unlike masculine, where genitive absorbed a never-distinct accusative — § "Genitive/Accusative Syncretism Pattern B" below). Connects to the same pronominal-genitive-ending family already fused onto adjectives (§ 4's нуве connection) — general comparative Slavic knowledge, not freshly cited.

### 8. The *e~o* stem ablaut (теб-/тоб- and analogues)

First flagged on [[../Words/ти|ти]], repeated without re-derivation on [[../Words/тебе|тебе]], [[../Words/теби|теби]], [[../Words/тобом|тобом]]: тебе/теби keep the stem-vowel *e*, while тобом shifts to *o*. Presented as inherited texture — a real, observed alternation, not one this project has derived from a stated sound law of its own.

### 9. Glide *v Hardens to Fricative /v/ (new 2026-09-23)

**The missing link between [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]]'s c. 1000 snapshot and the modern target.** Common Slavic *v is real, well-documented as a labial glide/approximant at that stage (descending from PIE *w) — but [[../Phonology/Modern Inventory|Phonology/Modern Inventory]] and [[../Phonology/Orthography|Phonology/Orthography]] both independently place modern Sisician's /v/ among the fricatives (f v, s z, š ž, x), not the glides/liquids. Rather than leave that a silent, unstated gap, this hardening is now recorded explicitly: **Common Slavic's glide *v hardens to a true fricative /v/** by the modern stage — a real, unremarkable, cross-linguistically common development (parallel to how several Indo-European branches harden an inherited *w/glide to a fricative-like /v/), not specific evidence one way or the other from the Sava-corridor devoicing facts already cited on [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Late Common Slavic]] (real Slavic /v/ shows that same obstruent-like devoicing behavior in dialects where it stays phonetically glide-like too, so it never disambiguated the two readings).

**Immediate consequence, already sitting in the vault before this was ever connected to it:** [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s own /w/→/v/ merger (already decided 2026-09-20) assumed exactly this modern fricative target — its own illustrative example, **mōrte → mwart → мварт** ("death," Bartoli's real closed-syllable diphthongization data), already gives the hardened -v- spelling. No word has been coined from мварт itself yet ([[../Etymology/Swadesh List|Etymology/Swadesh List]] doesn't include a standalone "death" item), but **the mechanism now has a real first worked example elsewhere: [[../Words/читвот|читвот]] "city" (2026-09-24)**, whose own real attested Dalmatian source (Vegliote *cituot*, cited on Wiktionary) has exactly this kind of glide-bearing diphthong (*-tuot*), hardening the same way at the Sisician-borrowing step to give читвот. The two cases differ only in *when* the hardening happens (мварт's own telling has it happen inside dying Dalmatian itself, post-1300; читвот is an early loan, so the same hardening is placed at borrowing instead) — the underlying reason (no native /w/ to receive the glide) is identical either way.

### 10. L-Vocalization: Coda /l/ → /o/ (new 2026-09-25)

**A real, documented South Slavic sound change, stated explicitly for the first time even though its output has been used correctly by eye since [[../Grammar/Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality|Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality]]'s very first draft.** Real Serbo-Croatian's l-participle suffix (Common Slavic *-lъ, masculine singular) loses its jer via the already-established [[Fall of the Jers]], leaving a bare word-final /l/ — which then undergoes a separate, later, real documented change: coda **/l/ (word-final or pre-consonantal) vocalizes to /o/** (a real, named phenomenon — "L-vocalization" — attested in South Slavic from roughly the 14th century onward, later than jer loss itself, not the same event). This is exactly why **радио** (masc. sg., от *radilъ*) contrasts with **радила/радили/радиле** (fem. sg./masc. pl./fem. pl.): the suffixal *-l-* is never in coda position in those three (it's followed by the vowel -a/-i/-e), so it never vocalizes — only the masculine singular, where historical *-lъ* ends up truly word-final, shows the change.

**Timeline placement**: roughly Middle Sisician into Early Modern Sisician (c. 1350), matching real Serbo-Croatian's own documented dating and sitting comfortably alongside [[../Grammar/Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality|Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality]]'s own independently-decided timeline, where the је/су fusion-and-drop system also fully generalizes by Early Modern Sisician — two separate changes reaching maturity around the same broad period, not forced into artificial sync.

**Consequence for coining new l-participles**: this is a *completed* historical sound change, not a live synchronic rule — a new verb (native or borrowed) doesn't re-trigger the change, it simply inherits the **-o/-la/-li/-le** paradigm shape by ordinary morphological analogy, the same way English "-ed" applies to a brand-new verb without re-deriving Germanic ablaut. First applied this way to a genuinely new coinage in [[../Words/респонџати|респонџати]]'s own l-participle (респонџао/-ала/-али/-але) — see that page and [[../Grammar/Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality|Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality]] for the worked example.

## Slavic Chain — Structural and Analogical Mechanisms (Not Sound Changes)

These reshape the paradigm without being phonological sound laws in the Havlík's-Law sense — worth keeping distinct from § "Sound Changes" above, since conflating the two would misrepresent how each cell of the paradigm actually got its modern shape.

### /j/+/r/ Onset Metathesis — a Live Synchronic Repair, Not a Historical Change (new 2026-09-25)

**A third category, distinct from both the historical sound changes above and the analogical mechanisms below**: when [[../Grammar/Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality|Past Tense Fusion and Evidentiality]]'s је-clitic fuses onto an /r/-initial verb root (радио, респонџао), the resulting word-initial /j/+/r/ sequence violates the Sonority Sequencing Principle (glides outrank liquids, so glide+liquid+vowel dips rather than rises) and metathesizes to the sonority-compliant /r/+/j/ — **рја̀дио**, not †јра̀дио. Spelled in plain lowercase, not capitalized mid-word — an earlier draft's capitalization convention (ЈРА̀дио-style) was a documentation aid later dropped, since no real orthography marks a morpheme boundary that way. The plural equivalent, /s/+/r/ (from су-fusion), needs no such repair, since obstruent+liquid+vowel already rises. Full account and both worked examples live on that page, not repeated here — flagged centrally only because it's exactly the kind of "why does this cell look like this" fact this page exists to collect. **Not the same mechanism as [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s own /rj/-metathesis** (real Vulgar Latin PARIA→*paire*, medial and vowel-seeking, cited for [[../Words/денајр|денајр]]) despite the thematic echo — this one is a live, synchronic, sonority-driven repair triggered fresh at every cliticization event, not a completed historical sound change inherited from Latin.

### Genitive/Accusative Syncretism — Four Genuinely Different Historical Patterns

The single biggest cross-cutting finding in the whole pronoun set: four pairs all end up with genitive=accusative syncretism in modern Sisician, but by four different historical routes. Conflating them would flatten a real, load-bearing distinction.

- **Pattern A — [[../Words/мене|мене]]/[[../Words/тебе|тебе]] (1st/2nd singular).** A real, dedicated Proto-Slavic accusative-only enclitic existed (*\*mę̃*, *\*tę̑* — real ancestors of attested OCS мѧ/тѧ), but Sisician doesn't carry it forward as a separate full form; genitive simply covers both cells. "A real simplification relative to... Old Church Slavonic, which kept мѧ distinct" — a design point, not a mechanical inheritance. (The enclitic itself *does* survive, denasalized, as the modern short clitics [[../Words/ме|ме]]/[[../Words/те|те]] — see § Sound Changes 2 above; only the *full/stressed* accusative form was lost.)
- **Pattern B — [[../Words/њега|њега]] (3rd singular masc.).** The opposite starting arrangement: accusative was never anything but the bare nominative-lookalike *\*jь* itself — no separate accusative shape ever existed. Genitive *\*jego* alone was the suffixed form, and modern syncretism is genitive absorbing accusative's job outright, via the general Slavic rule that animate accusative uses the genitive form (§ "Animate-Accusative-Uses-Genitive" below). "Sisician's accusative њега descends from the genitive branch of the tree, not the accusative branch — a real discontinuity," explicitly distinguished from Pattern A.
- **Pattern C — [[../Words/нас|нас]] (1st plural) and [[../Words/њих|њих]] (3rd plural masc., confirmed as the same pattern a second time).** A real distinct accusative-only form *did* exist (*\*ny̑* for 1pl; *\*ję̇* for 3pl) but died out, and genitive generalized to fill the resulting gap — the нас story, not the њега story, "confirmed for the 3rd-plural masculine as well" rather than assumed. *\*ny also doubled as short dative at the Proto-Slavic stage, a further syncretism distinct from the singular's kept-separate ме/ми.
- **Pattern D — [[../Words/вас|вас]] (2nd plural).** Genuinely different again: accusative wasn't just superficially but *actually* identical to nominative, because [[../Words/ви|ви]]'s own nominative *is* the old case-leveled accusative (a real Proto-Balto-Slavic case-leveling event, not suppletion or absorption). Genitive-for-accusative here is **active ambiguity-avoidance** (real subject/object disambiguation pressure), not inherited syncretism at all — the third application of the functional-load principle below, and structurally the odd one out among the four patterns.

### The Functional Load Principle

Stated first on [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] (via [[../Grammar/Nominal Morphology|Nominal Morphology]]'s neuter-merger discussion), and applied three separate times for three different reasons — worth naming as a recurring design tool rather than three unrelated coincidences: (1) explaining *where* the neuter-merger's systemic pressure resolved (genitive/accusative's high referential traffic vs. dative/instrumental's low traffic) — the motivation behind [[../Words/њех|њех]]'s own proportional-analogy innovation below; (2) [[../Words/вас|вас]]'s active ambiguity-avoidance (Pattern D above); (3) implicitly underlying why дом's own noun paradigm marks animacy on the accusative in the first place (§ "Animate-Accusative-Uses-Genitive" below).

### Animate-Accusative-Uses-Genitive (General Slavic Rule)

Cited on [[../Words/њега|њега]] as the general mechanism behind Pattern B: Slavic marks masculine animate accusative with the genitive form rather than a distinct accusative shape — real, well-documented, and already the same rule governing [[../Words/дом|дом]]'s own noun-paradigm animate/inanimate accusative split, not a pronoun-specific invention.

### ЊЕХ — An Internal Analogical Innovation, Not a Sound Change

[[../Words/њех|њех]] (and its clitic [[../Words/ех|ех]]) is the **one cell in the entire pronoun set with no Proto-Slavic or OCS antecedent at all** — real Common Slavic never distinguished 3rd-plural genitive/accusative by gender in any daughter branch, so there is nothing to derive this from historically. The full chain: (1) the neuter merger's removal of the paradigm's last person/thing nominative distinction created systemic pressure (functional-load principle, above); (2) genitive/accusative specifically resolved it, for the same functional-load reason; (3) the mechanism, once motivated, is proportional (four-part) analogy: **ЊЕ:ЊЕГА::ЊЕХ:ЊИХ**, using [[../Words/њих|њих]]'s own already-established *-их* ending as the model. Accusative joins genitive because they were already historically syncretic pan-Slavically (§ Pattern C). The clitic [[../Words/ех|ех]] was deliberately coined *simultaneously* with njeh rather than lagging — an explicit reversal of an earlier working assumption on [[../Grammar/Personal Pronouns|Personal Pronouns]] that clitics should be conservative. Full derivation lives on that page; [[../Words/њех|њех]]'s own page only summarizes it.

### Dative=Instrumental Plural Syncretism — Confirmed Three Times, With a Genuinely Unresolved Vowel

The first non-genitive/accusative syncretism type in the whole set. [[../Words/нама|нама]], [[../Words/вама|вама]], and [[../Words/њима|њима]] each merge a genuinely distinct Proto-Slavic dative/instrumental pair (*\*namъ*/*\*nami*, *\*vamъ*/*\*vami*, *\*jimъ*/*\*jimi* — different endings, *-ъ* vs. *-i*, a real merger, not inherited syncretism) — confirmed as the same pattern a third time by independently re-fetching Slovene comparanda for [[../Words/њима|њима]] specifically, rather than assuming the first two results would hold. **Neither historical form regularly derives the stressed *-a* ending all three show** — Fall of the Jers gives bare нам/вам/им (matching the clitics exactly), not a stressed *-a*-final shape. Most plausible account, **explicitly uncited, recalled general knowledge only**: pronoun-to-noun analogical leveling with the productive feminine *a*-stem plural dative/instrumental ending *-ама* (real Croatian *женама*), with Common Slavic's own dual number's independently-real dative/instrumental syncretism (from PIE *\*-me*/*\*-oH*) offered as supporting precedent for the merger-*type*, not proof of this specific vowel. Real branch tension, checked and reconfirmed independently for all three words: **Serbo-Croatian is the only branch checked where the merger actually happens** — OCS, Slovene, Russian, and Polish all keep dative and instrumental distinct.

## Honest Open Questions — Not Resolved, Not Papered Over

Carried forward here exactly as flagged on their own pages, because a consolidation page is exactly where an unresolved item is most likely to get silently smoothed into false confidence if it isn't restated plainly:

- **The *e→o* vowel shift, feminine dative/instrumental singular ([[../Words/њој|њој]], [[../Words/њом|њом]]).** Reconstruction gives *e* (*\*jeji*, *\*jejǫ*); real Serbo-Croatian shows *o* (њој, њом) instead. Three separate fetch attempts found no source explaining the shift. Slovene keeps the expected *e* (*njej*), making Serbo-Croatian the real branch outlier — an acknowledged, not resolved, choice. Working hypothesis (explicitly uncited): dative and instrumental analogically leveled together as a pair, since both — and only both — show the unexplained *o*. [[../Words/њом|њом]]'s own fetch is treated as supporting evidence for this hypothesis, not proof. Further complication: [[../Words/јој|јој]] found that Slovene's own dative clitic (*ji*) doesn't even match Slovene's own stressed forms — a second, independent messiness in this cell.
- **The short clitics [[../Words/га|га]] and [[../Words/је|је]].** Hypothesized *(j)ego→go→ga* and an analogous reduction from *\*jeję̇*, both with a genuinely unexplained vowel step. Real Serbo-Croatian Wiktionary itself gives no etymology for *ga* at all. "The least-well-grounded etymological claim in the pronoun set." Contrast [[../Words/му|му]]: *jemu* losing its unstressed initial syllable to leave *-mu* is "a straightforward, ordinary kind of reduction" — still technically uncited, but explicitly *more plausible* than га's case, a real stated asymmetry between two clitics that could otherwise be mistaken for the same kind of gap.
- **The dative=instrumental plural's own *-a* vowel** (нама/вама/њима, above) — the same evidentiary category as the ge→o puzzle: a real merger with a plausible-but-uncited account of its own surface vowel.
- **ǫ→u's citation asymmetry with ę→e** (§ Sound Changes 2) — not a gap in the *mechanism* (both are equally well-motivated as a single unified nasal-vowel-loss phenomenon), but a gap in how individually each one was flagged before this page existed. Recorded here so a future word-coining session doesn't need to rediscover that ǫ→u was always resting on the same recalled-knowledge basis as ę→e, just never named as its own line item until now.

## Cross-Ancestry Convergence

**[[../Words/нув|нув]] ("new") is currently the only example of a real third category**, distinct from both chains above: native Slavic *\*novъ* and Dalmatian's own *nuv* (itself the regular product of [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s own diphthongization of stressed Latin *ō*) are genuine PIE cognates, not a coincidental look-alike — and rather than staying two formally distinct words in two registers (the [[../Words/дом|дом]]/[[../Words/каза|каза]] model), they converged into one merged Sisician form, with the Dalmatian vowel winning out. **Explicitly not a regular sound law**: "no other Slavic *o* systematically becomes у in Sisician, this is a lexically-specific merger" driven by the two ancestries' unusually tight double ancestry for this one word specifically. Renamed from нов to нув (2026-09-20) once this was recognized. If more cognate pairs are ever identified between the two chains, this section is where they'd be catalogued — currently a category of one.

## Prestige-Driven Lexically-Specific Developments (new 2026-09-23)

**A second, genuinely distinct category from Cross-Ancestry Convergence above** — not two ancestries independently producing the same form, but Dalmatian-prestige social pressure reshaping (or replacing) one specific, highly salient native Slavic word, motivated by [[../AIOS/memory/project_prestige_register_principle|the prestige-register principle]] and, concretely, Siscia's own toll/commerce identity ([[../Culture/Geography and Economy|Culture/Geography and Economy]]) making decimal counting words unusually salient targets. Both instances arose while coining the numeral system and are catalogued here rather than left as one-off word-page trivia, the same reasoning that gave нув its own section.

- **[[../Words/дешет|дешет]] ("ten") — lexically-specific assibilation, с→ш.** The expected regular form — call it *десет*, from Proto-Slavic *\*desętь* via the already-established ę→e denasalization, the same shape real Croatian and Slovene both still show — was never a separately attested Sisician word; it's an intermediate stage that has its medial с sit between two front vowels — structurally the same "coronal softens near front vowels" environment that produces real Campidanese Sardinian *dexi* from Latin DECEM (medial /k/→[ʒ], confirmed via Wiktionary/comparative sources) — not the same historical sound law (stop-lenition vs. fricative postalveolarization), but the same family of effect, and the comparandum that suggested it. Dalmatian-philic prestige speech nudges "ten" — the single most commercially salient number in a toll economy — toward this Romance-sounding shape, and it wins out as **the** modern form; **this does not become a general с→ш rule** (compare [[../Words/скинија|скинија]], whose с is untouched).
- **[[../Words/ној|ној]] ("nine") — genuine lexical replacement, not reshaping.** Unlike дешет, native *девет* (< *\*devętь*, same ę→e mechanism as пет) doesn't share enough shape with a Dalmatian-descended form to reinterpret in place, so this is a real displacement: Dalmatian's own reflex of Latin NOVEM, independently losing its intervocalic /v/ (a real, recurring cross-Romance weakness in this specific word — confirmed for *both* Sardinian *noi* and Romanian *nouă* via Wiktionary, not a single-language coincidence), erodes to a vowel-hiatus shape that resolves to /noj/ by the time it enters Sisician's own borrowing chain. Adopted into the commercial/toll register for the same reason "ten" got reshaped — decimal/pricing salience — and, per the same "prices/counts ending in nine" commercial psychology real market cultures show, generalizes out from that register to fully displace девет as the ordinary vernacular word. **The weakest-grounded claim in this section**, flagged honestly: the NOVEM→noj chain is plausible pattern-matching against real comparative Romance data, not a citation for Dalmatian specifically (Dalmatian is barely attested at all — see [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Dalmatian]]'s own repeated caveats about this).
- **Why these two and not, say, "seven" or "eight":** both already had a real, independently-motivated reason to be salient (decimal counting, market pricing) before any sound change was proposed — the [[project_prestige_register_principle|governing design principle]]'s "every weird feature needs a historical reason" applied to word-selection, not just to the sound changes themselves.
- **A fourth subtype, new 2026-09-23: genuine hybrid compounds.** [[../Words/куи|куи]] ("which") and [[../Words/абкле|абкле]] ("whence") are both direct Latin citation-form borrowings (Latin QUI; Latin AB fused onto native -кле), entering not through spoken Dalmatian at all but through the **written legal/notarial Latin register** already established on [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Culture/Sociolinguistic History]] ("Latin survives only in diplomatic/foreign-trade/family-document contexts") — the first vocabulary this vault has actually placed in that specific, previously-unfilled niche. Абкле specifically is a genuine calque mixture, one morpheme from each ancestry (Latin AB + native -кле) fused into a single word — a real, attested type of contact phenomenon (hybrid/macaronic compounding), distinct from convergence (нув) and in-place reshaping (дешет).
- **A fifth subtype, new 2026-09-24: outright displacement of an unbuilt native candidate, modeled directly on a real English parallel.** [[../Words/фамаља|фамаља]] ("family," < Latin FAMILIA via Dalmatian *familja*) is the first word coined specifically to test [[../Sources/Dalmatian|Sources/Dalmatian]]'s newly-decided (2026-09-24) -LI-→ʎ and /ks/, sj/zj→š/ž loanword routes, and the first replacement modeled explicitly on a real donor-language precedent rather than an internal Sisician motivation (toll economy, legal register, etc.): real Old English *hīred*/*hād* were both displaced by Latin-derived *family*, and Sisician's own two native candidates (**породица**, a regular *по-+\*rodъ+-ица* compound, and the real Proto-Slavic **\*obitělь** "dwelling, abode" — whose real Croatian descendant *obitelj* independently shows the identical "dwelling→family" narrowing *hīred* shows) are passed over the same way, neither ever built as its own `Words/` page. Distinct from абкле/ноj's displacement pattern in one respect: those replaced an attested-in-passing intermediate native form; here neither native candidate was ever a real Sisician word to begin with, only a documented "what would have been coined" counterfactual.
- **Абкле turned out to combine two subtypes at once, not stay a pure hybrid.** Куи stayed confined to its legal-register niche, but абкле generalized out of it to fully displace its own native predecessor (an old compositional *од+кле* form, no longer independently attested) — the same trajectory [[../Words/ној|ној]] took against native девет. So абкле is simultaneously a hybrid compound *and* a full replacement, the first word in this catalog to combine two subtypes rather than being a clean example of one. Why абкле generalized while куи didn't isn't yet given its own historical account — flagged as an open item on [[../Grammar/Interrogatives|Grammar/Interrogatives]] rather than an invented asymmetry. The one thing that *did* survive the replacement cleanly: the native, genitive-shaped **-кле** stem stayed put underneath the swapped-in Latin preposition, meaning the case-marking pattern outlived the specific word carrying it.
