# Fall of the Jers

**Status: always followed.** A standing, pan-Slavic sound change, declared here once (2026-09-19) rather than re-derived on every word page that relies on it — link here instead. See [[../Sisician|Sisician]] for the hub page.

## The Jers

**ъ** (back jer) and **ь** (front jer) are short, reduced vowels inherited from Common Slavic — see [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Sources/Late Common Slavic]] and [[../Sources/Old Church Slavonic|Sources/Old Church Slavonic]] § Vowels, both of which list them as live vowels at that stage.

## Havlík's Law (the precise rule, not just "final jers drop")

This is real, extremely well-documented pan-Slavic historical phonology (traditionally dated ~1100–1200 AD — falling within [[../Culture/Sociolinguistic History|Old/Middle Sisician]]), not a Sisician invention. The rule, precisely:

1. **Count the jers in a word from right to left.**
2. **The rightmost jer is always weak.**
3. **Moving leftward, jers alternate: weak, strong, weak, strong…**
4. **Weak jers drop entirely.** **Strong jers vocalize to a full vowel.**

**South Slavic vocalization outcome (not the Russian one):** in strong position, both ъ and ь merge to **a** — this is the real Serbo-Croatian/Kajkavian pattern, distinct from East Slavic (Russian), where ъ→o and ь→e stay separate. Sisician follows the South Slavic outcome, matching its [[../Sources/Late Common Slavic|Kajkavian-leaning]] vernacular base.

## The Consequence: Mobile *a* (nepostojano a)

This produces a real, pervasive feature of Serbo-Croatian-type nominal morphology that Sisician should inherit directly: a root jer can surface as **a** in some case forms and **vanish** in others, depending on whether the *following* case ending is itself a full vowel (making the root jer the word's sole/rightmost jer, hence weak → drops) or not. Classic real example: Common Slavic *sъnъ* "sleep, dream" (two jers) → Serbo-Croatian nominative **san** (final ъ weak/drops; root ъ, now rightmost in this form, is... — worked below) / genitive **sna** (the *-a* ending is a full vowel, making the root jer the sole jer in this form, hence weak, hence it drops). Any future Sisician word with a root jer should show this same alternation, not a flat "keep the *a* everywhere" or "drop it everywhere" treatment.

## Applying It: дом

**домъ** has a single, word-final jer (ъ). Being the sole jer, it's trivially the rightmost — weak — and drops. **домъ → дом**, in every form (no alternation possible with only one jer in root-final position, and no oblique ending recreates a jer environment here). See [[../Words/дом|Words/дом]] for the full paradigm this produces — that page now links here rather than re-explaining this rule inline.

## How to Apply Going Forward

Any native-Slavic-sourced word inherited with a jer should be checked against Havlík's Law here rather than treated as a one-off decision. Words with a single, word-final jer (like дом) are trivial — it just drops. Words with a root jer plus other material will need the full alternating-count treatment, and may show mobile-*a* alternation across their own paradigm.
