# Etymology: Semantic Domains

Which vocabulary domains should come from which source language, and roughly how much of the lexicon each source should occupy. See [[Sisician]] for the hub page, [[Conlang Workflow]] for why domain-based distribution matters more than an overall percentage, and [[Etymology/Methodology]] for how individual loans should be recorded.

The design target:

> **Grammar:** overwhelmingly South Slavic
> **Core vocabulary:** overwhelmingly Slavic
> **Urban/civic vocabulary:** heavily Dalmatian
> **Learned vocabulary:** Greek/Church Slavonic + Dalmatian/Latin
> **Political vocabulary:** Hungarian + Dalmatian
> **Total lexicon:** perhaps 30–40% demonstrably Romance-derived

**The important thing is not the percentage. It is the semantic distribution** — matching how English became "Romance-looking" only because particular historical domains (law, cuisine, government) were disproportionately Frenchified, not because half the dictionary was replaced at random.

## Native Slavic Domains

Likely domains: body; kinship; ordinary household life; basic verbs; nature; agriculture; landscape; ordinary emotions; basic numerals and function words; core grammatical vocabulary.

## Dalmatian Romance Domains

Likely domains, organized by the social sphere where the founding elite had power (see [[Culture/History]] and [[Culture/Sociolinguistic History]]):

- **Government:** lord, court, charter, office, tax, toll, treasury, law, judgment, privilege, seal, document
- **Urban life:** street, square, warehouse, market, guild, merchant, shop, port, bridge, ferry
- **Military:** castle, wall, tower, garrison, soldier, armor, siege, banner, command
- **Architecture:** room types, masonry, arches, roofs, windows, doors, fortifications, civic buildings
- **Commerce:** cargo, price, debt, contract, measure, weight, coinage, shipping
- **Cuisine:** wine, preserved foods, sauces, pastries, imported foods, cooking terminology
- **Courtly culture:** clothing, jewelry, music, dancing, manners, titles, etiquette

Also more broadly: administration; law; court life; commerce; tolls; urban institutions; architecture; fortifications; maritime culture; luxury goods; fashion; elite military terminology; bureaucratic vocabulary. This is how Sisician can become Romance-looking without destroying its Slavic core.

## Greek / Church Slavonic Domains

The Orthodox turn (see [[Culture/Religion]]) produces a substantial second learned stratum, distinguished stylistically from the civic Dalmatian vocabulary above:

- **Greek-derived:** theology, metaphysics, Christology, liturgy, iconography, monasticism, ecclesiastical offices, philosophy, rhetoric
- **Church Slavonic-derived:** ordinary liturgical vocabulary, Scripture, prayer, feasts, sacraments, biblical idioms, religious titles

Culturally comparable to Germanic + French + Latin/Greek in English, except here: Slavic + Dalmatian + Greek/Church Slavonic.

## Hungarian / German / Italian / Other Regional Sources

Likely domains: specialized trade; frontier administration; later political terminology; military technology; mining; craft vocabulary; neighboring institutions. See [[Culture/History]] § 1102 and § 1242 for the specific historical events that introduce this layer.

## The Three-Layer Lexical Model

A mature Sisician lexicon should feel like it has three great historical layers:

**Layer I — Slavic Earth:** the words of family, land, body, weather, agriculture, animals, basic verbs, ordinary life.

**One deliberate, narrow exception, flagged rather than silently left contradicting the line above (2026-09-24):** [[Words/фамаља|фамаља]], the actual modern Sisician word for "family," is Dalmatian, not native — modeled directly on real English, where Latin-derived *family* displaced native *hīred*/*hād*. This isn't Layer I eroding generally: individual kinship terms (mother, father, brother, sister — none built yet) are still expected to stay native, exactly as they did in English even after *family* itself went Latinate. See [[Words/фамаља|фамаља]]'s own Etymology section for the full displacement story and the two native candidates it passed over.

**Layer II — Dalmatian City:** the words of law, trade, government, military, architecture, money, urbanity, court culture. **First worked example confirming the model, not an exception to it (2026-09-24):** [[Words/читвот|читвот]] "city" — Dalmatian, displacing native Proto-Slavic *\*gordъ*, the clean on-model case Layer II predicts (contrast [[Words/фамаља|фамаља]] above, which crosses the *other* direction).

**Layer III — Greek/Slavonic Heaven:** the words of God, Church, liturgy, theology, philosophy, saints, Scripture, sacrament.

This gives the culture an internally meaningful lexical map, and echoes [[Culture/Religion]]'s formulation: *Romance for city, Slavic for earth, Slavonic/Greek for heaven.*

**First worked example (2026-09-19):** the "home/house" concept, now across *four* words, since the Greek/Church-Slavonic layer split into two distinct registers — [[Words/дом|дом]] (native, warm, "the cozy feel of English *home*"), [[Words/каза|каза]] (Dalmatian, formal/architectural, secular), [[Words/скинија|скинија]] (Greek via Church Slavonic, the mystical "dwelling of God with humanity," Revelation 21:3/John 1:14 — a register with no direct English counterpart), and [[Words/икос|икос]] (Greek direct, not via Church Slavonic, the "household/community of God" sense, Ephesians 2:19/1 Timothy 3:15 — belonging rather than presence). Illustrates that even within one semantic layer, the *pathway* (via OCS vs. direct Greek contact) can itself carry meaning. See the [[Words/дом|дом]] page and its three siblings for the full derivations.
